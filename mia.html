Mia Goth Reportedly Files for Divorce in Los Angeles

By BuzzDaily Staff
· 8 October 2026

Mia Goth has filed for divorce from Shia LaBeouf in Los Angeles, formalising the end of a marriage that began a decade ago — and doing so, unusually for a couple who guarded their privacy for years, through court documents that set out her requests in detail. The petition was filed on 6 October 2026 in Los Angeles County Superior Court, citing irreconcilable differences. It was first reported by TMZ and confirmed by PEOPLE on 7 October 2026.

The filing lists 28 July 2025 as the couple’s date of separation — meaning that, on the documents’ own account, the marriage had been over in practice for more than a year before the petition reached the court. That gap between separation and filing is not unusual in California proceedings, but it reframes the story: this is not a sudden split, but the legal formalisation of an arrangement the couple had already been living with, kept out of public view until the paperwork made it a matter of record.

At the centre of the petition is the couple’s daughter, Isabel, born in 2022 and now four years old. Goth requests primary legal and physical custody of Isabel, with visitation for LaBeouf. On finances, she asks the court to terminate spousal support for either party — in plain terms, that neither she nor LaBeouf should receive ongoing support payments from the other. Both requests are exactly that: requests. A divorce petition sets out what one party is asking the court to order; it is not a ruling, nothing in it has been granted yet, and every element — custody, visitation and support — remains subject to judicial review and to any response LaBeouf files before a judge decides.

The couple’s history stretches back to 2013, when they met during the production of Lars von Trier’s “Nymphomaniac: Vol. II”. They married in October 2016 and welcomed Isabel in March 2022. Across that decade the relationship was conducted almost entirely in private — few interviews, no public social media presence from Goth, and long stretches in which the couple simply did not feature in the celebrity news cycle at all. That privacy is part of why the filing has drawn such attention: for a marriage that was never performed in public, its ending is being documented in unusual, court-record detail.

The wider context has been a turbulent year for LaBeouf. In February 2026 he was arrested in New Orleans during Mardi Gras, and in June 2026 he pleaded guilty to three counts of simple battery, receiving two years’ probation and an alcohol treatment programme. Separately, in October 2026 he agreed to void the non-disclosure agreement at the centre of the FKA twigs case. None of those matters is part of Goth’s petition, and the filing should not be read as commenting on them — but they form the backdrop against which a family court will now consider questions of custody and visitation, where a child’s welfare is the court’s governing concern.

The story has also produced a modern coda about fame and fabrication. On 13–14 October 2026, days after the filing became public, Goth’s team moved to denounce an AI-generated fake selfie that purported to show her mocking LaBeouf, confirming in the process that she has no social media accounts at all — a reminder that in a case already being fought over documents and records, even the images circulating online cannot be taken at face value.

Professionally, Goth’s career continues at full pace, with recent work including “Frankenstein” and “The Odyssey”. What happens next in the divorce is procedural rather than dramatic: a response, possible negotiation, and a court process that will decide custody and support on evidence rather than headlines. BuzzDaily will report the outcome when the court, not the petition, has spoken.
