Richard Gere, 77, Makes Candid Confession After Decades in Hollywood: “I Don’t Care”

By BuzzDaily Staff
· 9 October 2026

Richard Gere is reflecting on what matters after more than five decades in Hollywood. Speaking with Fox News Digital about the documentary “Wisdom of Happiness”, the 77-year-old actor said other people’s opinions no longer carry the weight they once did. “And frankly, I don’t care that much about it right now, what anyone thinks or says,” he said.

Gere connected that change in outlook to a recent conversation with his eldest son, Homer, who is 26. Remembering himself at 25 and 26, the “Pretty Woman” star said he had been “more susceptible to what people said and thought about me.”

Decades of Buddhist practice have shaped Gere’s belief that lasting peace cannot be supplied by fame, possessions or any other external reward. “Happiness and peace [don’t] come from the outside,” he said, describing that capacity as something already present within each person.

He described greed, anger, jealousy and impatience as passing mental states — “dark clouds coming through”. “They come, and they go,” Gere said, explaining that such feelings do not define the underlying mind.

That philosophy has also guided how Gere views the attention and material rewards attached to a career as one of Hollywood’s most recognisable leading men. His comments frame success less as public approval and more as an inward practice of clarity, perspective and peace.

The conversation was about “Wisdom of Happiness”, the 2024 documentary in which the 14th Dalai Lama speaks directly to the camera about inner peace and compassion. Directed by Barbara Miller and Philip Delaquis, the 90-minute film was executive produced by Gere alongside Oren Moverman, and filmed in the Dalai Lama’s private rooms in Dharamsala in early 2020.

For the interview, Miller used a technique devised by Errol Morris that let her speak face to face with the Dalai Lama while he answered straight into the camera lens — an intimate setup that carries the whole film. The documentary premiered at the Zurich Film Festival in October 2024, opened the Illuminate Film Festival in 2025, reached cinemas in the United States in October 2025, and arrived on digital and video-on-demand on 15 September 2026, on Apple TV and Prime Video.

Gere’s connection to the subject runs deep. A practising Buddhist for more than four decades, he first met the Dalai Lama in the late 1980s and was in Oslo when the Dalai Lama received the Nobel Peace Prize in 1989. He has campaigned for Tibet ever since, and has said his roughly twenty-year absence from the Oscars is tied to that advocacy. He watched the film with his wife, Alejandra Silva.

The film follows the Dalai Lama’s daily practice — he rises at 3:30 in the morning — and his argument that happiness is a discipline built on compassion, not something fame or possessions can supply. In September 2026, a screening at the University of Massachusetts drew a capacity crowd of 2,000 people, with Gere — a UMass alumnus — joining the panel afterwards.
