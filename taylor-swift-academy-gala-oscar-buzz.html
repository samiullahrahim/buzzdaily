Taylor Swift to Close Out Academy Museum Gala as Oscar Buzz Builds Around Her 'Toy Story 5' Song

By BuzzDaily Staff
· 10 October 2026
Photo: Paolo Villanueva / Wikimedia Commons

Taylor Swift is heading back to Hollywood's biggest stages — and this time, she's bringing her Oscar campaign with her.

The Academy Museum of Motion Pictures announced on October 8 that the global superstar will perform at the sixth annual Academy Museum Gala in Los Angeles on October 17, closing out one of the film industry's most prestigious nights of the year. The news instantly set entertainment media alight, with the announcement landing at a moment when Swift's relationship with the film world has never been more closely watched.

The annual gala honors figures who have made lasting contributions to cinema. This year's honorees are filmmaker and composer John Carpenter, the legendary director behind Halloween and The Thing; actor Colman Domingo, whose recent run of acclaimed performances has made him one of Hollywood's most respected leading men; and actress Charlize Theron, the Oscar winner whose career has spanned action blockbusters and searing dramas alike. The evening will be co-chaired by Tom Ford, Robert Rodriguez, Steven Spielberg, and Kate Capshaw, with Rolex returning as the event's exclusive partner and founding supporter.

Amy Homma, the director and president of the Academy Museum and Academy Collection, praised Swift's global influence when announcing the booking, describing her as an extraordinary talent whose work resonates around the world and saying the museum was thrilled to welcome her to close the evening.

Beyond the spectacle, the fundraiser carries real weight. Money raised at the gala supports the Academy Museum's exhibitions, educational programs, public screenings, and film preservation efforts — keeping the museum accessible to visitors of all ages. It is one of the cornerstone events of the fall awards-season calendar, drawing the industry's most influential power players into one room.

And that is exactly why the timing of Swift's appearance matters so much.

Her song "I Knew It, I Knew You," written for Pixar's Toy Story 5, is eligible for the Best Original Song category at the 2027 Academy Awards. The Academy begins preliminary voting in early December, with the shortlists announced on December 15 and final nominations revealed on January 21, 2027. Performing in front of Hollywood's most influential figures just weeks before voting begins is the kind of awards-season positioning that money can't buy — a reminder, delivered live, of exactly what she can do.

Swift, remarkably, has never received an Oscar nomination. It is one of the few major honors still missing from a trophy cabinet that includes a record number of Grammy Album of the Year wins. Her previous forays into film music have all fallen short of the Academy's recognition: "Carolina," her haunting contribution to Where the Crawdads Sing; "Beautiful Ghosts," written for the film adaptation of Cats; and "I Don't Wanna Live Forever," her Fifty Shades Darker collaboration with Zayn. Each generated conversation; none secured the nomination.

Industry watchers say this year could be different. Toy Story 5 is one of the year's biggest family films, and Disney's awards machinery is formidable. An original song performed by one of the most famous musicians on the planet, in one of the most beloved film franchises in history, is precisely the kind of package that Oscar voters notice. The gala performance — placing Swift on stage in front of Academy members and industry tastemakers at the height of campaign season — only sharpens that advantage.

The gala booking caps a remarkable run for Swift. At the 2026 MTV Video Music Awards, she received the show's first-ever Artist Director Honors, earned 11 nominations, and took home Video of the Year for "The Fate of Ophelia." She also premiered the music video for "Patient Zero," which featured surprise cameos from Dakota Johnson and Colin Farrell — a flex of her Hollywood connections that now looks like a preview of this very moment.

For fans hoping to watch, there is a catch: organizers have not announced a public livestream of Swift's performance, and the gala will not air as a televised awards ceremony. The evening is designed as an intimate industry gathering, not a broadcast event — which means the moments that matter will likely surface the way they always do: through a thousand phone cameras and a million social media posts.

For UK audiences watching from across the Atlantic, the story carries its own resonance. Swift's relationship with Britain — from her record-breaking Eras Tour stops to her well-documented love of London — has made her one of the most searched celebrities in the country, and any step she takes toward Hollywood's top honors will be followed just as closely in Manchester and Glasgow as in Los Angeles.

The question now is whether October 17 becomes the night Taylor Swift's Oscar story truly begins. The gala won't guarantee a nomination — nothing does — but it puts her exactly where she needs to be, in front of exactly the people who decide, at exactly the right time. For an artist who has turned timing into an art form, that is no accident.

Hollywood has a way of deciding these things long before the envelopes are opened. On October 17, all eyes will be on the closing performance — and on whether the film industry is finally ready to give Taylor Swift its highest honor.
