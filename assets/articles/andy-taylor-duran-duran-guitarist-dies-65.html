Duran Duran Guitarist Andy Taylor Dies at 65 After ‘Long and Courageous Battle’ With Prostate Cancer

BuzzDaily Staff · Entertainment Desk
· 26 November 2026 · 6 min read
Photo: Luz A. Villa / CC BY 2.0 via Wikimedia Commons · Andy Taylor performing live in Bogotá, 2008 (file photo)

LONDON — Andy Taylor, the original guitarist of Duran Duran and co-writer of some of the defining pop records of the 1980s, has died aged 65. Taylor died peacefully at home on Monday, 23 November 2026, after what his family and former bandmates described as a “long and courageous battle” with metastatic prostate cancer. His death was announced on Wednesday, 25 November, in a joint statement from his family and the band, posted on Duran Duran’s website and social media accounts. His wife, Tracey Jane, shared a separate statement on behalf of the family. The news closed a period of illness that Taylor had faced publicly, and with characteristic directness, for the last four years of his life.

DIAGNOSED IN 2018, PUBLIC IN 2022

Taylor was diagnosed with stage 4 metastatic prostate cancer in 2018, but the illness remained a private matter for four years. It became public in November 2022, in the cruellest of circumstances: Duran Duran were being inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame, and Taylor, his health no longer equal to the occasion, could not attend the ceremony because of his condition. The announcement of his diagnosis on what should have been the classic line-up’s crowning night reframed the evening entirely — from triumph into something closer to a vigil — and introduced millions of fans to a fight he had already been waging, quietly, for years. He spoke about living with the disease in the years that followed, and this week’s announcement returned to the language of that long fight: a battle, his family and bandmates said, that had been both long and courageous.

THE GUITAR BEHIND THE HITS

Taylor completed the five-piece line-up — Simon Le Bon, Nick Rhodes, John Taylor, Roger Taylor and Andy Taylor — that took Duran Duran from Birmingham clubs to the top of the charts on both sides of the Atlantic. His contribution is written into the band’s biggest records: he co-wrote “Rio”, “Hungry Like the Wolf”, “Girls on Film” and “The Reflex”, songs whose blend of funk, rock guitar and glossy pop production defined the New Romantic era. Where Rhodes supplied the art-school sheen and John Taylor the disco pulse, Andy Taylor’s guitar gave the records their muscle — the riff-driven edge that let Duran Duran sit as comfortably on rock radio as on the dancefloor, and in the video age made them pioneers of MTV. The band won two Grammy Awards with him in the ranks, and their biggest songs have never really left the airwaves since.

THE POWER STATION, SOLO WORK AND A FINAL ALBUM

Taylor’s ambitions were never contained by one band. He was a member of The Power Station, the supergroup that took his playing to a harder funk-rock audience, and he recorded as a solo artist in his own right. Periods of distance from Duran Duran over the decades eventually gave way to reconciliation, and his last contribution to the band’s catalogue was a significant one: he played on Danse Macabre, Duran Duran’s most recent studio album. That work is now certain to be remembered as his final recording with the group that made his name — a closing credit that connects the teenage guitarist from Birmingham directly to the band’s twenty-first-century output, more than forty years after “Girls on Film” first charted.

TRIBUTES FROM THE BAND AND BEYOND

The joint announcement was followed by tributes from each of his former bandmates — Le Bon, John Taylor, Rhodes and Roger Taylor — four men who had known Taylor since they were young musicians in Birmingham, and whose statements marked the end of one of British pop’s most famous five-way partnerships. The wider music world joined them: Rod Stewart and Belinda Carlisle were among the artists who paid public tribute. The scale of the reaction was visible in the search data as much as in the statements. On Thursday, 26 November, Taylor’s death was the number one entertainment story on Google Trends in the United Kingdom, Canada and New Zealand, number two in Australia and number three in the United States — the day’s biggest entertainment story across all five countries, and a measure of how completely Duran Duran’s music had travelled.

A BAND ALREADY IN TRANSITION

Taylor’s death lands on a band already absorbing a farewell. Only two weeks earlier, drummer Roger Taylor announced that he was stepping down from Duran Duran and retiring from music because of worsening tinnitus. The two announcements, arriving within a fortnight of each other, close a chapter that began more than four decades ago: of the five young men who conquered the charts in the early 1980s, one has now retired and one has died, and the surviving members are left to carry a catalogue built by all five of them. As BuzzDaily reported in its obituary of Countdown host Molly Meldrum earlier this week, and in its farewell to Love Affair singer Steve Ellis, the tributes for the generation that built modern pop have begun to arrive with a regularity that simply reflects the age of the music itself.

FAMILY, AND DONATIONS IN HIS NAME

Taylor married Tracey in 1982, at the height of Duran Duran’s first fame, and the marriage endured through every phase of his career. He is survived by her, by their four children — Andy, Georgie, Bethy and Izzy — and by his grandson, Albie. In place of flowers, the family has asked that donations be made to Prostate Cancer UK, the charity whose cause Taylor’s very public illness had already done so much to highlight. It is a fitting final note for a musician who, having kept his diagnosis private for four years, spent his last years making sure other men heard about it — and whose guitar, on records that have never left the radio, will go on being heard regardless.
