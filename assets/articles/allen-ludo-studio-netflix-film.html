From Bluey to the Big Screen: Ludo Studio's Alien Adventure 'Allen' Lands on Netflix Today

BuzzDaily Staff · Entertainment Desk
· 12 November 2026 · 7 min read
Photo: Vanderven / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 2.5) · Coolum Beach on Queensland's Sunshine Coast, where Allen's story begins (file photo) · Free licence — full author and licence details on the Commons file page

BRISBANE — The studio that gave the world a family of animated blue heelers has made its boldest leap yet. Allen, the first live-action feature film from Ludo Studio, premieres globally on Netflix today — Thursday 12 November 2026 — from 7.00pm AEDT. The launch follows a hometown celebration: a Brisbane red-carpet premiere last night, Wednesday 11 November, at Angelika Cinemas, where cast members TJ Power, Josh Helman, Emily Barclay and Benedict Hardie walked the carpet alongside co-directors Daley Pearson and Jeffrey Walker. For a Brisbane company whose cartoon export conquered the planet, the film is both a homecoming and a first — a live-action feature, shot entirely in Queensland, about the friend who came back from the stars.

FROM BLUEY TO LIVE ACTION

Ludo Studio is the Brisbane-based, Emmy and BAFTA-winning company behind Bluey, the global phenomenon that turned seven-minute episodes about a Queensland family into one of the most beloved children's programmes on Earth. Allen is something else entirely: the studio's first live-action feature film, created by Daley Pearson, co-directed by Pearson and Jeffrey Walker — whose credits include Apple Cider Vinegar and The Artful Dodger — and co-written by Pearson with Sophie Miller, of Thou Shalt Not Steal. The step from animation to live action is the risk at the centre of the release: everything Ludo is famous for — warmth, humour, emotional precision — now has to survive contact with real actors, real locations and one very unreal visitor. It joins a busy season for the streamer, which BuzzDaily has also covered through Ben Affleck's Netflix thriller 'Animals', though few Netflix titles this year carry a production story quite like this one.

THE STORY: THE FRIEND WHO CAME BACK

In 1998, in Coolum Beach on the Sunshine Coast, a 10-year-old boy named Petey befriended a small alien named Allen. Then Allen vanished — without a goodbye. Twenty years later, the adult Petey, played by TJ Power, is still living in his childhood home, his life paused at the moment his friend disappeared — until Allen returns, and the reunion neither of them got to have as children becomes the film's beating heart. Charlotte Nicdao, Josh Helman, Emily Barclay and Benedict Hardie co-star. The premise is a fable about arrested development in the most literal sense: a man who never quite grew up, because growing up meant accepting that the most important friendship of his life ended without explanation. At about 105 minutes, the film gives that small, strange idea feature-length room to breathe.

MADE ENTIRELY IN QUEENSLAND

Allen was filmed entirely in Queensland — across Brisbane, the Sunshine Coast and the Gold Coast — and based at Screen Queensland Studios, with principal photography beginning in July 2025. The geography is not decoration: Coolum Beach, where the story opens in 1998, is the real Sunshine Coast town whose shoreline anchors the film's sense of place, and the production's footprint is a showcase for the state's screen industry — the same Queensland production ecosystem BuzzDaily has reported on as major features choose Australia as their filming base. A global Netflix premiere for a film made wholly in the state where its story is set is the kind of full-circle release local industries dream about.

BUILDING ALLEN: EIGHT PUPPETS AND A FAILED ANIMATRONIC

Allen himself was built the old way. The creature was created by Brisbane house Formation Effects, which produced eight puppets for the production — and one animatronic that failed, a detail that says much about the film's practical philosophy. Rather than conjuring its alien from pixels, the production put a physical creature in front of its actors, with all the craft and all the risk that involves: eight versions of Allen, built by hand in Brisbane, and a mechanical one that did not survive the attempt. The score, meanwhile, comes from a familiar Ludo name — Joff Bush, Bluey's composer, whose music carried the studio's animated world and now scores its first step into live action.

GRIEF, AND “PRETTY AGGRESSIVE NODS” TO E.T.

Beneath the adventure sits the reason Pearson made the film. He told The Guardian that Allen draws on the grief of losing his brother Liam — the story of a friend who vanishes without goodbye, and the man still waiting in the childhood home two decades later, is a fable built from real loss. Pearson also acknowledged the film carries what he called “pretty aggressive nods” to E.T., including a poster that echoes the famous finger-touch between boy and alien. The lineage is worn openly: a child, a visitor, a departure without closure — and, this time, a return. The official trailer dropped on 28 October 2026, and the premiere-week programme continues with a free “Making of Allen” panel at the State Library of Queensland on Tuesday 17 November, where the production's Queensland story will be told by the people who made it.

TODAY'S THE DAY

From 7.00pm AEDT tonight, Allen is available to Netflix subscribers worldwide — a Brisbane story, built by a Brisbane studio, performed by an Australian cast and filmed on Queensland beaches, arriving everywhere at once. Ludo Studio conquered the world once with seven-minute cartoons about a family of dogs. Tonight it tries something harder: asking the world to fall for a small alien, a grown man who never left home, and the goodbye that took twenty years to finish.
