Olivia Rodrigo Releases First-Ever Christmas Song ‘Take Me Home (4 Christmas)’ — With a Star-Studded Music Video

BuzzDaily Staff · US Desk
· 5 November 2026 · 7 min read
Photo: Raph_PH · CC BY 4.0 · via Wikimedia Commons · Olivia Rodrigo performing at the Glastonbury Festival, June 2025 · Free licence — full author and licence details on the Commons file page (file photo)

LOS ANGELES — Olivia Rodrigo has spent 2026 breaking records, but until today she had never done the most traditional thing a pop star can do: release a Christmas song. That changes this evening. “Take Me Home (4 Christmas)”, her first-ever original Christmas single, arrives today, Thursday 5 November, at 5 p.m. Pacific / 8 p.m. Eastern — 1 a.m. GMT on Friday — and it does not arrive quietly. The song lands with a music video starring two performers nobody would have predicted on a Rodrigo release: Cynthia Nixon of Sex and the City and Ben Marshall of Saturday Night Live.

Rodrigo announced the single herself on Instagram on Monday 2 November, telling fans simply that it was “my first Christmas song”. The artwork made the theme impossible to miss: Rodrigo in a red Santa hat and red lipstick, a deliberately classic piece of festive imagery for an artist whose visual world usually runs to purple, black and Y2K nostalgia. Within hours the announcement had become one of the most-shared music posts of the week — and it set up one of the earliest, and biggest, openings of the 2026 festive season.

THE SONG: NIGRO, ALLEN AND A LOVE STORY

“Take Me Home (4 Christmas)” was co-written by Rodrigo with Dan Nigro and Amy Allen, and produced by Nigro. The credits matter. Nigro is the producer and songwriting partner behind the whole of Rodrigo’s career to date — the collaborator who helped turn a Disney Channel actor into the defining pop songwriter of her generation — while Allen is one of the most in-demand pop writers of the moment. A first Christmas song might have been treated as a novelty assignment, handed to outside writers for a quick seasonal cash-in. Instead, Rodrigo has written it with her core team, which suggests she intends it to stand alongside her albums rather than beneath them.

As for what the song is about, her official webstore — as reported by Paste Magazine — describes it as a track about “the excitement and nerves of spending the holidays with someone you love.” That framing fits the Rodrigo songbook precisely: her best-known songs have always lived in the gap between wanting something and fearing it, and Christmas, with its forced proximity, old memories and new relationships, is unusually rich territory for that tension. A Rodrigo Christmas song was probably never going to be about sleigh bells.

A VIDEO WITH TWO VERY DIFFERENT STARS

The music video premieres at the same moment as the song, and its casting is the release’s second headline. Cynthia Nixon — Miranda Hobbes to a generation of Sex and the City viewers, and a Tony Award-winning stage actor — stars alongside Ben Marshall, the Saturday Night Live performer known to younger audiences as one third of the Please Don’t Destroy comedy trio. It is a pairing that spans roughly four decades of television comedy, and it underlines something Rodrigo has done consistently well: borrowing cultural weight from outside her own demographic without ever looking like she is trying too hard. Whether Nixon and Marshall play a couple, a family or something stranger is a question the premiere will answer tonight; the casting alone has already done its job of making a Christmas single feel like an event.

VINYL, CASSETTE AND A B-SIDE

The release is also being given the full physical treatment. A limited-edition 7-inch vinyl and a cassette edition are available to pre-order now, with copies reaching stores on Friday 6 November. Both formats carry a B-side, “miss this christmas” — a second, otherwise unreleased festive track that cannot be streamed as part of the main single. For an artist whose fanbase collects physical editions as a matter of identity, the strategy is familiar from her album campaigns, and it gives collectors a reason to move quickly: limited Christmas pressings, by definition, have a short season in which to sell.

A BANNER YEAR TO END

The timing of the single is no accident. Rodrigo’s third studio album, you seem pretty sad for a girl so in love, arrived in June 2026 and produced a Billboard Hot 100 No. 1 in “Drop Dead”, while the album itself spent three weeks on top of the Billboard 200. She reaches this Christmas release, in other words, at the commercial peak of her career so far — the point at which a first festive single stops being a curiosity and starts being a genuine chart event. She is also not the only major artist moving early this year: in the UK, Elton John and CMAT have today released a new version of “Step Into Christmas” in a bid for the Christmas No. 1, a reminder that the festive chart race now begins in the first week of November.

WHY A FIRST CHRISTMAS SONG MATTERS

Analysis — Christmas songs occupy a strange place in pop. Recorded once, the right one returns every December for decades, outliving the albums and eras that produced it; the wrong one is forgotten by January. For an artist of Rodrigo’s songwriting reputation, the stake is higher than for most: her catalogue is built on specificity and emotional honesty, and festive music punishes both vagueness and cynicism. The early signs — her own writers, her own producer, a video cast like a short film, and a physical campaign with an exclusive B-side — point to an artist treating the format seriously rather than lending her name to it. Whether “Take Me Home (4 Christmas)” joins the permanent December rotation will not be known for several Christmases yet. But as of 5 p.m. Pacific tonight, Olivia Rodrigo is officially part of the season.
