Avengers: Doomsday Will Run About Two and a Half Hours, Joe Russo Confirms — Shorter Than Endgame

BuzzDaily Staff · US Desk
· 23 November 2026 · 8 min read
Photo: Gage Skidmore / CC BY-SA 2.0 via Wikimedia Commons · Joe Russo speaking at San Diego Comic-Con, 2019 (file photo)

DOHA — Avengers: Doomsday will run about two and a half hours, co-director Joe Russo has confirmed — a runtime that would make Marvel’s next Avengers film notably shorter than Avengers: Endgame. Speaking at Doha Industry Days in Qatar on Saturday, 21 November 2026, Russo described the film’s teasers and character reveals as “pieces of something that are going to equal two-and-a-half hours of your time on December 18”, according to Screen Daily, via Deadline. The remark is the clearest official word yet on the length of the film, which opens in cinemas on 18 December 2026 with Robert Downey Jr. as Doctor Doom.

THE PRECISE NUMBER DOING THE ROUNDS

Russo’s “two-and-a-half hours” has a more precise unofficial counterpart. The Brazilian outlet Omelete reported on 16 November, citing anonymous sources, a runtime of 2 hours 29 minutes 36 seconds for the standard version of the film, and 2 hours 30 minutes 55 seconds for the Infinity Vision version. Those figures should be treated as a report, not a confirmation: Disney declined to comment on them, and no ratings board has yet published a certified runtime for the film. Until a classification body or the studio itself puts a number on record, the only figure with a director’s name attached to it is Russo’s — two and a half hours, or thereabouts.

SHORTER THAN ENDGAME, LEVEL WITH INFINITY WAR

Put against the Russos’ previous Avengers films, the reported length is a statement of intent. Avengers: Infinity War ran 2 hours 29 minutes — almost exactly the figure Omelete reported — while Avengers: Endgame ran 3 hours 1 minute, a three-hour epic that asked a great deal of its audience and was rewarded with one of the biggest box-office totals in history. Joe Russo’s brother and co-director Anthony Russo gave the same ballpark to Fandango in September, calling Doomsday “a two-and-a-half-hour movie, or so”. An earlier figure — 165 minutes, or 2 hours 45 minutes, reported by The Hollywood Reporter in July — was never final, and the directors’ repeated “two and a half” now looks like the settled answer.

THE ROAD TO 18 DECEMBER

The runtime news lands in the middle of an accelerating publicity campaign. A sneak peek at the film is set for 30 November, during The Wonderful World of Disney: Holiday Spectacular on ABC, and the film’s global tour has already launched at the Las Vegas Grand Prix. The release date itself sets up one of the biggest box-office clashes in years: Avengers: Doomsday opens on 18 December 2026, the same day as Dune: Part Three. Downey Jr.’s return to Marvel as Victor von Doom remains the film’s central talking point — and, as BuzzDaily reported in its coverage of Sir Patrick Stewart’s death, the shape of the returning X-Men generation in the film is a question Marvel has still not fully answered.

WHY THE RUNTIME MATTERS

Runtime is not a trivial detail for a film of this scale. Every minute of an Avengers movie shapes how many screenings a cinema can fit into a day, how the opening weekend is scheduled, and how comfortably the film plays for family audiences over the Christmas period. Russo’s phrasing in Doha is also worth noting: he reached for the runtime while talking about the teasers and character reveals, presenting the drip-feed of announcements as pieces of a single two-and-a-half-hour whole. It was a director’s way of saying the marketing fragments add up to a defined, finished object — not an ever-expanding one. For exhibitors planning the busiest fortnight of their year, with Dune: Part Three opening the same day, a firm two-and-a-half hours is a number they can build a schedule around.

The comment also closes down one line of speculation that has followed the film for months. Through the autumn, reported runtimes swung between the Hollywood Reporter’s 165 minutes and vaguer claims of a three-hour cut to rival Endgame. The directors have now given the same answer twice, in September and again in November, on two continents: about two and a half hours. Unless the certified listing surprises everyone, the debate is over — and attention can return to the question the runtime was always standing in for, which is what the Russos have fitted into it.

A two-and-a-half-hour Avengers film is, by the standards the franchise set with Endgame, an exercise in restraint — closer to the propulsive Infinity War than to its three-hour sequel. Whether the reported 2 hours 29 minutes survives contact with a ratings board listing is a question for the coming weeks. On the directors’ own repeated word, though, audiences booking their 18 December tickets should plan for two and a half hours of Doom — and not a minute of Endgame-sized sprawl more.
