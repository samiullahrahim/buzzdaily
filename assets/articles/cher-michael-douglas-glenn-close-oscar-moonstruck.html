Cher's Four-Word Reply to Michael Douglas Over Her 'Moonstruck' Oscar: 'Cher Adores Michael Douglas'

BuzzDaily Staff · US Desk
· 14 November 2026 · 8 min read
Photo: David Carroll / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 2.0) · Cher performing on stage during the Dressed to Kill tour (file photo) · Free licence — full author and licence details on the Commons file page

LOS ANGELES — It took Michael Douglas one talk-show answer to reopen one of Hollywood's oldest Oscar arguments — and it took Cher exactly four words to shut it down again. Appearing on The Graham Norton Show, in an episode broadcast on 30 October 2026, Douglas said that his Fatal Attraction co-star Glenn Close deserved to win the Best Actress Oscar for that film — the award Cher won in 1988 for Moonstruck. When the remark reached Cher's camp, her representative gave Page Six a response of elegant brevity, published on 4 November: “Cher adores Michael Douglas.”

FOUR WORDS, NO FEUD

In an industry that usually answers a perceived slight with a carefully lawyered statement, Cher's reply did the opposite. There was no counter-attack, no relitigating of the 1988 ceremony, and no complaint about Douglas's loyalty to his old co-star — just affection, offered in four words and left to speak for itself. The exchange travelled quickly precisely because the quote was irresistible. Just Jared had covered Douglas's Norton comments on 3 November; TMZ reported the exchange on 4 November, the same day Page Six published Cher's response. What could have been packaged as a feud between three Hollywood veterans in their eighties instead became a small masterclass in how to decline one.

WHAT DOUGLAS ACTUALLY SAID

Douglas's comment, made on Norton's sofa in the episode aired on 30 October, was an act of advocacy rather than an attack. He and Close led Fatal Attraction together in 1987, in the thriller that became one of the defining hits of the decade, and his argument was that Close's performance in it deserved the Best Actress Oscar. Read in context, the remark was about Close — and about her long, famous wait for a competitive Oscar — rather than about diminishing the performance that beat her. Douglas, now 82, was championing the co-star opposite whom he gave one of his own most celebrated performances, nearly four decades after they made the film.

THE 1988 RACE

The award at the centre of the exchange is one of the most re-argued in Oscar history. At the 1988 ceremony, Cher won Best Actress for Moonstruck, the romantic comedy that turned a pop superstar into an Academy Award winner and remains the performance most associated with her acting career. Close was nominated the same year for Fatal Attraction. The two performances — one all warmth and comic timing, the other ice-cold menace — have been compared ever since, usually by people taking sides. Cher, born on 20 May 1946 and now 80, has never needed to re-argue the verdict; the statuette, and the film's enduring reputation, have done the arguing for her.

CLOSE, THE HONORARY OSCAR AND THE ‘CONSOLATION PRIZE’

If Douglas's timing was pointed, it was also understandable, because Close's Oscar story is about to change. Close, 79, has eight competitive Oscar nominations and has never won — one of the most cited statistics in awards season — but this month, in November 2026, she receives an honorary Oscar at the Governors Awards. Close herself has joked that the honour is a consolation prize, a line that has only underlined how graciously she has carried a record many actors would find galling. Douglas's comment landed just weeks before that ceremony, in the middle of an awards season BuzzDaily has been tracking since the autumn — including the Oscar buzz that built around the Academy Museum Gala — and it read, above all, as one final piece of advocacy before the Academy finally puts a statuette in her hands.

CHER AT 80: THE MEMOIR AND THE FOUNDATION

The lightness of the reply also fits where Cher is in her own career. This fall sees the publication of Cher: The Memoir, Part Two, the second volume of the life story she began telling in print with Part One — a project that has put her in a reflective, legacy-defining mode. Alongside it, she hosts charity dinners for her Cher Cares Foundation, the philanthropic vehicle that has become a fixture of her public life. At 80, with a memoir in shops and a foundation to run, Cher is long past the point of needing to defend a win the Academy awarded her 38 years ago — which may be exactly why four warm words were all the story required.

WHY FOUR WORDS WERE ENOUGH

Any conventional response would have made the story bigger and smaller at the same time. A defence of the Moonstruck win would have started the feud narrative the headline writers wanted; silence would have been read as a snub. Affection disarmed both readings at once. Douglas's remark, after all, was pro-Close rather than anti-Cher, and Cher's representative answered it in the spirit it was offered — as one veteran speaking fondly of another, about work they both did in the 1980s that audiences still argue about today. “Cher adores Michael Douglas” is not a rebuttal. It is a full stop, placed precisely where a feud was supposed to begin.

The Governors Awards take place this month, and when Close finally holds an Oscar, the last open file from the 1988 Best Actress race closes with it. Cher's name stays in the record books where it has always been, Douglas's loyalty to his co-star is on the record too — and the exchange ends, fittingly, with the shortest and warmest quote of the three. Four words were enough.
