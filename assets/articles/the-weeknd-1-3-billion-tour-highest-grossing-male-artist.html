The Weeknd’s $1.3 Billion Tour Becomes the Highest-Grossing Ever by a Male Artist

BuzzDaily Staff · US Desk
· 6 November 2026 · 7 min read
Photo: Ludovic Delot · CC BY-SA 4.0 · via Wikimedia Commons · The Weeknd performing at Foro Sol, Mexico City, September 2023, on the After Hours Til Dawn tour · Free licence — full author and licence details on the Commons file page (file photo)

KUALA LUMPUR — It ended, four years and 176 shows after it began, in a stadium in Kuala Lumpur. The Weeknd played the final show of his After Hours Til Dawn stadium tour on Wednesday 4 November at TM Stadium National in the Malaysian capital, closing a 17-date run through Asia. When promoter Live Nation added up the final numbers, the tour stood at more than $1.3 billion grossed — the highest-grossing tour ever staged by a male artist.

The figure, confirmed by Live Nation and reported by USA Today and Pollstar, moves The Weeknd past one of the most durable records in live music. Elton John’s Farewell Yellow Brick Road tour — the farewell trek that ran from 2018 to 2023 across 330 shows — grossed more than $939 million, and until this week it stood as the benchmark for a male artist. The Weeknd has now cleared it by more than $360 million in barely half the number of shows. It is a fitting week for the record to change hands: John is back in the headlines himself, having teamed up with CMAT for a new version of “Step Into Christmas” in a bid for the 2026 Christmas number one.

THIRD IN HISTORY — BEHIND ONLY SWIFT AND COLDPLAY

In the all-time table, After Hours Til Dawn now sits third. Ahead of it remain only two tours: Taylor Swift’s Eras Tour, which grossed more than $2 billion, and Coldplay’s Music of the Spheres tour, at more than $1.5 billion. As Pollstar notes, the three now form an exclusive billion-dollar club — the only tours in history to pass ten figures at the box office. That a solo R&B and pop artist now keeps that company underlines how completely the economics of stadium touring have been rewritten in the streaming era; Swift’s own record-setting run is a story BuzzDaily has followed closely, most recently at the Academy Museum Gala in Los Angeles.

THE NUMBERS BEHIND THE RECORD

The scale of the run is best understood in totals. Since launching in July 2022, the tour played 176 shows in 67 cities across 30 countries and five continents, selling more than 9 million tickets, according to Live Nation. It originally launched in support of his albums After Hours and Dawn FM, and eventually incorporated the concluding chapter of the trilogy, Hurry Up Tomorrow, released in January 2025. What began as a stadium production built around two albums became, by increments, one of the longest and largest global tours ever mounted by a single artist — and the first by a male artist to pass a billion dollars.

A FINAL YEAR BIGGER THAN MOST TOURS’ LIFETIMES

The most startling number in Live Nation’s tally may be the most recent one. In 2026 alone — the tour’s fifth calendar year — After Hours Til Dawn grossed more than $500 million and sold more than 3 million tickets. Put plainly, the final year of a four-year-old production out-earned the entire lifetime gross of all but a handful of tours in history. Demand, unusually, grew as the run went on rather than fading, and the closing leg took the production somewhere it had never been: Asia, for the first time in the tour’s history.

SEVENTEEN SOLD-OUT SHOWS TO FINISH

The Asia run that closed the tour comprised 17 sold-out stadium shows, including three nights in Bangkok and four nights at Hong Kong’s Kai Tak Stadium, before the Kuala Lumpur finale. Ending a tour of this size in a market it had never played — rather than with a victory lap in North America or Europe — underlined the point the grosses had already made: by 2026, The Weeknd’s audience was no longer centred on any one continent, but genuinely global, and still expanding into new territory in the tour’s final weeks.

GIVING BACK ALONG THE WAY

Live Nation also reported that $8.5 million has been donated to the XO Humanitarian Fund and Global Citizen over the course of the tour, supporting hunger relief and education initiatives worldwide. The charitable thread ran alongside the commercial one for the full four years, a standing feature of a production that was setting box-office records on five continents.

WHAT THE RECORD MEANS

Analysis — Farewell tours by legacy artists were supposed to own this corner of the record book: decades of catalogue, a final-chance premium on tickets, and hundreds of dates played over years. Elton John’s 330-show farewell fitted that model exactly. The Weeknd has broken it with a different one — a single, continuously evolving stadium production, carried by streaming-era hits, staged at a spectacle scale that made every date an event, and extended for exactly as long as demand kept rising. Whether anyone catches him soon is an open question: Swift’s Eras total remains out of reach, and Coldplay still sit ahead of him. But the male-artist record changed hands this week for the first time in years, and on the evidence of that $500 million final year, it may prove just as durable as the one it replaced.
