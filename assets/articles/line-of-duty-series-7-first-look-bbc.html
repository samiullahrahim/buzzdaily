Line of Duty Series 7 First Look: BBC Reveals Arnott, Fleming and Hastings Return as AC-12 Is Disbanded — and Robert Carlyle Joins as Guest Lead

BuzzDaily Staff · Entertainment Desk
· 12 November 2026 · 7 min read
Photo: Christopher William Adach / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 2.0) · Vicky McClure, who returns as Kate Fleming (file photo) · Free licence — full author and licence details on the Commons file page

LONDON — Bent coppers, be warned: the interview room lights are back on. The BBC released the first-look images for Line of Duty series 7 on Thursday 12 November 2026, confirming that Martin Compston, Vicky McClure and Adrian Dunbar all return as Steve Arnott, Kate Fleming and Ted Hastings. It is the trio's first series together in almost six years — series 6 aired in 2021 — and the images arrive with a twist that resets the show's foundations: AC-12, the anti-corruption unit at the heart of the drama since 2012, has been disbanded. In its place stands a rebranded successor, the Inspectorate of Professional Standards, and into that new world steps this series' guest lead: Robert Carlyle.

THE TRIO RETURNS, ALMOST SIX YEARS ON

Some reunions are casting news; this one is an event. Compston's Arnott, McClure's Fleming and Dunbar's Hastings became, across six series, the most scrutinised partnership in British television — every interview-room scene a national conversation, every “bent copper” a catchphrase. Series 6 aired in 2021, and its finale was one of the BBC's biggest-ever ratings events, the kind of shared, appointment-viewing moment that streaming-era television is supposed to have made impossible. That the three actors return together — not one or two of them, but the full triangle of loyalty, suspicion and command — is the foundation everything else in series 7 is built on. British drama is in a reunion mood this autumn, as BuzzDaily's coverage of James Acaster's extraordinary Celebrity Traitors exit showed, but no returning ensemble carries quite this weight of expectation.

AC-12 IS DEAD. LONG LIVE THE INSPECTORATE

The boldest detail in the first look is institutional. AC-12 has been disbanded and rebranded as the Inspectorate of Professional Standards — a change that does more than repaint the office door. Across six series, AC-12's survival was always precarious, its officers perpetually one restructure away from abolition; series 7 simply makes the threat real. A new name means new politics, new oversight and a new question hanging over Arnott, Fleming and Hastings: what does it mean to hunt corruption inside a unit that no longer exists, wearing the badge of its replacement? Creator Jed Mercurio has built the show's tension on procedure as much as plot, and a disbanded unit gives him a fresh procedural battlefield — the investigators themselves now working inside a structure designed, perhaps, to watch them.

ROBERT CARLYLE JOINS AS GUEST LEAD SHAUN MASSIE

Every series of Line of Duty is defined by its guest lead, and series 7 has cast a heavyweight. Robert Carlyle joins as Police Constable Shaun Massie, a Specialist Rifle Officer and veteran marksman serving with Tactical Operations Unit 7 — TO-7. Massie is a quiet man living a quiet life, and it is thrown into turmoil when his commanding officer, DI Dominic Gough — played by Tom Weston-Jones — is accused of sexual misconduct. It is a storyline that puts the guest lead in the show's classic position: neither straightforward suspect nor straightforward victim, but the still point around which an investigation begins to turn. Newcomer Laura Aikman also joins the cast as DC Paula Beckman, adding a fresh face to the unit's ranks as the old order is rearranged around her.

MERCURIO BEHIND THE CAMERA, BELFAST ON SCREEN

Series 7 runs to six parts, created and written by Jed Mercurio, who also directs the final three episodes — a level of direct authorial control that underlines how personally he is steering the show's return. The series was filmed in Belfast in spring 2026, continuing the production's long association with Northern Ireland, and is made by World Productions, part of ITV Studios, the company behind the show since its earliest days. Six episodes, a disbanded unit, a new guest lead and the original trio: the architecture is deliberately familiar in shape and deliberately unsettled in detail, which has always been the Mercurio method.

WHEN WILL IT AIR? EARLY 2027 — AND NOTHING MORE PRECISE

The BBC confirms the series for early 2027, and that is where the confirmed facts end. A January 2027 slot has been reported, but it remains unconfirmed, and BuzzDaily is stating it as exactly that — reported, not scheduled. What is confirmed is the road here: series 7 was commissioned in November 2025, almost five years after series 6 ended, a gap that turned the show's return into one of British television's longest-running questions. The first-look images are the answer's first instalment. UK audiences, meanwhile, are hardly short of event television in the meantime — from the stage, where BuzzDaily reported Sacha Baron Cohen's Tarantino stage play, to Saturday-night finals such as Cate McHugh's win on The Voice UK, the appetite that made series 6's finale a ratings landmark has not gone anywhere.

THE INTERVIEW ROOM AWAITS

There is a reason first-look photographs of a police procedural can lead the entertainment news. Line of Duty is not merely returning; it is returning changed — its unit abolished, its name replaced, its officers older and its guest lead drawn from the top rank of British screen acting. Arnott, Fleming and Hastings are back at the table. The Inspectorate of Professional Standards is open for business. And somewhere in TO-7, a veteran marksman named Shaun Massie is about to have his quiet life dismantled, one interview at a time. Mother of God, indeed.
