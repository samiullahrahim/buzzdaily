The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping Divides Critics but Audiences Love It — 95% Score Is a Franchise Record

BuzzDaily Staff · US Desk
· 21 November 2026 · 8 min read
Photo: Kevin Paul / CC BY 4.0 via Wikimedia Commons · McKenna Grace at the WWD Style Awards, 2026

LOS ANGELES — The Hunger Games is back in cinemas, and the argument has already started. The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping opened on Friday, 20 November, in cinemas and IMAX, directed by Francis Lawrence and adapted from Suzanne Collins's 2025 novel — and within hours of the first screenings it had produced the biggest split between critics and audiences in the franchise's history — a 27-point gap between its two Rotten Tomatoes scores, as ScreenRant's franchise-wide comparison showed on 20 November: a mixed critical reception on one side, and a 95 per cent audience score, the highest any Hunger Games film has ever recorded, on the other.

Start with the critics, because their number is the one still moving. When the review embargo lifted on Tuesday, 17 November, the first counts put the film in the high 60s and 70s: NME reported 75 per cent from 80 reviews as the verdicts arrived. By Friday, 20 November, with the count much larger, Deadline put the Tomatometer at around 68 per cent from 135 reviews. Those figures are not contradictions so much as snapshots of a score in motion — the denominator keeps growing, and the percentage keeps shifting with it. Metacritic, which weights its scores differently, has the film at 56 out of 100 from 39 critic reviews, squarely in its “mixed or average” band.

THE AUDIENCE VERDICT: A FRANCHISE RECORD

Audiences, meanwhile, are not split at all. The film's Popcornmeter score on Rotten Tomatoes stands at 95 per cent from more than 500 verified ratings — the highest audience score in Hunger Games history, as ScreenRant reported on 20 November. The previous record was an 89 per cent tie between Catching Fire (2013) and the 2023 prequel The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes. Against the critics' figure, that is a 27-point gap between the two scores — the kind of divide that usually belongs to cult comedies and horror films, not to the sixth instalment of a billion-dollar franchise.

Parade's report captured the mood in the auditorium: moviegoers were left in tears by a story whose ending, in outline at least, they have known for years. Critics, the magazine noted, have been cooler — several questioning whether the franchise still has anything new to offer — even while conceding the emotional force of the film itself. For comparison, Catching Fire remains the critics' favourite of the series, at 90 per cent.

WHAT THE CRITICS ACTUALLY SAID

NME's verdict on the verdicts was that the prequel is “dividing critics”, and the spread of reviews it collected shows why. The Guardian's Peter Bradshaw gave the film one star, calling it “yet another iteration of the utterly exhausted, unbearably extended and now surreally boring Hunger Games franchise”. The Times's Kevin Maher awarded two stars. At the other end, Time Out gave four stars, judging it “far stronger than so many fished-from-the-bin franchise revivals”, and TheWrap suggested it “may even be the Hunger Games film that holds up best”. A film cannot be both exhausted and the best in the series — which is rather the point: this is a genuinely contested release, not a consensus disappointment.

BACK TO PANEM, 24 YEARS EARLIER

The film is set 24 years before the first novel and tells the story of the 50th Hunger Games — the Second Quarter Quell — through the young Haymitch Abernathy, the District 12 victor who would grow up to mentor Katniss Everdeen. Joseph Zada plays the young Haymitch, with McKenna Grace as Maysilee Donner, Ralph Fiennes as President Snow and Jesse Plemons also starring. The original trilogy's leads return — Jennifer Lawrence, Woody Harrelson and Josh Hutcherson all appear — a reunion BuzzDaily reported when the cast gathered for the film's premiere earlier this week. At 158 minutes, it is a substantial sit, and Francis Lawrence, who has directed every Hunger Games film except the first, is back behind the camera.

Grace's week has been remarkable in its own right: the actor has been trending in the United States' top 10 entertainment searches in her own name, with more than 20,000 searches, on the strength of this film alone. The film's music has travelled with it, too — singer-songwriter Gigi Perez wrote her song “Favor” from personal grief for the film, a story BuzzDaily told last month.

THE BOX OFFICE: SECOND-BEST PREVIEWS IN FRANCHISE HISTORY

Whatever the critics decide, the opening numbers are not in dispute. Thursday previews took $21.25 million — including $5 million from Wednesday's early-access premium-format screenings, according to Deadline's figures — the second-best preview total in franchise history, behind only Catching Fire. Early estimates for the domestic opening weekend, reported by Deadline, put the film at $90–120 million, which would return the series to the nine-figure openings of the original run, after The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes opened to $44.6 million in 2023.

The fuller picture arrives over the weekend: Friday's actual takings today, studio estimates on Sunday, and a Tomatometer that will keep moving as more reviews are counted. But the shape of the story is already clear. Critics see a franchise repeating itself; audiences see the film they waited for. On the evidence of the previews and that record 95 per cent, it is the audiences who are buying the tickets.
