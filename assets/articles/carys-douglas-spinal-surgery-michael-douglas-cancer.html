Carys Douglas Opens Up About the Spinal Surgery That Ended Her Dancing Dreams — and Her Father's Cancer

BuzzDaily Staff · UK Desk
· 21 November 2026 · 8 min read
Photo: David Shankbone / CC BY 3.0 via Wikimedia Commons · Catherine Zeta-Jones at the Vanity Fair Oscar Party, 2012 (file photo)

LONDON — Carys Douglas has given the most personal interview of her young career. The 23-year-old actor — the daughter of Catherine Zeta-Jones, 57, and Michael Douglas, 82 — is the cover star of ELLE Spain's latest issue, published on Thursday, 19 November, and in a wide-ranging conversation she has opened up about the spinal surgery that ended her childhood ambition, and about what it meant to watch her father fight cancer when she was seven years old.

THE SURGERY THAT ENDED THE DANCING

Dance came first. Carys grew up watching her mother — a formidable dancer long before she was a film star — and took classes of her own, with professional ambitions. Those ended at thirteen. “I used to dance a lot, but I had spinal surgery when I was thirteen,” she told the magazine, in comments reported by Hello Magazine. “I still dance, although not professionally or in competitions anymore.”

The interview is full of the household behind the famous names. She recalled refusing, as a girl, to be taught tap dancing by her own mother — “I told her I didn't want to learn from my mother” — before Zeta-Jones demonstrated a few steps. “Then she did a few steps, and I was stunned: ‘Wow, that's impressive. I'm not going to compete with that.’”

“MY FATHER COULD HAVE DIED WHEN I WAS SEVEN”

The hardest passages concern her father. Michael Douglas was diagnosed with throat cancer in 2010, when Carys was seven; he beat the disease in 2011. “As a child, I didn't fully understand that my father could have died when I was seven,” she said, in the interview reported by PEOPLE on 20 November. “I knew it was serious, but only now can I begin to imagine what it would have been like to grow up without him.”

“His illness brought us even closer and made me think about mortality,” she added. She was candid, too, about the arithmetic of having a father six decades her senior: “Having an older father is an enormous gift; however, sometimes I wish he weren't 82, even though I am very grateful for his experience and wisdom. I don't think I'll ever fully understand death or find a definitive answer.”

THE “NEPO BABY” WARNING

Acting, when it came, came with a warning attached. Carys told ELLE Spain her parents had not hidden from her that people might have “preconceived notions” about her career because of the “nepo baby” label — and that building her own identity was her responsibility: “They taught me that building my own identity depended on me and no one else. It was my responsibility, and it still is.” Zeta-Jones has previously called the term “horrific”, and the family's position is that the work decides. Michael Douglas, himself the son of Kirk Douglas, has said he is not a fan of the label either.

BUILDING HER OWN RÉSUMÉ

The résumé, so far, has been built the slow way. Carys graduated from Brown University in 2025, with a degree in Film and International Relations, and this year made her professional stage debut as Nina in Chekhov's The Seagull in Brooklyn, New York — one of the great test-piece roles for a young actor. Her next film is The Problem With Poets, written and directed by Jake Hoffman, in which she appears alongside Scoot McNairy, Taissa Farmiga and Debra Messing. Her father, meanwhile, remains a headline in his own right — most recently when Cher's four-word reply to him over her Moonstruck Oscar resurfaced, as BuzzDaily reported on 14 November.

The interview caps a month in which the family has been unusually visible together. On 11 November, Carys attended the Gucci x Moda Operandi event in New York wearing her mother's black lace Gucci top from 1996 — the one Zeta-Jones wore to the premiere of The Long Kiss Goodnight on 8 October 1996. A week earlier, on 4 November, she and her brother Dylan supported their father at the Tibet House US Art of Freedom Gala, where Michael Douglas received a Lifetime Achievement Award for his humanitarian work.

There is context, too, in how she was raised. Carys and her brother Dylan, 26 — who has also gone into acting — spent much of their early childhood in Bermuda, where the family lived largely out of the spotlight. At home, she has recalled, her parents encouraged the children to join conversations about everything from politics to philosophy, and expected them to hold their own. The interview arrives, too, in a busy season for her father, whose memoir, One Helluva Ride, was published in October — one more reason the family's past, and its future, is being talked about at once.

It is, in other words, a carefully managed emergence: a stage debut in Brooklyn rather than a franchise launch, a degree finished before the cover stories began. The surgery at thirteen closed one stage door. The interview suggests Carys Douglas intends to open the next one herself.
