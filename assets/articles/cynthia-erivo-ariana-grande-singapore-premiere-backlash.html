Cynthia Erivo Speaks Out on Backlash Over Protecting Ariana Grande at Singapore Premiere

BuzzDaily Staff · Entertainment Desk
· 28 October 2026 · 6 min read
Photo: Kevin Paul · CC BY 4.0 · via Wikimedia Commons · Cynthia Erivo at a Wicked FYC panel in Santa Monica, California · Free licence — full author and licence details on the Commons file page (file photo)

LOS ANGELES — Cynthia Erivo has given her fullest response yet to the backlash that followed one of the most replayed moments of the Wicked: For Good press tour: the split second in Singapore when she stepped in front of Ariana Grande as a man rushed the red carpet. In an interview with The Guardian published on Wednesday, 28 October, Erivo said the criticism of her actions went too far — and described feeling as though her humanity had been, in her word, “bastardised” by the reaction.

The remarks, made as the sequel’s tour continues to dominate entertainment conversation in the United States, address two subjects that have followed the production for months: what actually happened on that red carpet, and the intense public scrutiny of the friendship between the film’s two stars. Erivo’s answer to both was measured and unmistakably firm: a protective instinct is not a performance, and a friendship is not a puzzle for strangers to solve.

WHAT HAPPENED IN SINGAPORE

At the Singapore premiere of Wicked: For Good, a man rushed the red carpet and grabbed Grande’s arm as he tried to get a photograph with her. Erivo, who was standing alongside her co-star, moved immediately to shield Grande, placing herself between Grande and the man. Security staff then intervened, the man was removed from the carpet, and he was subsequently arrested and charged.

The whole encounter lasted seconds. Its afterlife has lasted far longer. Footage filmed by fans at the premiere spread across social media within hours, and with it came two distinct conversations. The first concerned the man himself — how he reached the carpet, and what his actions said about the safety of public appearances. The second, which grew louder, concerned Erivo: why she had reacted as she did, what her reaction supposedly revealed, and what it all meant. It is that second conversation she is now addressing.

THE CRITICISM THAT WENT TOO FAR

Speaking to The Guardian, Erivo said the criticism went too far. Her objection, as she framed it, was not to being discussed — a press tour of this scale makes discussion unavoidable — but to the terms of it: a split-second act of protection, offered to a colleague in a frightening moment, was treated less as a human reaction than as material to be decoded. Her description of feeling that her humanity had been “bastardised” is a striking phrase, and a deliberate one. It captures the experience of watching an instinctive act be taken apart, frame by frame, until the person inside it all but disappears.

PSYCHOLOGISTS FROM THEIR COUCHES

Erivo also turned to the wider scrutiny of her friendship with Grande, which has been one of the defining storylines of the Wicked era. She spoke about fans and commentators who act like psychologists from their couches — analysing the pair’s interviews, public appearances and interactions as though two women being friends were a behaviour to be explained, rather than a relationship to be taken at face value.

The two actors’ closeness has been visible throughout both press tours: joint interviews, ready mutual praise, and an ease with each other that audiences have generally read as genuine. For many fans, that bond is part of the films’ appeal. But visibility has brought a particular kind of attention with it — the sort that treats affection as evidence and emotion as a symptom, and that mistakes access for understanding. Erivo’s point, put plainly, is that a friendship between two women does not require a diagnosis.

THE DOUBLE-EDGED SWORD OF SHOWING EMOTION

That scrutiny connects to another theme of the interview: the double-edged sword of showing emotion. Erivo spoke about what it means to be a performer whose work is emotional display — and whose emotions, once displayed off screen, become public property. Feeling things openly invites connection; it also invites commentary, judgment, and the quiet suspicion that nothing felt in public can simply be felt.

There is a particular irony in that dynamic for the stars of Wicked. The story they have spent years telling is, at its heart, about two women whose bond survives a world determined to misunderstand both of them. Audiences have embraced that story in enormous numbers. Erivo’s remarks suggest that living its real-world counterpart — a close friendship conducted under constant observation — has proved more complicated than the fairytale framing allows.

ELPHABA AND GLINDA, ON SCREEN AND OFF

Wicked, released in 2024, starred Erivo as Elphaba opposite Grande as Glinda, bringing the stage phenomenon to the screen in the first of two films. Both actors returned for Wicked: For Good, the concluding chapter, whose premiere tour has carried them — and the conversation around them — around the world. Casting two performers whose real rapport mirrors their characters’ bond was widely seen as one of the production’s great strengths. It is also, unavoidably, what made the friendship itself part of the story.

WHAT TO WATCH

Analysis — the Singapore moment sits at the meeting point of several forces reshaping modern stardom: fan access that can tip into intrusion, red carpets staged as global broadcasts, and social platforms that convert seconds of footage into weeks of interpretation. A man grabbing a star’s arm on a red carpet is, first and foremost, a security failure. That the conversation so quickly became about the woman who stepped in front of it says as much about the current culture of celebrity as it does about the incident itself.

Erivo’s interview will not end that conversation, and she does not appear to expect it to. What it does is return her own account to the centre of a story that had largely been told about her — in clips, in commentary, and in couch-side psychology. Her version is simpler than most of the theories: a friend was in harm’s way, and she moved. As Wicked: For Good continues its run and the tour rolls on, that may be the version worth holding on to.
