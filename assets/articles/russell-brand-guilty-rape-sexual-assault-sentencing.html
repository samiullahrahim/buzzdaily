Russell Brand Found Guilty of Rape and Sexual Assault — Sentencing on Monday

BuzzDaily Staff · UK Desk
· 8 November 2026 · 6 min read
Photo: appolinar · CC BY-SA 2.0 · via Wikimedia Commons · Russell Brand performing stand-up at the Roxy, Los Angeles, photographed in 2008 · Free licence — full author and licence details on the Commons file page (file photo)

LONDON — Russell Brand, once one of Britain’s most famous comedians, has been found guilty of rape and sexual assault. A jury at Southwark Crown Court in London convicted Brand, 51, on Thursday 5 November 2026 of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault against four women, for offences committed between 2004 and 2009. He was acquitted of two further rape charges and one count of attempted indecent assault. The trial judge, Mr Justice Bennathan, refused him bail, remanded him in custody and warned him that he faces a “significant” prison sentence when he returns to court for sentencing on Monday 9 November.

THE VERDICTS

The verdicts came at the end of a four-week trial that began on 12 October 2026. The jury deliberated for about eight and a half hours before delivering its decisions on the seven counts Brand faced. On four of those counts — one of rape and three of sexual assault, involving four women — the jury found him guilty. On the remaining three — two further charges of rape and one count of attempted indecent assault — he was cleared. The offences of which he was convicted span five years, from 2004 to 2009, a period during which Brand moved from the stand-up circuit into mainstream television, radio and film. In line with the reporting restrictions that govern cases of this kind, this report sets out only the charges, the verdicts and the outcome published by the court, and includes no graphic detail of the allegations themselves.

SENTENCING ON MONDAY

Brand will learn his punishment on Monday 9 November 2026, when he returns to Southwark Crown Court to be sentenced by Mr Justice Bennathan. Refusing bail after the verdicts, the judge remanded Brand in custody and warned him that he faces a “significant” prison sentence — a clear signal of the gravity with which the court views the convictions. He will remain in custody until the sentencing hearing. Sentencing will determine the length of the prison terms attached to the rape conviction and the three sexual-assault convictions, and how those terms will run. It will also bring to a close, at least in the crown court, a legal process that has moved from media investigation to police inquiry to full criminal trial over the course of three years.

HOW THE CASE BEGAN

The road to Southwark Crown Court began in September 2023, when allegations about Brand were reported in a joint investigation by Channel 4’s Dispatches programme and The Sunday Times. The Metropolitan Police investigated, charges followed, and the prosecution was brought by the Crown Prosecution Service, which published its response to the verdicts on 5 November 2026. Nor does the story necessarily end with Monday’s sentence: the Metropolitan Police has said it is investigating further allegations made by two more women, separate from the complainants in this trial. Those investigations are continuing, and while they do, nothing further can responsibly be reported about them. Under British law, complainants in sexual-offence cases are entitled to lifelong anonymity; none of the women in this case is named in this report, and none can legally be identified.

Reporting of the case has been shaped throughout by the strict rules that apply to sexual-offence trials in England and Wales. Those rules exist to protect complainants and to keep proceedings fair, and they are why coverage of a verdict such as this one confines itself to what the court itself has established: the counts, the deliberations, the verdicts and the sentence to come. Within those limits, the outcome at Southwark is unambiguous — a jury of Brand’s peers heard four weeks of evidence and convicted him on four counts.

A VERY PUBLIC CAREER

For two decades, Brand was one of the most recognisable figures in British entertainment. He emerged from stand-up comedy to front television and radio programmes in Britain, then crossed the Atlantic to star in Hollywood comedies including “Forgetting Sarah Marshall” and “Get Him to the Greek”. British comedy of that era produced a generation of outsized personalities — the world BuzzDaily recently visited in its coverage of Sacha Baron Cohen’s return to period comedy — but few matched Brand’s ubiquity across stand-up, broadcasting, film and bestselling books. In the decade that followed his mainstream peak, he rebuilt himself as an online commentator with a large and loyal following, largely outside the media that had made him famous. The entertainment landscape he left behind keeps moving — Danny Dyer’s turn as host of the UK’s Saturday Night Live is among the latest chapters — but this week’s verdicts return Brand himself to the centre of mainstream attention in the starkest possible way: as a convicted offender awaiting a prison sentence.

WHAT HAPPENS NEXT

Attention now turns to Monday’s sentencing hearing, the final scheduled act of the trial process. The judge’s warning of a “significant” sentence, delivered as bail was refused, frames what is expected to be a substantial term of imprisonment. Whatever its length, the sentence will be the definitive legal conclusion of the offences tried at Southwark — while the Metropolitan Police’s continuing investigation into the further allegations proceeds on its own separate track. The verdicts have already redrawn Brand’s public standing. The broadcaster and film star of the 2000s, and the online commentator of the 2020s, is now, in the eyes of the law, a man convicted of rape and sexual assault against four women. BuzzDaily will report the sentence in full when it is handed down on Monday.
