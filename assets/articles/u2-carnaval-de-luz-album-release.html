U2 Release 'Carnaval de Luz' Today: First New Album in Nine Years Closes With Dolly Parton's Final Recorded Vocal

BuzzDaily Staff · Entertainment Desk
· 13 November 2026 · 8 min read
Photo: Phillip Lebowitz / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0) · Bono performing live with U2 at Sphere, Las Vegas (file photo) · Free licence — full author and licence details on the Commons file page

Nine years is a long silence for the biggest rock band of their generation. It ends today. "Carnaval de Luz," U2's 16th studio album, is released this Friday, 13 November 2026, on Island Records — their first album of new material since "Songs of Experience" in 2017, and a record that closes with a piece of music history: "Torn," a duet between Bono and Dolly Parton that the band says is Parton's final recorded vocal performance.

FIRST NEW ALBUM IN NINE YEARS

The album runs to twelve tracks, produced by Jacknife Lee, and is built around four themes the band have circled for five decades: spirituality, friendship, freedom and endurance. The gap since 2017 is the longest between U2 studio albums in their career, which makes the framing of this one matter. Rather than a comeback record chasing the charts, "Carnaval de Luz" has been presented — in the band's official announcement on u2.com and in the coverage around it — as a summation: an album made by a band marking its 50th year, looking at what has held it together. Lee's production, and the choice of collaborators, point the same way.

DOLLY PARTON'S FINAL VOCAL

The closing track is the headline. "Torn" pairs Bono with Dolly Parton, and was co-written with Johnny McDaid. The band says it is Parton's final recorded vocal performance — a claim that, if it holds, gives the album's last song a weight no marketing campaign could manufacture. Parton's voice closing a U2 record is also a genuine musical crossing: country music's most beloved figure, on the final track of a rock album about endurance, recorded in the band's 50th year. It is the kind of ending that reframes everything before it, and the band have placed it exactly where endings go — last.

A GUEST LIST ACROSS GENERATIONS

Parton is the finale, not the only guest. Rosalía features on "The Mirror Maker" — a collaboration that lands on the same morning she wakes up as the Latin Grammys' five-award winner, as BuzzDaily reports in our Latin Grammys report. Brian Eno, the band's defining early producer, co-wrote and played synthesizers on three tracks — "Go (Carnaval De Luz)," "The Greatest Show on Earth" and "Can't Stop Us Now" — a reunion with the man who helped build the sound of "The Unforgettable Fire" and "The Joshua Tree." Martin Garrix co-wrote "Glorify," and Ryan Tedder added production to "Silencio." The credits read like a map of where U2 have been and who has followed them: Eno from the beginning, Tedder and Garrix from pop's present, Rosalía and Parton from entirely different traditions.

FROM TWO SURPRISE EPS TO 24 SONGS

"Carnaval de Luz" did not arrive cold. It grew out of two surprise EPs released earlier in 2026 — "Days of Ash," released on Ash Wednesday, and "Easter Lily," released on Good Friday — a liturgical release pattern that matches the album's spiritual thread. With the album's twelve tracks added, U2 will have released 24 new songs in 2026 alone, a work rate that makes the nine-year gap look less like a silence and more like a dam breaking. The singles have trailed the album in sequence: "Street of Dreams," "Silencio" — whose video was directed by Cliqua and filmed in Mexico City — and "The Greatest Show on Earth," the track that shares its name with the band's next world tour.

FIFTY YEARS ON

The anniversary framing is real. The album arrives in the band's 50th year, half a century since Larry Mullen Jr. pinned the notice on a Mount Temple Comprehensive School board in Dublin that brought the band together. Fifty years, sixteen studio albums, and a line-up that has never changed: whatever "Carnaval de Luz" is judged to be as a record, its context is unmatched in rock. Bands do not usually get to release a summation album at 50 years with all four original members still in the room — and with Dolly Parton's final vocal, Rosalía and Brian Eno woven through it.

RELEASE DAY: FORMATS AND SIRIUSXM

The album is available on standard CD and vinyl, digitally, and in limited-edition deluxe vinyl and CD boxsets. SiriusXM marks the release today with an exclusive Front Row interview and an intimate live performance, airing Friday 13 November at 6pm ET on U2 X-Radio (Channel 32) — the band's own channel carrying the release-day conversation and performance for North American listeners.

THE TOUR ALREADY AHEAD

The album does not land alone. The "Greatest Show on Earth" world tour for 2027 was already announced, opening in Mexico City, São Paulo and Buenos Aires, with a 12-date UK and European leg supported by Wolf Alice — BuzzDaily covered the tour announcement and presale in full on 2 November in our U2 2027 tour report. Today's release is the other half of that story: the songs those stadiums will be singing. Nine years after "Songs of Experience," U2 have a new album, a 50th anniversary, Parton's final vocal on its closing track and a world tour on sale. For a band in its sixth decade, that is not a quiet return.
