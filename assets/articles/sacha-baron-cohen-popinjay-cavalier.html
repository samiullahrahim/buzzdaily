Sacha Baron Cohen to Star in Quentin Tarantino's First-Ever Stage Play as West End Tickets Go On Sale

BuzzDaily Staff · Entertainment Desk
· 12 October 2026 · 6 min read
Illustration: Original artwork created for BuzzDaily

Quentin Tarantino has spent more than three decades redefining American cinema — from the razor-edged crime of Reservoir Dogs to the sun-soaked revisionism of Once Upon a Time in Hollywood. Now, at 63, the two-time Oscar winner is stepping into uncharted territory: the live theatre stage. And for his theatrical debut, he has cast one of the most unpredictable comic performers alive — Sacha Baron Cohen — in the title role of his brand-new play, The Popinjay Cavalier.

Producers Sony Pictures Entertainment and Sonia Friedman Productions announced the engagement on Sunday, October 11, with full details confirmed as tickets went on sale today, October 12. The play will have its world premiere at London's Phoenix Theatre, with previews beginning February 23, 2027 and an official opening night on March 18, 2027. The production is currently booking through June 13, 2027.

For Cohen, the role marks a major career milestone: his West End debut. The British actor and comedian — best known to global audiences for his shape-shifting comic creations Ali G, Borat, Bruno, and Admiral General Aladeen — trained under the legendary French clown teacher Philippe Gaulier early in his career, so the stage is not entirely foreign ground for him. But a West End leading role, in a play written and directed by Tarantino, is something else entirely.

The casting carries a touch of Hollywood symmetry. More than a decade ago, Cohen was attached to appear in Tarantino's Django Unchained before scheduling conflicts forced him to withdraw. The two have circled each other ever since — and The Popinjay Cavalier finally brings them together.

So what exactly is The Popinjay Cavalier? Details are being guarded closely, but producers describe it as a “rambunctious comedy of deception and disguise inspired by the grand swashbuckling epics of stage and screen.” The play is set in 1830s Europe — a world of duels at dawn, mistaken identities, and sweeping romance — and draws on Tarantino's lifelong love of classic Hollywood swashbucklers, with influences including the writing of George Bruce, Ernst Vajda, and Jesse Lasky Jr.

Tarantino, who is both writing and directing the production, said he is “keen with anticipation to give the West End a great night out.” Cohen, in his own inimitable fashion, joked that he agreed to the role the moment Tarantino called: “When the best director in the world calls, you don't ask questions.” He added that he is “beyond delighted” the two will be making their theatrical debuts together.

The announcement caps a long road. Tarantino first revealed he was developing a stage project in August 2024, later telling the Church of Tarantino podcast that a theatre production was in his future. He began writing the play in January 2025 and confirmed that August that he had finished the script and was moving to London to set the production in motion. The development announcement followed in March 2026, when Sonia Friedman Productions and Sony Pictures Entertainment formally boarded the project — at that stage with no title, cast, or dates attached.

By this September, reports had locked in both the title and Cohen's casting, with speculation mounting that the Phoenix Theatre would host the premiere once its current tenant, Stranger Things: The First Shadow, closes in December 2026. Today's confirmation makes it official.

The creative team behind the production underscores its ambition. Tom Pye handles scenery and costume design, Natasha Chivers lights the stage, Ian Dickinson designs the sound, and Tim Phillips composes the music. Philip d'Orleans serves as fight director with Sam Lyon-Behan as action coordinator — a fitting pairing for a swashbuckling comedy — while Jim Carnahan leads casting and James Paterson has painted the production artwork. Further casting announcements are expected in the coming months.

Tickets went on sale today with priority access at 9 AM BST for those who registered at PopinjayPlay.com, followed by general sale at noon BST. Prices start at 25 pounds, with producers making more than 16,000 tickets available for under 50 pounds across the run — a deliberate effort to keep the production accessible beyond the inevitable celebrity-opening frenzy.

The play also lands at a fascinating moment in Tarantino's career. The director has long spoken of retiring from filmmaking after his tenth feature, and he has reportedly said that if The Popinjay Cavalier lands well with audiences, he could adapt it into that tenth and final film. His latest screen project, The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth — a Netflix spinoff of Once Upon a Time in Hollywood, which he wrote and produced — is set to hit the streamer this December.

For London's theatre scene, the arrival is a coup. The West End has hosted countless Hollywood stars in recent years, but a brand-new play written and directed by one of cinema's most celebrated auteurs, starring one of Britain's most celebrated comic actors, is a genuine event. And for American audiences — where Tarantino's fanbase runs deep and Cohen's Borat became a cultural phenomenon — the London premiere is likely to become a pilgrimage destination, with talk already swirling of a possible future Broadway transfer.

For now, though, all roads lead to the Phoenix Theatre next March. The curtain is about to rise on a theatrical debut nobody saw coming — and everybody will be watching.
