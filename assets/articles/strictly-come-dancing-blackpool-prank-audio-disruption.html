Strictly Come Dancing Blackpool Show Hit by Prank Audio Disruption as Police Seize Phone

BuzzDaily Staff · UK Desk
· 22 November 2026 · 8 min read
Photo: Michael D Beckwith / CC BY 2.0 via Wikimedia Commons · The Blackpool Tower Ballroom, home of Strictly's Blackpool special (file photo)

BLACKPOOL — Strictly Come Dancing's live Blackpool special was disrupted on Saturday, 21 November 2026, when explicit noises were heard over the broadcast during the judges' feedback — an incident the BBC is investigating, the show's presenter apologised for on air, and police are now looking into after a mobile phone was seized. The sounds were heard as judge Motsi Mabuse gave her comments to the first couple of the night, singer Delta Goodrem and her professional partner Kai Widdrington, in the Blackpool Tower Ballroom.

“IT'S NOT ME”

Mabuse stopped mid-feedback as the noises played, telling the room: “It's not me.” The judging panel looked visibly confused, and the moment spread quickly online as viewers at home clipped and shared it. The disruption was brief, and the live show continued from the Tower Ballroom — Strictly's traditional mid-series trip to Blackpool, the spiritual home of British ballroom dancing, and one of the biggest and most scrutinised nights of the entire run.

THE PRANKSTER WHO CLAIMED IT

Responsibility was claimed, during the show itself, by the online prankster Daniel Jarvis, known as Jarvo69, who was livestreaming as the programme aired. Jarvis said the prank had been aimed at judge Craig Revel Horwood, telling his viewers: “This is for all the people you keep bullying. Don't bully people.” He appeared to place a phone call to trigger the noise — but complained that a delay meant it played during Mabuse's feedback instead, saying: “Sorry, I was trying to get the other one.” He then tried to ring again, but the second call did not go through.

AN ON-AIR APOLOGY, AND A BBC INVESTIGATION

Before the leaderboard was revealed, presenter Emma Willis apologised to viewers, saying: “we'd like to apologise if you heard any audio disruptions earlier in tonight's show and for any offence caused”. A BBC spokesperson also apologised, and said the corporation is investigating how the incident happened. The version of the programme placed on BBC iPlayer will be an edited one, without the incident — so the disruption exists, officially, only in the live broadcast and in the clips viewers made of it.

POLICE SEIZE A PHONE

Lancashire Police said a formal complaint had been made about the disruption, that a mobile phone has been seized, and that officers are looking into whether any criminal offences took place. The involvement of the police moves the story beyond a broadcasting embarrassment: what was presented online as a prank is now the subject of a formal complaint and an active police inquiry, running alongside the BBC's own investigation into how the audio reached the broadcast at all.

THE SHOW WENT ON

Inside the ballroom, the competition itself continued regardless. The night ended with a three-way tie for first place on the leaderboard — a result that, on any other Saturday, would have been the story of the evening. The Blackpool special is the centrepiece of the Strictly calendar, the week the series leaves its London studio for the Tower Ballroom's sprung floor and painted ceiling, and the couples had performed the full programme before a live audience. Dance-competition television has had an eventful season on both sides of the Atlantic: in the United States, Dancing With the Stars reached its season 35 semifinals this week, and its Super Bowl night special drew headlines of its own — though none quite like Blackpool's.

A FAMILIAR PRANK

Jarvis has claimed responsibility for a similar disruption before: the sex-noises prank that interrupted the BBC's Match of the Day coverage in 2023, which also made headlines and drew an apology. Saturday's incident revives every question that followed that stunt — about the security of live broadcasts, about how easily a phone call can find its way to air, and about where the line sits between a prank and an offence. That last question is now, formally, one for Lancashire Police.

For the BBC, the immediate tasks are the investigation and the edit: establishing how the audio got into a live flagship broadcast, and publishing the cleaned-up programme on iPlayer. For Strictly, the series moves on to its results and the run towards the final. But this year's Blackpool — the glitteriest night of the Strictly year — will be remembered, unavoidably, for the few seconds nobody in the Tower Ballroom could explain, and a judge obliged to tell the nation: it's not me.
