Prince William ‘Very Sorry’ as the Diana Award Charity Goes Into Administration After 27 Years

BuzzDaily Staff · Australia Desk
· 27 November 2026 · 6 min read
Photo: Number 10 / CC BY 2.0 via Wikimedia Commons · Prince William in London, January 2025 (file photo)

LONDON — The Diana Award, the charity founded in memory of Diana, Princess of Wales, has been placed into administration after 27 years, prompting a personal response from Prince William, its joint patron. The charity announced the news on Thursday, 26 November 2026, ending a run that began in 1999 and saw more than 50,000 young people honoured for social action and humanitarian work. William, 44, thanked everyone whose ‘dedication and hard work’ had made the charity’s work possible, in a statement signed simply ‘W’.

THE ANNOUNCEMENT

In its statement, the charity said: “It is with heavy hearts that we announce The Diana Award charity has been placed into administration, after independent advice made clear that the charity can no longer continue to meet its obligations.” The directors have appointed Nick Parsk of Oury Clark Chartered Accountants as Joint Administrator. Administration is the formal process under which an organisation that can no longer meet its obligations is placed in the hands of administrators — in this case, bringing to an end more than a quarter of a century of work carried out in Diana’s name, and leaving the charity’s future in the administrators’ hands.

The statement gave no indication of a rescue, a merger or a successor organisation. The single confirmed next step is the appointment itself: Nick Parsk of Oury Clark Chartered Accountants, as Joint Administrator, now oversees the charity’s affairs. For an organisation whose work was measured in ceremonies, school programmes and mentoring relationships, it is an abrupt and bureaucratic ending — delivered, as these endings usually are, in the language of obligations and independent advice.

WILLIAM’S RESPONSE

Prince William responded with a personal statement signed ‘W’. “The Diana Award has recognised and encouraged thousands of young people who are changing the world for the better,” he said. “I want to thank everyone whose dedication and hard work helped make that possible over so many years.” Reporting of the announcement described the Prince as ‘very sorry’ at the news. William is joint patron of the charity alongside his brother, Prince Harry, although neither brother holds an official position with it. As of the reporting on 27 November, Harry had not yet commented.

The statement’s brevity was itself notable. Signed with the single initial William uses for personal messages, it did not address the reasons for the collapse, the administrators’ next steps or the future of the charity’s programmes. It thanked, and it recognised — and it left every practical question about the Diana Award’s ending to be answered by others.

A CHARITY BORN OF DIANA’S LEGACY

The Diana Award was founded in 1999 and launched by the then Prime Minister, Gordon Brown — two years after Diana died in a car crash in Paris on 31 August 1997. It was built on the belief that young people could change the world, a belief William’s statement echoed almost word for word in praising young people ‘changing the world for the better’. Over 27 years the charity honoured more than 50,000 of them for social action and humanitarian work. Alongside its awards, it ran school anti-bullying initiatives and mentoring programmes, taking Diana’s name into classrooms as well as ceremonies. Reporting has noted that it was the last active charity carrying Diana’s name that both William and Harry still jointly supported.

The work itself was practical as much as ceremonial. The anti-bullying initiatives took the charity into schools; the mentoring programmes paired young people with support over time; and the awards themselves were designed to find young people whose contribution might otherwise have gone unrecognised — the social action and humanitarian work the charity spent 27 years celebrating. More than 50,000 recipients across that period is the statistic that will stand as its legacy: an average of well over a thousand young people honoured every year of its existence.

THE MOST RECENT HONOURS

The charity’s work continued almost to the end. Its most recent Roll of Honour, at a virtual ceremony on 4 November 2026, recognised 198 young people from 44 countries — with William and Harry each sending congratulatory letters to the recipients. Past recipients of the award include Sophie Pender, the founder of The 93% Club, and the sprinter Asafa Powell — a measure of the range of young people, from community organisers to athletes, that the award sought out over its 27 years. That the final Roll of Honour was completed just three weeks before the administration announcement underlines how abruptly the ending came.

AN ENDING FELT AROUND THE WORLD

The response to the news was immediate and international. ‘Prince william news’ was the number one entertainment search in Australia, up more than 1,000 per cent; ‘william, prince of wales’ topped the chart in New Zealand; ‘diana award’ reached number six in the United States; and ‘diana, princess of wales’ appeared at number twelve in Canada. The spread of that interest — across the countries where Diana’s memory remains strongest — underlined what the charity represented. For 27 years, the Diana Award turned remembrance into recognition, attaching Diana’s name not to ceremony but to young people doing the work. Its administration closes that chapter, and leaves the question of what, if anything, replaces it unanswered.
