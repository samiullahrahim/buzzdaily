Netflix Drops First Trailer for 'The Murder of JonBenét Ramsey' as Boulder Police Hold Rare DNA Briefing

BuzzDaily Staff · UK Desk
· 25 November 2026 · 5 min read
Photo: Wikimedia Commons · Boulder County Courthouse, Boulder, Colorado (file photo)

LONDON — Netflix has released the first trailer for The Murder of JonBenét Ramsey, its dramatisation of one of the most scrutinised unsolved cases in American criminal history, and confirmed that the limited series will premiere globally on 10 December 2026. The trailer arrived on 24 November, in the same week that Boulder’s police chief and district attorney held a rare joint press conference about DNA evidence in the case that is still waiting to be tested. Between the trailer and the briefing, a tragedy that is about to mark its thirtieth anniversary has returned to the centre of the entertainment and news agenda on both sides of the Atlantic.

MCCARTHY AND OWEN AS PATSY AND JOHN RAMSEY

The series casts Melissa McCarthy as Patsy Ramsey and Clive Owen as John Ramsey, the parents at the centre of the case, with Emily Mitchell playing JonBenét and Angus Caldwell playing her brother, Burke. It is written and executive-produced by Richard LaGravenese, who also serves as showrunner, and directed by Anne Sewitsky. Netflix describes the project as a dramatisation of the case and its aftermath rather than a whodunit: the series is concerned less with proposing a solution than with retelling what happened to the Ramsey family and how the investigation, the media coverage and public suspicion unfolded around them over the years that followed. It is being released as a limited series. Reports have differed on the number of episodes, and BuzzDaily is not treating any single episode count as confirmed.

SHELVED BY A MERGER, RESCUED BY NETFLIX

The production has taken an unusually long road to the screen. The series was originally greenlit by Paramount+ in 2024 and filmed in Calgary, Canada, between September 2024 and January 2025. After the Paramount–Skydance merger, however, the completed project was shelved, and its future was in doubt. Netflix bought the global rights on 1 July 2026, rescuing the series and setting it up as one of the streamer’s major December releases. The journey explains the timing of this week’s trailer: a production that wrapped filming almost two years ago is only now meeting its audience, arriving in the same month that marks thirty years since the events it dramatises.

THE DNA THAT IS STILL WAITING TO BE TESTED

The entertainment story is running in parallel with a real-world development in Boulder. This week, Boulder Police Chief Stephen Redfearn and District Attorney Michael Dougherty held a rare joint press conference about DNA evidence in the case that is still waiting to be tested. At the centre of that discussion is the unidentified DNA profile known as “Unknown Male 1”, recovered during the original investigation and never matched to a named person. The briefing was a reminder that, three decades on, the case remains an open investigation rather than a closed chapter of television history — and that decisions about testing evidence rest with investigators and prosecutors in Colorado, not with the dramas and documentaries the case continues to generate.

THIRTY YEARS SINCE DECEMBER 1996

JonBenét Ramsey was six years old when she was found dead at her family’s home in Boulder, Colorado, on 26 December 1996. The case became one of the most intensely covered criminal investigations of its era, and it has never been solved. December 2026 therefore marks thirty years since her death — an anniversary that goes some way to explaining why a shelved dramatisation, a network documentary special and a police press conference about DNA have all converged within a few weeks of each other. BuzzDaily’s coverage of the case is deliberately factual in tone: the facts of the investigation are a matter of public record, but the death at the centre of it was that of a six-year-old child, and the family at the centre of the new series have lived with its consequences for three decades.

WHY THE CASE IS EVERYWHERE AGAIN

The Netflix series is not the only screen treatment drawing audiences back to Boulder this winter. On 6 November 2026, ABC’s 20/20 aired a Diane Sawyer special, “Race Against Time, Who Is ‘Unknown Male 1’?”, devoted to the same unidentified DNA profile discussed at this week’s Boulder briefing. And Netflix itself has recent evidence of the audience for the subject: its 2024 docuseries Cold Case: Who Killed JonBenét Ramsey? drew 13.4 million views in its first week on the platform. The dramatisation now arriving on 10 December is a different proposition from that documentary — scripted, cast with two of the most recognisable screen actors of their generation, and focused on the family’s experience — but it lands in a landscape the documentary helped create, in which the Ramsey case is once again one of the most searched and discussed true-crime subjects in the world.
