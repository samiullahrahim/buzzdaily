‘The Hunting Wives’ Season 2 Is Out Now on Netflix — John Stamos Joins as Five Stars Leave Maple Brook

BuzzDaily Staff · Entertainment Desk
· 27 November 2026 · 6 min read
Photo: The Heart Truth / Public domain via Wikimedia Commons · Brittany Snow at the Red Dress Collection show, New York Fashion Week, 2009 (file photo)

LOS ANGELES — Maple Brook is open again. Season 2 of ‘The Hunting Wives’ premiered on Netflix on Thursday, 26 November 2026 — Thanksgiving Day in the United States — with all eight episodes released at once. The new season also marks a promotion for the series itself: what began as a sleeper hit is now officially a Netflix Original, available in every country where Netflix operates. It arrives with a reshaped cast — John Stamos leads a list of high-profile newcomers, while five familiar faces from season 1 do not return.

FROM SLEEPER HIT TO NETFLIX ORIGINAL

The show’s rise has been quick. Based on May Cobb’s 2021 novel, ‘The Hunting Wives’ landed on Netflix in July 2025 and became a sleeper hit, the kind of word-of-mouth success that builds week by week rather than arriving with a marketing campaign. Netflix renewed it for season 2 in September 2025. Season 2 moves the story beyond the book, and the streamer’s confidence in the series is already on record: season 3 was renewed as well, announced in early October 2026, before season 2 had even premiered. Few streaming dramas get a third season confirmed before their second has aired.

THE OFFICIAL SYNOPSIS

Netflix’s official synopsis for the new season sets up a reunion under pressure: “At the start of season 2, Sophie and Margo are on the outs. But soon enough, old secrets and new foes force them back together. As they play their dangerous games, the question arises. Are they the hunters or the hunted?” It is a premise that returns the show to its central relationship — Sophie O’Neil and Margo Banks, the friendship whose secrets powered season 1 — while surrounding it with new enemies and new temptations.

It is also a premise with a body count behind it. Season 1 ended with the revelation that Margo killed Abby Jackson — the secret that now sits underneath every scene the two women share. Season 2 begins with Sophie and Margo estranged, and the official synopsis promises that old secrets, as much as new enemies, are what drag them back into each other’s orbit. The question the synopsis poses — hunters or hunted — is really a question about which of them the past catches up with first.

WHO IS BACK IN MAPLE BROOK

Brittany Snow returns as Sophie O’Neil and Malin Åkerman as Margo Banks. Dermot Mulroney is back as Jed Banks, who is now running for governor — a storyline that raises the public stakes around the Banks family considerably. Also returning are Evan Jonigkeit, George Ferrier, Hunter Emery and Branton Box. Karen Rodriguez, who plays Deputy Wanda Salazar, has been promoted to series regular for season 2, a sign that the law in Maple Brook will have a larger part to play this time around.

THE NEW FACES

The headline arrival is John Stamos, who joins as Chase Brylan, a fast-talking country music king and producer. Dale Dickey plays Zelda Moffitt, leader of a biker gang from Margo’s hometown of Alba, Texas, with Cam Gigandet as Gentle John Moffitt. Kim Matula plays Nadia Kelly, a magnetic Hollywood producer and newcomer to Maple Brook; Alex FitzAlan plays Lincoln Trout, Jed’s conniving Conservative campaign advisor; and Casey Mills plays Nico. There is also a notable acting debut: WNBA star Angel Reese appears as Trainer Barbie.

The casting mixes generations of television fame. Stamos brings one of the most recognisable faces in American TV to Maple Brook, while Reese’s appearance as Trainer Barbie marks the WNBA star’s acting debut — the kind of crossover casting that signals how large the show’s audience has become since its sleeper-hit first season. Between a country music king, a biker gang from Alba and a Hollywood producer new in town, season 2 is clearly widening Maple Brook’s world well beyond the hunting set.

FIVE WHO ARE NOT RETURNING

Not everyone survived the journey from season 1 — in one case, literally. Madison Wolfe does not return as Abby Jackson, who was murdered in season 1, with Margo revealed as her killer. Also absent are Chrissy Metz, who played Starr; Katie Lowes, who played Jill; Pastor Pete; and Michael Aaron Milligan, who played Kyle, Margo’s brother. The departures clear space for the new arrivals, but they also underline how much of season 2 is built around the consequences of season 1’s ending — and around what Margo did.

A GOVERNOR’S RACE IN MAPLE BROOK

One storyline lifts the show beyond its cul-de-sac setting. Jed Banks is running for governor, and the arrival of Lincoln Trout — described as Jed’s conniving Conservative campaign advisor — puts a political operation inside the Banks household at exactly the moment its secrets are most dangerous. A campaign means scrutiny, and scrutiny is the one thing Maple Brook’s social set has spent two seasons avoiding. It gives season 2 a public stage on which its private games can go wrong.

THE EPISODES, AND WHEN TO WATCH

All eight episodes dropped at once — at 12am Pacific, 3am Eastern and 8am in the UK — in Netflix’s usual all-at-once pattern, ready for Thanksgiving-weekend viewing in the United States. The episode titles, listed alphabetically because the chronological order has not yet been confirmed, are: Back in the Saddle, Dead Ringer, Freak on a Leash, Good Luck, Babe!, Last Dance, Riders on the Storm, Scared Straight and Sonny Came Home. Interest has been international from the first day: ‘the hunting wives’ ranked third among entertainment searches in the UK, fourth in Canada and fourth in Australia, while ‘hunting wives cast’ reached number 12 in the United States, up 400 per cent — a measure of how many returning viewers went straight to checking who was in, and who was out.
