Rosalía Makes History at the 2026 Latin Grammys: Third Album of the Year Win for 'LUX' Caps a Five-Award Night

BuzzDaily Staff · Entertainment Desk
· 13 November 2026 · 8 min read
Photo: Secretaría de Cultura de la Ciudad de México / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY 2.0) · Rosalía performing live at the Zócalo, Mexico City (file photo) · Free licence — full author and licence details on the Commons file page

LAS VEGAS — By the time Ricky Martin opened the envelope for Album of the Year at the MGM Grand Garden Arena on Thursday night, the 27th Annual Latin Grammy Awards had already become Rosalía's evening. When he read out "LUX (Complete Works)," it became a historic one. The Spanish star's win made her the first woman ever to win Album of the Year three times as a lead artist — and, with five trophies in total, capped the defining night of the 2026 Latin music calendar.

A THIRD ALBUM OF THE YEAR, AND A PLACE IN HISTORY

The ceremony, held on Thursday 12 November 2026 in Las Vegas and hosted by Roselyn Sánchez, ended with the award that has tracked Rosalía's entire career arc. She first won Album of the Year for "El Mal Querer" in 2019, then again for "Motomami" in 2022. Thursday's victory for "LUX (Complete Works)" puts her alongside Alejandro Sanz and Juanes as one of only three artists to win the category three times — and she is the first woman to do it as a lead artist. Three albums, three eras of her sound, three times the Latin Recording Academy's voters have decided that the year's defining record was hers.

"A LEAP INTO THE VOID"

Accepting the trophy from Ricky Martin, Rosalía spoke in Spanish, through tears, and reached for the image that has followed LUX since its release. "This is the third time I've won this award, and — just as 'El Mal Querer' felt like a dream, or 'Motomami' was a gamble — 'Lux' has been, for me, a leap into the void," she said. The line landed because it described the record accurately: where "Motomami" was a provocation, LUX was the risk of following it with something stranger, more theatrical and entirely unconcerned with repeating the formula that made her a global star.

FIVE AWARDS, ONE NIGHT

Album of the Year was the headline, but the tally told the fuller story. Rosalía won five awards in total. "La Perla," her collaboration with Yahritza y Su Esencia, took both Song of the Year and Best Pop Song — and when the Song of the Year win was announced, she invited the group up on stage with her, thanking Mexico in a moment that played as both gratitude and recognition of how central Mexican music has been to the song's life. She also won Best Alternative Album and Best Short Form Music Video, completing a sweep that stretched from the night's biggest general-field prizes to its craft and genre categories.

OPENING THE SHOW

Before any of the trophies, there was the performance. Rosalía opened the ceremony with a theatrical medley of tracks from LUX, staged with elaborate dancing and halo-like headpieces — an opening that set the visual language for the night and underlined the album's conceit before a single award had been handed out. Opening an awards show is a statement of where an artist sits in the industry's pecking order; closing it as Album of the Year winner is the confirmation. On Thursday, Rosalía did both, bookending the 27th Latin Grammys with the same record.

A WEEK THAT BECAME A LANDMARK

The timing sharpened the story. LUX was released on 7 November 2026 — less than a week before the ceremony — and last week Rosalía was inducted into Billboard's Latin Music Hall of Fame. Album, honour, then a five-award coronation inside seven days: few artists at any point in Latin music's history have compressed so much institutional recognition into a single week. And the run is not finished. As BuzzDaily reports today, she also appears on U2's new album "Carnaval de Luz," released this Friday, guesting on "The Mirror Maker" — a credit that underlines how far her reach now extends beyond the Latin categories she keeps winning.

THE REST OF THE NIGHT

Rosalía dominated, but she did not sweep everything. Record of the Year went to Milo J for "Niño," and the Argentine also collected Best Folk Album — a breakthrough night for one of the night's youngest headline winners. Daddy Yankee was named Person of the Year, the first Latin urbano artist to receive the honour, a recognition of the reggaeton pioneer's foundational role in the genre's global rise. Composer Edgar Barrera reached 30 career Latin Grammy wins, making him the most awarded person in the show's history. And Best New Artist went to Colombian singer Aria Vega, the night's clearest changing-of-the-guard moment.

WHAT THE NIGHT CONFIRMED

Awards shows rarely settle arguments, but Thursday's ceremony settled one: the story of Latin music in 2026 runs through LUX. A third Album of the Year, a Song of the Year shared with Yahritza y Su Esencia, an opening performance that doubled as a manifesto, and a Hall of Fame induction days earlier — Rosalía leaves Las Vegas not just as the night's biggest winner but as the first woman to hold the Latin Grammys' biggest album prize three times. The leap into the void, it turns out, landed exactly where she aimed it. The wider live-music calendar she now headlines will come into focus next spring — BuzzDaily has the full picture in our Coachella 2027 lineup report — but for now, the record of the year in Latin music is not in dispute.
