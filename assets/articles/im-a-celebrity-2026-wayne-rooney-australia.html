Wayne Rooney Lands in Australia for I’m A Celebrity 2026 as ITV Confirms 16 November Start

BuzzDaily Staff · UK Desk
· 9 November 2026 · 6 min read
Photo: Austin Osuide · CC BY 2.0 · via Wikimedia Commons · Wayne Rooney in his Manchester United playing days · Free licence — full author and licence details on the Commons file page (file photo)

MURWILLUMBAH — Wayne Rooney has landed in Australia. The former England and Manchester United captain, 41, was spotted arriving on Monday 9 November dressed down in a grey zip-up hoodie and cap — the first sighting of the headline signing for the new series of I’m A Celebrity… Get Me Out Of Here!, and the starting gun on three weeks of jungle television. ITV has now confirmed that series 26 begins on Monday 16 November at 9pm on ITV1 and ITVX, and in a scheduling twist with a distinctly footballing flavour, the launch has been pushed back 24 hours from the show’s usual Sunday slot so the camp does not clash with England v Spain in the UEFA Nations League.

THE ARRIVAL

Rooney’s low-key touchdown — hoodie, cap, no fanfare — is the traditional first image of every I’m A Celebrity season, the moment the rumour mill gives way to the real thing. This year’s carries more weight than most. Rooney is a genuine headline signing: one of the biggest names in modern English football, a player whose career at Everton, Manchester United and England made him a household name far beyond sport. But the family storyline is what gives his casting its charge. His wife Coleen was the runner-up in the 2024 series, winning over viewers across her own jungle run — and Rooney has said he wants to do better than her. Second place, in other words, is the family benchmark he has publicly set out to beat.

A START DATE WITH A FOOTBALLING REASON

The confirmed start date tells its own story about the two national obsessions the series sits between. I’m A Celebrity traditionally launches on a Sunday night, but ITV has moved series 26 back by 24 hours to Monday 16 November to avoid a head-on clash with England’s UEFA Nations League meeting with Spain — a fixture that would have split exactly the audience the launch episode needs. Once underway, the series settles into its familiar rhythm: nightly episodes across roughly three weeks, with the final expected in early December, when one campmate will be crowned King or Queen of the Jungle.

THE REPORTED LINE-UP

A note on accuracy matters here, because this is the point in the I’m A Celebrity cycle where reporting and fact are most easily blurred. ITV has confirmed the start date — and only the start date. The broadcaster has not officially confirmed any individual campmates. The twelve names in circulation come from The Sun’s reported line-up, and they should be read as exactly that: reported, not confirmed. Alongside Rooney, The Sun reports that the camp will include Gavin & Stacey star Joanna Page; presenter and athlete Ade Adepitan, who would be the first campmate to use a wheelchair, with the camp adapted for access; AJ Odudu; broadcaster Mariella Frostrup; Kaiser Chiefs frontman Ricky Wilson; Leigh Francis; Claire Sweeney; model Leomie Anderson; Chloe Burrows; and creators Sharky and Jack Skipper. If the reporting holds, it is a deliberately broad mix — soap and comedy favourites, a Paralympian broadcaster, a chart-topping frontman and a pair of social-media stars — but until ITV names its campmates, every name on that list carries an asterisk.

One name on that reported list carries a significance beyond casting. If Ade Adepitan does enter the camp, he will be the first campmate to use a wheelchair, and the production has reportedly adapted the camp for access — a genuine first for a format that has been running for more than two decades and has built its trials around physical endurance, heights, water and confinement. How a series famous for making its contestants uncomfortable adjusts its challenges so that every campmate faces them on equal terms will be one of the production stories of the series, and a test of whether the jungle can modernise its machinery without losing the ordeal that makes it compelling.

ANT AND DEC, AND THE UNPACKED TEAM

What is certain is the presenting team. Ant McPartlin and Dec Donnelly return to host, broadcasting live from Murwillumbah in Australia, where the camp sits in the rainforest of New South Wales. The companion show also returns: I’m A Celebrity… Unpacked will air on ITV2 and ITVX, hosted by Sam Thompson and Kemi Rodgers, picking over each night’s episode. Reality television has had a crowded autumn in Britain — BuzzDaily’s coverage of James Acaster’s self-unmasking on The Celebrity Traitors showed how completely these formats now dominate the conversation — and ITV’s own schedules have just crowned Cate McHugh as the winner of The Voice UK 2026. The jungle, though, remains the biggest beast of the season.

WHY ROONEY IS THE STORY

Every series has a signing who defines it before a single Bushtucker Trial has been eaten, and this year it is Rooney — for reasons that run in both directions. To football fans, he is a figure of genuine stature testing himself in the least controlled environment television offers. To the show’s wider audience, he arrives with a ready-made narrative: can he go one better than Coleen? That question will hang over every trial vote and every camp conversation until the public decides otherwise. The hoodie-and-cap arrival photos are, as ever, the calm before it. On 16 November, the calm ends.
