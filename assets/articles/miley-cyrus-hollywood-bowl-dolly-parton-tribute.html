Miley Cyrus' Hollywood Bowl Homecoming: A Tearful "Jolene" for Dolly Parton, Days After a Historic Chart Collapse

BuzzDaily Staff · Entertainment Desk
· 17 October 2026 · 7 min read
Illustration: Original artwork created for BuzzDaily

LOS ANGELES — There are comebacks, and then there is what Miley Cyrus did on Friday night. Headlining the first of two sold-out shows at the Hollywood Bowl — her first full concert set in four years — the singer turned a milestone homecoming into something far more personal: a tear-stained tribute to her late godmother, Dolly Parton, built around a raw, aching performance of "Jolene."

It was the kind of night that reminded everyone in the 17,500-seat amphitheater that chart numbers only ever tell half of an artist's story. And Cyrus, of all people, would know — her new album has just made the wrong kind of Billboard history.

A DREAM VENUE, DECADES IN THE MAKING

Hours before showtime, Cyrus told her followers exactly what the evening meant to her. "Opening night at the Hollywood Bowl is here," she wrote on Instagram alongside a black-and-white carousel showing the venue's marquee advertising BASS PERSUADES LIVE with a SOLD OUT message. "It's always been a dream of mine to play such an iconic venue. I've spent so many unforgettable nights watching the greats. Excited to make it my own."

She wasn't exaggerating. For years Cyrus sat in those seats as a fan — including, memorably, one night watching the very woman she would honor on Friday. The two shows, October 16 and 18, with experimental rock band Model/Actriz opening, are her first performances at the Bowl, and her first major concerts since 2022, according to SeatGeek. Tickets started at around $100, with Friday's show the easier get — demand for the run was so fierce that reports put nearly 1.5 million fans in the presale queue.

The Bowl dates were announced in early September alongside her tenth studio album, Bass Persuades, released September 18 through Atlantic Records — and they arrived with a personal rebrand, too. Cyrus quietly dropped her surname from her social media handles, going simply by Miley. Her father Billy Ray backed the change, telling press: "I always called my little girl Miley. Just… Miley."

THE TRIBUTE: "ME AND DOLLY, WE ARE ETERNAL"

Midway through Friday's set, the tone shifted. As the opening notes of Parton's 1973 signature song "Jolene" began, a photograph of Parton appeared on the screens behind her, and Cyrus — visibly emotional — sang it through tears. During "Wrecking Ball," one of Parton's favorite songs, she held up a heart-shaped frame containing a photo of her godmother.

Then she told the story. Cyrus said she had once asked her "Aunt Dolly" if she could attend one of Parton's own Hollywood Bowl concerts the way a normal fan would — from a seat, no backstage, no performing. Parton agreed. But it didn't go as planned: Parton opened the show with "The Best of Both Worlds," the theme from Cyrus' Disney Channel series Hannah Montana, and then, "like a proud aunt," told the entire stadium that her goddaughter was sitting right there in the audience. Nobody knew she was there, Cyrus recalled, "until she drew an obnoxious amount of attention to it."

The memory has clearly been weighing on her. Parton died in August following a battle with cancer, and Cyrus penned an emotional message remembering her that month. On Friday she looked out at 17,500 people and made the loss feel shared: "Sitting here with you all right now, feeling her all around me, knowing that she's right here beside me, every day, more than she could be when she was here; I feel her more now than ever."

"Knowing that I get to share this story with you all, it makes that night very worth it," she added. "This memory is gold to me. Me and Dolly, we are eternal."

It is worth remembering how deep that bond ran. Parton knew Cyrus before she was born, appeared on Hannah Montana to support her goddaughter, and the two later shared powerful duet performances after Cyrus became a star in her own right.

THE PARADOX: A NO. 1 DEBUT, THEN A RECORD FALL

Here is the twist that makes this weekend's triumph so striking. Bass Persuades — the album Friday's show was built around — made Billboard history earlier this month, just not the kind any artist hopes for.

After debuting at No. 1 on the Billboard 200 on September 28 with roughly 61,000 equivalent album units — her first US chart-topper since 2013's Bangerz and the sixth No. 1 of her career — the album plunged 156 places to No. 157 in its second week. That is the biggest second-week drop from No. 1 by a female artist in Billboard history, and among the steepest falls ever for any No. 1 debut. The only steeper declines on record belong to Bon Jovi's 2018 reissue, which fell from No. 1 to No. 169, and Travis Scott's decade-old mixtape Days Before Rodeo, which vanished off the chart entirely the following week. The previous female-artist record holders were Madonna's Madame X, which fell to No. 77 in 2019, and Celine Dion's Courage, which dropped to No. 111.

The debut itself was hard-won. Atlantic offered the digital album at a discount and issued a mountain of variants — 16 vinyl editions, nine CD versions, six download variants and a cassette — and Bass Persuades edged country singer Ella Langley's Dandelion for the top spot by roughly 70 units. Cyrus celebrated at the time, writing on Instagram that "Bass Persuades being #1 transcends a win," and thanking her fans: "To the fans — you make my world go round."

The title single, meanwhile, debuted at No. 35 on the back of a viral video — her first solo top-40 hit since "Used to Be Young" reached No. 8 in 2023. Critics were split: NME called the record "leaner, more direct and more obviously built for the dancefloor" in a three-star review, adding that "nearly two decades into her recording career, Cyrus has nothing to prove and knows it."

WHY THE BOWL MATTERS MORE THAN THE CHART

The contrast is the story. Streaming-era chart math punishes front-loaded debuts — variant-stuffed first weeks, then a cliff. But no algorithm manufactures 1.5 million people in a presale queue or two sold-out nights at one of America's most storied venues.

And Cyrus has been quietly rebuilding her live presence for years without a conventional tour. Since her last full-scale run, she headlined a Mexico show, staged intimate invite-only sets at the Chateau Marmont in Los Angeles, introduced her previous album Something Beautiful through a fan-focused TikTok event, performed a private Spotify set in Paris, and recorded for the Hannah Montana 20th anniversary special. Her 2024 Grammy wins for "Flowers" — record of the year and best pop solo performance, her first two Grammys — reset her critical standing. The Bowl is the moment she converts that capital back into a live audience.

A note for UK fans: the Dolly connection runs deep on this side of the Atlantic too. Cyrus headlined Glastonbury in 2019 to huge acclaim, and Parton's own 2014 Glastonbury set remains the stuff of legend — so a Hollywood Bowl "Jolene" sung through tears lands just as hard in Manchester as it does in Los Angeles.

WHAT TO WATCH ON NIGHT TWO

The engagement wraps on Sunday, October 18, and if night one is any indication, surprises are likely. Will "Jolene" stay in the set? Will the Dolly tributes continue? For an artist who has spent this era rewriting her own narrative — new label, new mononym, new sound — Friday proved the old story still carries the most power: a goddaughter, a godmother, and 17,500 people singing along through tears.
