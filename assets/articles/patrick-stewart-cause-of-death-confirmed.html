Patrick Stewart's Cause of Death Confirmed Three Weeks After His Death at 86

BuzzDaily Staff · Entertainment Desk
· 20 November 2026 · 8 min read
Photo: Maximilian Bühn / CC BY-SA 4.0 via Wikimedia Commons · Sir Patrick Stewart at the Logan photo call, Berlinale, February 2017

LOS ANGELES — Three weeks after his death, Sir Patrick Stewart's official cause of death has been confirmed. TMZ reported on Thursday, 19 November, that it had obtained the Star Trek and X-Men star's death certificate, which lists his causes of death as cardiopulmonary arrest and a neurocognitive disorder with Lewy body, following Alzheimer's disease and Lewy body dementia. Stewart died on Tuesday, 28 October, at his home in Los Angeles. He was 86.

To be clear about what is new here: Stewart's death itself is not today's news. BuzzDaily reported it on 30 October, a day after his longtime representative, Kelly Bush Novak, announced that he had died following a journey with Alzheimer's disease and Lewy body dementia that had never previously been publicly disclosed. What the death certificate adds, three weeks on, is the official medical confirmation — the causes are now a matter of public record, set down in a Los Angeles County document rather than described by his family.

In her statement of 29 October, Bush Novak said: “Patrick died yesterday at his home in Los Angeles, in the loving care of his beloved wife Sunny, following a brief and poignant journey with Alzheimer's Disease and Lewy Body Dementia. He has been held in the warm embrace of family and friends, surrounded by live music, prayer, tears, laughter, and the kind of profound emotion that only he could inspire after a lifetime of devotion to us all.”

WHAT THE CERTIFICATE CONFIRMS

Cardiopulmonary arrest — cardiac arrest — is the immediate cause recorded on the certificate, with the neurocognitive disorder with Lewy body listed alongside it, and Alzheimer's disease and Lewy body dementia as the underlying conditions behind both. The findings are consistent with everything the family shared last month. There is no suggestion of anything unexpected, and no detail that changes the account of a death at home, in his wife's care, among family and friends. The certificate also records that Stewart's remains underwent alkaline hydrolysis — water cremation — on 3 November, the flameless process that has become an increasingly chosen alternative to conventional cremation in California.

The public response to the confirmation has been striking in itself. On Google Trends over the past 24 hours, “patrick stewart death cause” was the number one entertainment search in the United States, with more than 50,000 searches and a rise of more than 1,000 per cent. It also topped the entertainment charts in the United Kingdom, Canada and New Zealand, and ranked second in Australia. Three weeks after his death, the world is still looking him up — a measure of how personally millions of people took the loss of an actor many of them felt they knew.

FROM MIRFIELD TO THE ROYAL SHAKESPEARE COMPANY

Patrick Stewart was born on 13 July 1940 in Mirfield, West Yorkshire, the son of a textile worker and a regimental sergeant major. He won a scholarship to the Bristol Old Vic Theatre School and, at 26, joined the Royal Shakespeare Company, spending much of the 1960s in the most demanding repertory in the English-speaking theatre. The diction, the stillness, and the sense that a line of dialogue was something to be weighed before it was spoken were all formed on those stages, years before television made his face famous around the world.

THE CAPTAIN AND THE PROFESSOR

Fame, when it came, came late and from an unlikely direction. Stewart was 47 when he was cast as Captain Jean-Luc Picard in Star Trek: The Next Generation (1987–94), and across seven seasons, four feature films and a return a generation later in Star Trek: Picard (2020–23), he turned a British theatre actor largely unknown to American audiences into a global figure. In parallel he became Professor Charles Xavier, the moral centre of the X-Men films from 2000 to 2017, and played Xavier again in Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness (2022). Few actors get one defining role. Stewart had two, in the two biggest science-fiction franchises of the modern era, and played both with the intelligence he first brought to Shakespeare.

Honours followed the work. Stewart was knighted in 2010, for services to drama — a formal recognition of a career that had never really left the stage behind, even at the height of his screen fame. To theatre audiences he remained, first and always, a company actor: the RSC training visible in every pause, and that voice — perhaps the most recognisable instrument in British acting — deployed as readily for kings and captains as for comedy and animation.

THE DOOMSDAY QUESTION

One question the certificate cannot answer is the one now hanging over Marvel Studios. Stewart had been announced among the returning X-Men cast for Avengers: Doomsday, which is due in cinemas on 18 December 2026. Marvel has not said how his death affects the film, whether any performance was recorded, or whether the character will appear at all — and BuzzDaily is not going to guess. What can be said is that the announcement alone testified to his standing: at 86, more than two decades after he first played Xavier, the studio still considered him essential casting.

In the end, the death certificate changes little and settles much. It confirms, in the flat language of an official form, what his family said with far more grace three weeks ago: that Patrick Stewart's final journey was with Alzheimer's disease and Lewy body dementia, and that it ended at home, in the loving care of his wife, Sunny Ozell. He is survived by Sunny and by his children, Daniel and Sophie. The captain's chair is empty. The record, now, is complete.
