Letterkenny Creator Jared Keeso’s New Comedy I Kill the Bear Premieres on Crave — With Nickelback’s Chad Kroeger and a Real Bear

BuzzDaily Staff · Entertainment Desk
· 7 November 2026 · 6 min read
Photo: Gregory “Slobirdr” Smith · CC BY-SA 2.0 · via Wikimedia Commons · A grizzly bear in the wild · Free licence — full author and licence details on the Commons file page (file photo)

SUDBURY — Jared Keeso has built two of the most beloved comedies in Canadian television out of small towns, short tempers and perfectly timed silence. His newest series keeps the formula’s soul and adds something neither of its predecessors ever had: a bear. I Kill the Bear, a six-part Crave Original comedy, premiered on Friday 6 November 2026 on Crave in Canada, in English and French, with its first two episodes. Keeso — the creator of Letterkenny and Shoresy — created, wrote, stars in and executive-produces the series, made with New Metric Media, the company behind both of those shows, from a story by Keeso and producer Trevor Risk.

A CENTURY OF BEARS

The premise is pure Keeso: deadpan on the surface, unexpectedly heartfelt underneath. The Thibaults are a fourth-generation Franco-Ontarian family who have wrangled bears for film sets for 100 years — and after a century in the business, they have decided to make this film their last. The series is set in rural Ontario and was filmed in Sudbury, Ontario, where production began in October 2025. Within the show’s lore, the family’s résumé is the stuff of legend: they worked on virtually every major bear movie, from The Great Outdoors to Cocaine Bear, and consulted on Ted. A hundred years of other people’s bear pictures, in other words — and one last job of their own to finish.

AND YES, THERE IS A REAL BEAR

The question every cast interview eventually reaches is whether the bear is real, and Dylan Playfair — who plays Timothy Hunt — has answered it plainly: “There’s very much a bear.” Working alongside one, however, came with precautions he described with a comedian’s precision: “They kept us at a very, very safe distance from the bear. They kept the bacon in another area code.” It is the kind of detail that defines the show’s appeal — a genuinely unusual production, played entirely straight by the people inside it.

THE CAST

Keeso leads the family as Gabby Thibault. Around him: former MMA champion Georges St-Pierre as Olivier Thibault, Sarah-Maude Beauchesne as Andrée Thibault, Jonathan Torrens as Cedric Thibault, Adrian Holmes as Bob, Kristin Kreuk as Kathy, Eliana Jones as Rose, Scott Thompson of Kids in the Hall as Edward, Frederick Roy as Alex and Megha Sandhu as Ricky. Dylan Playfair — Reilly in Letterkenny — plays Timothy Hunt. And in the casting coup that has led every headline, Nickelback frontman Chad Kroeger appears as “the Son of Grizzly Adams”. The guest-star bench is just as deep: Jann Arden, Brent Butt, Kim Coates, Tricia Helfer, Michelle Mylett, Melanie Scrofano, George Stroumboulopoulos and Hannah Simone all appear, alongside B.C. performer Art d’Ecco — a roll call that reads like a cross-section of Canadian entertainment, assembled in Sudbury.

PLAYFAIR, REINVENTED

For Letterkenny fans, the most intriguing performance may be Playfair’s. Keeso and Playfair deliberately built Timothy Hunt to be very different from Reilly, the lovably dim-witted hockey player who made Playfair famous. Hunt is a rising Hollywood star who comes to Canada to film a movie about releasing a bear into the wild — and gets caught up in fallout from something in his past. “On the surface, it’s quite trivial,” Playfair said of Hunt’s troubles. “However, it’s taken very, very seriously by some people. He finds solace with the bear wranglers.” Of the series itself, he was equally direct: “It’s an exaggerated world. It’s a very unique show tonally. There’s absolutely nothing like it that I’ve ever seen.”

MUSIC, AS ALWAYS

No Keeso production treats music as decoration, and I Kill the Bear is no exception. Keeso selected the soundtrack before filming began and referenced songs directly in the script; Playfair, for his part, built a playlist to read the scripts to, and confirmed the selections amount to “a good mix of Canadian songs” — while remaining sworn to secrecy on the specific tracks. “Jared is a really big fan of music, and it’s a huge part of what has made Letterkenny and Shoresy so successful,” Playfair said. It is a fitting emphasis in a season when Canadian music keeps making international noise of its own, from Bryan Adams’ love letter to his home country to Canada’s step toward the Eurovision stage.

HOW AND WHEN TO WATCH — AND WHAT ABOUT OUTSIDE CANADA

The first two episodes of I Kill the Bear are available now on Crave, with two more arriving each Friday — the next pair on 13 November and the final two on 20 November — for six half-hour episodes in total, in English and French. For viewers outside Canada, patience is required: no platform outside Canada has been announced yet, and a US Hulu date is unconfirmed. Based on the pattern set by Shoresy, an arrival in December 2026 or early 2027 has been widely expected — but that remains an expectation, not an announcement, and BuzzDaily will report it as news only when a date is confirmed. On the evidence of its premiere — a century-old family business, a former MMA champion, a rock frontman playing the Son of Grizzly Adams, and one very real bear — it should be worth the wait.
