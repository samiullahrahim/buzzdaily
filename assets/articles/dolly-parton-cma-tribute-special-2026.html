Dolly Parton Tribute Special Airs Tonight: Carrie Underwood, Reba McEntire, Lainey Wilson and More Honor the Queen of Country on ABC

BuzzDaily Staff · Entertainment Desk
· 21 October 2026 · 8 min read
Illustration: Original artwork created for BuzzDaily

NASHVILLE — Country music rarely agrees on anything, but it agrees on this: there will never be another Dolly Parton. Tonight, the genre she defined gets its chance to say goodbye in public. “CMA Presents I Will Always Love You: A Celebration of Dolly Parton” airs this evening, Wednesday 21 October, from 9:00 to 11:00 p.m. ET on ABC and Disney+ — a two-hour televised tribute filmed before a live audience in Nashville and stacked with one of the most star-heavy lineups ever assembled for a single night of country music.

The special arrives just under two months after Parton's death on 25 August, at the age of 80. Parton, characteristically, had made her wishes plain: she chose not to have a public memorial service. There was no stadium farewell, no televised funeral, no final curtain call — just a private goodbye, on her own terms, in keeping with a woman who wrote her own story for six decades and was never going to let anyone else write the last page. Which is precisely why tonight matters. Without a public memorial, the tributes have had nowhere official to go. This two-hour special is where they land: the big, communal, televised celebration that Parton's own modesty declined to ask for, staged instead by the industry she spent a lifetime lifting up.

A WHOLE NIGHT, NOT A SEGMENT

The Country Music Association could have done this the easy way. Award shows pay tribute in segments — a medley here, a montage there, folded into the November CMA Awards somewhere between categories. Instead, the CMA gave Parton something it almost never gives anyone: an entire prime-time special of her own, separate from the awards, built around her music and her alone. Executive producer Robert Deaton has been clear about the thinking behind the scale of the show. “Dolly gave country music its heart, and giving some of that heart back to her is an incredible honor,” he said. It is a line that explains the running time. Two hours is not a segment; it is a statement — an acknowledgment that a five-minute medley would have been nowhere near enough for a catalogue this deep and a career this long.

A LINEUP ONLY DOLLY COULD ASSEMBLE

Read the performer list slowly, because it doubles as a map of the last fifty years of country music. From the generation Parton helped invent: Reba McEntire, Vince Gill and Ricky Skaggs. From the bluegrass and Americana world she always championed: Béla Fleck, Molly Tuttle and Sierra Hull. From the arena era she made possible: Carrie Underwood, Brad Paisley, Darius Rucker, Lady A, Little Big Town and Zac Brown. And from the new class now carrying the genre forward: Lainey Wilson, Ella Langley, Kelsea Ballerini, Carly Pearce, Ashley McBryde and Lukas Nelson.

The bill reaches beyond country's borders, the way Parton herself always did. Kristin Chenoweth and Kristen Bell bring Broadway and Hollywood polish to the night; special guests Amy Grant and Kimberly Williams-Paisley join the celebration; and in one of the evening's most fitting touches, a student ensemble from Belmont University also takes part — the next generation of Nashville musicians, on stage to honor the woman in whose shadow they will happily work for the rest of their careers. It is difficult to name another artist, in any genre, who could draw this exact room: Opry royalty, bluegrass virtuosos, pop-crossover stars and college students, all showing up for the same person, on the same night, for the same reason.

FILMED WHERE IT HAD TO BE FILMED

The special was filmed before a live audience at Belmont University's Fisher Center for the Performing Arts in Nashville — not in Los Angeles, not in a television studio, but in the city where Parton's story became America's story. The live audience matters more than it might seem. Tributes on this scale can feel embalmed, all polish and no pulse; a room full of fans and peers, in Music City itself, gives tonight's performances the one thing the occasion demands — witnesses. These are not studio reconstructions or clip packages. They are Parton's songs, sung back to her city, in a hall full of people who grew up inside them.

THE GIFT THAT KEEPS THE STORY GOING

Fittingly, the night gives something back in the most Dolly way imaginable. A portion of the proceeds from the special benefits Dolly Parton's Imagination Library, the book-gifting program that has become as central to her legacy as any song she ever wrote — the project built on her belief that every child deserves books of their own, delivered to their door. Of all the causes attached to all the tributes this year, this is the one that sounds most like her: practical, generous, aimed squarely at children, and designed to keep working long after the applause ends. Even in a farewell, she is still giving things away.

HOW TO WATCH TONIGHT

The broadcast airs tonight, Wednesday 21 October, from 9:00 to 11:00 p.m. ET on ABC, and streams on Disney+. If you miss the live airing, the special arrives on Hulu tomorrow, Thursday 22 October. The CMA has structured the evening as a concert celebration rather than an awards package, so expect the performances — twenty of country's biggest names working through the most beloved catalogue in the genre's history — to carry the night from first note to last.

AND THE CELEBRATIONS ARE NOT OVER

Tonight is the centerpiece of a season of remembrance, not the end of it. This winter, Parton's story reaches the stage in “Dolly: A True Original Musical,” the Broadway production built around her life, with previews set to begin on 7 December. Between the CMA special and the musical, the shape of the coming months is clear: the tributes will keep coming, because the work — and the woman — can bear the weight of all of them.

There is a temptation, with an artist this beloved, to reach for superlatives. Parton made that unnecessary decades ago. She wrote, by her own oft-told account, “Jolene” and “I Will Always Love You” in the same burst of songwriting; she built an empire without ever sanding down the accent, the wit or the kindness; and when the end came, she asked only that the goodbye be small. Country music honored the letter of that wish for two months. Tonight, from nine o'clock in Nashville, it honors something bigger — the songs, the heart Robert Deaton described, and the simple fact that for sixty years, whenever country music needed to remember what it was for, it could just point at Dolly.
