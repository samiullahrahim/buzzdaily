Flight of the Conchords Return Home: First New Zealand Tour in 14 Years Kicks Off Tonight in Wellington

BuzzDaily Staff · Entertainment Desk
· 10 November 2026 · 7 min read
Photo: Raph_PH · CC BY 2.0 · via Wikimedia Commons · Flight of the Conchords performing at the Soho Theatre, London, February 2018 · Free licence — full author and licence details on the Commons file page (file photo)

WELLINGTON — The wait ends tonight. At 8:00pm this evening, Tuesday 10 November 2026, Flight of the Conchords — Bret McKenzie and Jemaine Clement — walk on stage at the Michael Fowler Centre in Wellington to open their first New Zealand national tour in 14 years. Their last NZ-wide tour was in 2012. In the years since, the duo have become, separately and together, one of New Zealand’s most successful cultural exports. Tonight, in the city where they formed in 1998, they begin the eight shows that bring the story home.

TONIGHT AT THE MICHAEL FOWLER CENTRE

Opening night is in the capital, at a venue a short walk from the rooms where the act was built. Wellington hosts four of the tour’s eight shows at the Michael Fowler Centre — tonight, then 14, 15 and 16 November. The run also takes in Christchurch’s Wolfbrook Arena on 12 November, and closes in Auckland with two nights at The Civic on 18 and 19 November and a final show at Spark Arena on 21 November. The tour is presented by Eccles Entertainment, with tickets sold through Ticketmaster NZ on a mobile-only basis — the same ticketing route through which every one of these dates was fought over in September.

THREE MINUTES TO SOLD OUT

The demand was plain from the first morning of sales. When tickets for the initial Auckland and Wellington shows went on general sale on Friday 18 September 2026 at 1pm NZST, they sold out in just three minutes — a result that forced three extra dates to be added and stretched the run to its current eight shows. For a duo returning after an eight-year break from live performing, and fourteen years away from a home tour, the speed of that sellout settled the only question the comeback really posed: whether the audience had waited with them. It had.

THE ROAD BACK: MEOW NUI TO LOS ANGELES

The homecoming has been building all year, and it was built the slow way. The 2026 comeback began in a small room: four sold-out warm-up shows at Meow Nui in Wellington, in front of the home audience that knew the act first. Then came the international proof of concept — two sold-out nights at Los Angeles’ Greek Theatre as part of Netflix Is A Joke Fest, the duo’s first Los Angeles appearance since 2016. An eight-year break from live performance is an eternity in comedy and music alike. The warm-up shows and the Greek Theatre nights were the duo’s way of demonstrating — to promoters, and perhaps to themselves — that the act still worked at every scale, before the home tour was even announced.

FROM WELLINGTON TO THE WORLD

The outline of the career that brings them back is a matter of documented record. McKenzie and Clement formed Flight of the Conchords in Wellington in 1998. Their HBO series Flight of the Conchords ran for two seasons, from 2007 to 2009 — 22 episodes that turned two deadpan New Zealand folk parodists into cult stars in the United States. In 2008, the duo won the Grammy Award for Best Comedy Album for their EP The Distant Future, and their work collected eight Primetime Emmy nominations in total. McKenzie went on to win the Academy Award for Best Original Song for “Man or Muppet”, from The Muppets, in 2012 — the same year, as it happens, as the duo’s last New Zealand tour. The gap between that tour and tonight maps almost exactly onto the years in which the pair became film and television figures in their own right.

WHAT TONIGHT STARTS

From tonight, the tour moves quickly: eight shows inside twelve days, a compact itinerary that mixes the country’s theatre rooms with its arenas. Wellington’s four Michael Fowler Centre nights frame the run — the opener tonight, the Christchurch trip on 12 November, then back to the capital for 14, 15 and 16 November — before Auckland takes the final three: The Civic on 18 and 19 November and the Spark Arena finale on 21 November. For fans, the practical points are the ones set out when the tour was announced: the shows are presented by Eccles Entertainment and tickets are mobile-only through Ticketmaster NZ, so the ticket lives on a phone, not in an envelope. For everyone else, the shape of the run makes its own point — this is not a one-night nostalgia appearance but a full national tour, the duo’s first on home soil since 2012, built to reach all three main centres in under a fortnight.

A BIG SEASON FOR NEW ZEALAND LIVE MUSIC

The Conchords’ return lands in the middle of a notable run of major live music on these shores — BuzzDaily has been in Auckland this month for Iron Maiden’s 50th anniversary shows at Spark Arena, the same venue where this tour finishes on 21 November. But where the visiting acts bring scale, the Conchords bring something rarer: a homecoming that is literal. The act was formed in Wellington, its humour is Wellington humour, and its first audience was here. Tonight’s show is the opening date of a national tour; it is also, in the oldest sense, a local gig. Twenty-eight years after they formed, and fourteen since they last toured the country, Flight of the Conchords are home — and on the evidence of a three-minute sellout, home has been waiting.
