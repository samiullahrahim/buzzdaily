Flight of the Conchords Return Home: First New Zealand Tour in 14 Years Starts 10 November

BuzzDaily Staff · Entertainment Desk
· 5 November 2026 · 7 min read
Photo: Raph_PH · CC BY 2.0 · via Wikimedia Commons · Flight of the Conchords performing at the Soho Theatre, London, February 2018 · Free licence — full author and licence details on the Commons file page (file photo)

WELLINGTON — Fourteen years is a long time between tours, even by Flight of the Conchords’ unhurried standards. But next week the wait ends. Bret McKenzie and Jemaine Clement open their first New Zealand tour since 2012 on Tuesday 10 November at Wellington’s Michael Fowler Centre — the start of an eight-show homecoming run for a duo who became world-famous playing fictional versions of themselves struggling to make it anywhere at all. The fictional struggle is long over. What remains is the demand: when the tour’s original five dates went on sale, they sold out in just three minutes.

The tour, presented by Eccles Entertainment with tickets through Ticketmaster NZ, covers the country’s three main centres. Wellington’s Michael Fowler Centre hosts four nights — 10, 14, 15 and 16 November. Christchurch’s Wolfbrook Arena follows on 12 November. Auckland closes the run with two nights at The Civic on 18 and 19 November, plus a final show at Spark Arena on 21 November. Eight shows in twelve days is a compact itinerary, but for a duo whose last national tour predates some of their younger fans, it is the scale of the venues — arena nights in Auckland and Christchurch alongside the theatre dates — that tells the story of how large their New Zealand audience has grown in their absence.

THREE MINUTES TO SOLD OUT

The arithmetic of the announcement tells its own story. The tour was first announced with five shows; those dates sold out in three minutes. Promoters responded by adding three more — extra Wellington nights on 15 and 16 November, and the Spark Arena finale in Auckland on 21 November. Selling out a theatre run is one thing; selling it out fast enough, and emphatically enough, to justify an arena date is another. It also vindicates the slow-burn strategy the duo have taken all year: rather than announcing a stadium-scale return cold, McKenzie and Clement rebuilt their live act in small rooms first, and let word of mouth do the work that marketing usually has to.

THE ROAD BACK: MEOW NUI TO THE GREEK THEATRE

The homecoming has been carefully staged. After an eight-year pause from live performance, the duo returned earlier in 2026 with five intimate warm-up shows at Wellington’s Meow Nui — small-room performances in their home city, in front of the audiences who knew them first. From there the step up was dramatic: two sold-out performances at Los Angeles’ Greek Theatre as part of Netflix Is A Joke Fest, proof that the act’s appeal in the United States — where their HBO series made them cult stars — had survived the long silence intact. Only after Wellington’s small rooms and Los Angeles’ big one had both said yes did the full New Zealand tour follow. It is, in its way, a very Conchords trajectory: begin at home, quietly, and let the rest of the world come to you.

FROM WELLINGTON TO THE WORLD

For international readers who know the names better than the backstory, the outline is this. Flight of the Conchords rose to global fame with their Emmy-nominated HBO series Flight of the Conchords, which ran from 2007 to 2009 and recast their deadpan folk-comedy songs as the misadventures of two New Zealand musicians failing upward in New York. The television fame never fully translated into a conventional pop career — deliberately so — but the members’ solo work flourished. McKenzie won an Academy Award for Best Original Song for “Man or Muppet” from The Muppets, becoming one of the few New Zealanders to hold an Oscar for songwriting. Clement co-created two of the most beloved comedy series of the past decade, Wellington Paranormal and What We Do In The Shadows, both rooted in the same Wellington comic sensibility that produced the Conchords themselves.

A HOMECOMING, FOURTEEN YEARS ON

Announcing the tour, the NZ Music Commission described the run as the homecoming they’ve been hoping for — and the phrase captures something real about the duo’s place in New Zealand culture. McKenzie and Clement are not returning as conquering heroes from Hollywood so much as local musicians whose other jobs happen to include an Oscar and two hit television franchises. The warm-up shows at Meow Nui — a venue a fraction of the size they could have filled — were a statement about where this tour is aimed: at home audiences first. Readers planning to attend should note that the added dates were announced in response to demand, and availability should be checked with Ticketmaster NZ, the tour’s official seller.

Analysis — live comedy-music acts are not supposed to survive eight-year silences, let alone fourteen-year gaps between home tours. Flight of the Conchords have, because their catalogue was never built on hits that date, and because both men spent the intervening years making work that kept their sensibility in front of audiences worldwide. The three-minute sellout suggests New Zealand never really let them go. From 10 November in Wellington, the country gets them back — in theatres, in arenas, and, for the first time since 2012, on a proper national tour. It is shaping up as one of the live events of the New Zealand year, alongside other major tours reaching the country this season, including Robbie Williams’ Britpop tour dates in New Zealand — though few visitors will receive quite the welcome reserved for the home team.
