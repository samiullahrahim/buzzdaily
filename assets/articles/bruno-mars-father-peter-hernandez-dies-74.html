Bruno Mars Announces Death of His Father Peter Hernandez, 74: 'Last Week I Got to Hug My Father One Last Time'

BuzzDaily Staff · US Desk
· 20 November 2026 · 8 min read
Photo: Glenn Samonte, CC BY 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons · Bruno Mars performing on The Romantic Tour

LOS ANGELES — Bruno Mars has announced the death of his father, Peter Hernandez, the Brooklyn-born percussionist who put him on a stage before he was old enough to start school. The 41-year-old singer shared the news on Instagram on Thursday, 19 November, writing: “Last week I got to hug my father one last time.” Peter Hernandez was 74.

The post paired a black-and-white photograph of Mars hugging his father in a hospital bed with throwback pictures of the two of them across the years. His tribute read, in full: “If you've ever seen me talk about him I've always said that he's the man that taught me everything I know, especially when it comes to music. He raised my four beautiful sisters, my brother, and me. In my eyes he was the greatest dad in the world, and I will miss him everyday until we meet again. I Love you Pops. Kiss mom up there for me.”

The photographs Mars chose told the family's story in sequence: the hospital-bed hug first, then the throwbacks — his father in his younger years, the two of them together when Bruno was a small boy in the show, Peter Hernandez smiling out from an audience, and, in the final image, his mother Bernadette sitting in his father's lap. It was Bernadette's presence in that last photograph that made the tribute's closing line land the way it did, as thousands of replies beneath the post made clear.

THE MAN WHO TAUGHT HIM EVERYTHING

Peter Hernandez was a percussionist from Brooklyn who moved to Hawaii in the 1970s and built his life in music there, fronting a doo-wop revue in Waikiki. He introduced his son to music at the age of four and performed with him as a child — the boy who would become Bruno Mars began, quite literally, in his father's show. So when Mars says his father taught him everything he knows, especially when it comes to music, it is not a metaphor. It is a description of his childhood: the harmonies, the stagecraft and the old-school showmanship that still run through his records were learned at his father's side.

The debt is in his name, too. Bruno Mars was born Peter Gene Hernandez, named after his father. The stage name came later; the first name, the one on his birth certificate, is his father's. It is a detail fans returned to as the announcement spread — on Google Trends, “peter hernandez” reached number two in the United States with more than 20,000 searches, and the top three in the United Kingdom, Canada and Australia.

A SECOND LOSS

Mars has now lost both of his parents. His mother, Bernadette Hernandez, died in 2013, aged 55, reportedly from a brain aneurysm — which is what gives the final line of his tribute its weight. The post announcing his father's death closes with a son asking his father to carry a message to his mother: “Kiss mom up there for me.”

What the family has not shared, BuzzDaily will not invent. No cause of death has been disclosed for Peter Hernandez, and no exact date of death has been announced, beyond Mars's words that he hugged his father “one last time” last week. Any report claiming either detail should be treated with caution until the family itself says more.

TRIBUTES, AND THE TOUR AHEAD

Among those to pay tribute was Rosé, Mars's collaborator on their global hit “APT.”, who joined the flood of fans and fellow musicians responding to the post. The outpouring reflected how openly Mars has always spoken about his father: across his career, in interview after interview, the percussionist from Brooklyn has been a constant presence — credited with the doo-wop harmonies, the footwork and the reverence for the great entertainers that defined his son's act long before the stadiums.

The influence is written across his catalogue. His debut album was titled Doo-Wops & Hooligans — the doo-wop of his father's Waikiki revue carried into the name of the record that made him famous — and his stage show, from the horn lines to the choreographed harmonies, remains a tribute to the era of music Peter Hernandez performed for a living. That is why this week's announcement has been read by fans as more than a family bereavement. The man being mourned is, in a real sense, the origin of the act itself.

Mars is scheduled to resume The Romantic Tour with shows in Mexico City from 3 December 2026, and no change to those dates has been announced. For now, the tour — like everything else in his career — runs through the man who started it: a father who handed a four-year-old a place in the band, and who received his goodbye, forty years on, in front of the whole world.
