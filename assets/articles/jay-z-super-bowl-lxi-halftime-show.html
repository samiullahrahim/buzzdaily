Jay-Z Confirmed to Headline Super Bowl LXI Halftime Show at SoFi Stadium

BuzzDaily Staff · Entertainment Desk
· 27 October 2026 · 9 min read
Photo: Rory · CC BY 2.0 · via Wikimedia Commons · Jay-Z performing at The O2 Arena, London, 2012 · Free licence — full author and licence details on the Commons file page (file photo)

INGLEWOOD, California — The biggest unanswered question in American entertainment has its answer. Jay-Z has been officially confirmed as the headliner of the Super Bowl LXI halftime show, in a joint announcement by the NFL, Apple Music and Roc Nation made on Monday night, 26 October, during halftime of the Dallas Cowboys vs Philadelphia Eagles Monday Night Football game. The performance — officially the Apple Music Super Bowl LXI Halftime Show — will take place on Sunday 14 February 2027 at SoFi Stadium in Inglewood, California: the same stadium where, just two days earlier, Jay-Z had closed his 'Jay-Z 30' concert run and walked off stage without saying a word about it.

“From Reasonable Doubt to standing on the Super Bowl Halftime stage, this has been a journey,” Jay-Z said in a statement released with the announcement. “Thirty years of music, work, and culture on the world's biggest stage. This run continues to be a dream come true.” The booking makes Jay-Z the latest — and, by any measure, the most institutionally significant — headliner of the Roc Nation era of the halftime show: the era he himself has run since 2019. For American audiences, who have spent the autumn searching for exactly this answer, the speculation that has followed every halftime announcement for seven years is over. The curator is taking the stage.

AN ANNOUNCEMENT MADE FOR PRIME TIME

The staging of the reveal was itself a statement. Rather than a press release dropped on a weekday morning, the confirmation arrived at halftime of Monday Night Football, one of the most-watched weekly slots in American television, during a Cowboys–Eagles game — a rivalry fixture guaranteed to hold a mass national audience. By the time the second half kicked off, the news had already ricocheted across social media in the United States and, within hours, through British entertainment coverage as well, with the Bury Times and PA among the UK outlets carrying the announcement to readers for whom the Super Bowl halftime show has become an annual pop-cultural fixture in its own right.

NFL Commissioner Roger Goodell framed the booking as the fulfilment of a long-standing personal request. Goodell said that when the Roc Nation agreement began in 2019, he had one request of Jay-Z: that he take the halftime stage himself one day. That day, seven years on, is now set. Oliver Schusser, Apple's vice president of Apple Music and Beats, added Apple's welcome in the joint announcement, with Apple Music presenting the halftime show at Super Bowl LXI — the latest edition of a partnership that has turned the thirteen-minute performance into one of the most valuable stages in global music.

THE CURATOR BECOMES THE HEADLINER

To understand why this confirmation lands differently from any other halftime announcement, it helps to understand the job Jay-Z has held since 2019. That year, the NFL entered its partnership with Roc Nation and Jay-Z became the league's live music entertainment strategist, a role that has made him the most influential figure in shaping the modern halftime show. Under his stewardship, the show has been rebuilt into a run of era-defining headline performances: Rihanna's aerial return in 2023, Usher's catalogue-spanning showcase in 2024, Kendrick Lamar's headline set in 2025, and Bad Bunny's performance at the 2026 show, which averaged 128.2 million viewers and went on to win Emmy Awards.

Each of those bookings carried Jay-Z's fingerprints — an emphasis on cultural weight, catalogue depth and artists at a defining moment. What the run never included, year after year, was his own name. Every announcement cycle renewed the same question from fans and commentators alike: if the halftime show is now curated to this standard, when does its curator qualify? With Monday's confirmation, the league and Roc Nation have answered that the standard now points to the man who set it. It is the first time in the show's modern history that the executive most responsible for choosing the headliner and the headliner himself are the same person.

THIRTY YEARS FROM REASONABLE DOUBT TO SOFI

The arc Jay-Z referenced in his statement is a matter of record. His debut album, “Reasonable Doubt”, arrived in 1996, released through the Roc-A-Fella Records label he co-founded after major labels passed. The three decades since have produced one of the most decorated careers in popular music: 25 Grammy Awards, a place as the first rapper inducted into the Songwriters Hall of Fame, and induction into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2021. Along the way he moved from performer to institution — president of Def Jam, founder of Roc Nation, and hip-hop's first billionaire — while his catalogue, from “Hard Knock Life” to “Empire State of Mind”, became part of the shared American songbook.

That history is precisely what the 'Jay-Z 30' run has been celebrating this year. The concert series marked the thirtieth anniversary of “Reasonable Doubt” across five cities, closing with two nights at SoFi Stadium on 23 and 24 October. As BuzzDaily reported on Monday, the finale brought Dr. Dre, Snoop Dogg and Too $hort to the stage — and ended with Jay-Z declining to address the halftime speculation swirling around the building. This confirmation is a new development, and it changes that story rather than repeating it: what was an open question on Saturday night is, as of Monday night, officially settled.

THE DEBATE: WHAT IT MEANS WHEN THE SELECTOR SELECTS HIMSELF

The announcement has also revived a debate that has run online since the Roc Nation partnership began, and it is one BuzzDaily reports without taking a side. Critics of the arrangement argue that the optics are unavoidable: as the NFL's live music entertainment strategist, Jay-Z occupies the most influential seat in the room where the headliner is chosen, and naming himself invites questions about how independent that process can appear — however strong the artistic case may be. Some fans, too, have spent recent cycles campaigning for other artists and have greeted the news as the closing-off of a slot they hoped would go elsewhere.

Supporters answer with the record. They point out, first, that Commissioner Goodell's request that Jay-Z perform predates the announcement by years — made, on Goodell's own account, at the very start of the agreement in 2019 — which they argue reframes the booking as a long-deferred invitation rather than a sudden self-appointment. They note that the halftime stage has only grown in scale and prestige on Jay-Z's watch, that his catalogue and honours place him among a small handful of living artists with the stature the show demands, and that previous headliners from Rihanna to Bad Bunny were celebrated picks from the same system. Between those two positions sits the undisputed fact at the centre of it all: whoever one believes chose him, Jay-Z will be the halftime headliner on 14 February 2027, and the performance will be judged the way every halftime show is judged — live, in thirteen minutes, in front of the largest audience in television.

WHAT HAS NOT BEEN ANNOUNCED

For all the certainty of Monday's confirmation, almost everything about the performance itself remains unannounced, and readers should treat the speculation already circulating with care. No setlist has been released. No guest performers have been confirmed, and none were named in the joint announcement. Online discussion about surprise appearances, medleys and staging is, at this stage, exactly that — unconfirmed speculation, however confidently it is presented on social media. BuzzDaily will report guests, creative details and production plans only when they are confirmed by the NFL, Apple Music, Roc Nation or Jay-Z's representatives.

ANALYSIS: THE LAST STAGE, ON HIS OWN TERMS

Analysis — the halftime show was, for years, the one major American stage Jay-Z had never headlined, and the structure of Monday's announcement suggests he understood its weight. The news was not folded into his own concert finale at the same stadium two nights earlier, when it would have been a Jay-Z story told to Jay-Z's audience. It was delivered instead by the league, the sponsor and his company together, at halftime of a football game — an institutional announcement, made on the NFL's turf, in the NFL's biggest weekly window. For US audiences, February's show now sets up as a genuine cultural summit: a thirty-year catalogue, performed by the man who spent seven years redefining what that stage is for. For UK viewers, who have made the halftime show a Monday-morning ritual of clips and conversation, it is the rare booking whose backstory is already as famous as the performance promises to be.

There is a symmetry to it that no other candidate could have offered. In February, Jay-Z returns to the stadium where 'Jay-Z 30' ended — not as the executive watching somebody else's thirteen minutes, but as the artist inside his own. “This run continues to be a dream come true,” he said on Monday. Whatever side of the selection debate readers take, the run itself now has its final date: Super Bowl LXI, SoFi Stadium, 14 February 2027.
