Australia Win Fourth Straight Rugby League World Cup as Kangaroos Beat New Zealand 32–12 in Suncorp Final

BuzzDaily Staff · Australia Desk
· 15 November 2026 · 8 min read
Photo: Sampajanna / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 4.0) · Suncorp Stadium, Brisbane, packed for State of Origin 3 in 2022 (file photo) · Free licence — full author and licence details on the Commons file page

BRISBANE — The dynasty rolls on. Australia beat New Zealand 32–12 at Suncorp Stadium on Sunday to win a fourth consecutive Rugby League World Cup — the Kangaroos’ 13th world title overall — in front of a crowd of 52,461. On a day when New Zealand’s search traffic was consumed by this single fixture, the Kangaroos settled the final early and never really let go.

84 SECONDS THAT SET THE TONE

Finals are supposed to be tense. This one was tilted inside two minutes. Liam Martin scored the fastest-ever try in a World Cup final after just 84 seconds, and Australia were on their way. Martin scored two tries in all, and by half-time the Kangaroos led 24–6 — a scoreline that turned the second half into the defence of a lead rather than a contest for one. New Zealand, chasing the game almost from kick-off, never found the foothold a comeback would have required.

The setting deserved a tighter contest. Suncorp Stadium is rugby league’s cathedral in Brisbane, and 52,461 people filled it for a final between the sport’s two defining nations — the fixture the whole tournament had been bending towards. Instead, the home crowd got a coronation. An 84-second try does something psychological as well as numerical: it tells the favourites the day is theirs, and it tells the underdogs that the plan they carried into the biggest game of their lives has about ninety seconds of life left in it. Everything New Zealand did afterwards was done uphill, against the scoreboard and against an Australian side that could afford to play the percentages.

BOSTOCK’S NIGHT

The player-of-the-match award went to Jack Bostock, who scored two tries on the biggest night of his career. Bradman Best and Tom Trbojevic also scored for Australia, spreading the damage across the backline: six Australian tries in all, accounting for the bulk of the 32–12 scoreline. It was too much strike, in too many places, for the Kiwis to cover — every time New Zealand steadied, another Kangaroo found the line.

TEDESCO’S LAST TEST

There was a farewell folded inside the celebrations. James Tedesco, the veteran fullback who captained Australia to the 2022 title, came off the bench late in what was his last Test match. A captain of one World Cup win closing his international career as a squad man in another is a tidy piece of symmetry, and the Suncorp crowd recognised the moment. The final had been billed all week as a changing of the guard as much as a title decider — BuzzDaily’s pre-match preview of the final set out the stakes for both sides — and Tedesco’s late cameo gave the transition a human face.

23 STRAIGHT — AND 2008 STAYS A MEMORY

The records keep compounding. Australia have now won 23 consecutive World Cup matches, a streak of dominance that stretches across tournaments and generations of players. For New Zealand, the wait goes on: their only World Cup final win remains 2008, which is also the last time Australia lost a final. Every Kiwi campaign since has run into the same green-and-gold wall, and Sunday added another brick to it.

THE JILLAROOS MAKE IT A DOUBLE

The men’s final was only half the day’s story at Suncorp. In the women’s final at the same stadium on the same day, Australia’s Jillaroos beat New Zealand 34–10, with Jaime Chapman scoring a hat-trick. Two finals, two Australian wins, one stadium: a clean sweep of the trans-Tasman double-header, and a day Brisbane will remember as its own.

THE MATCH NEW ZEALAND COULDN’T STOP SEARCHING FOR

If you want a measure of what this fixture means across the Tasman, look at the search bar. New Zealand’s Google Trends top 10 today is dominated by the match — “suncorp stadium”, “new zealand vs australia”, “kalyn ponga” and “australia vs new zealand” all riding high as Kiwi fans picked over the defeat and the occasion. That is what a World Cup final between these two countries does: for a day, it becomes the whole national conversation, searched from every angle — the venue, the rivalry, the star fullback, the fixture itself — even when the result has run away from the team doing the searching. Rugby league’s place in the culture runs beyond the field, too: the code’s most famous club owner remains Russell Crowe, whose orbit BuzzDaily recently entered as Daniel MacPherson joined Crowe and Priyanka Chopra in the sci-fi thriller Bluefly, filming on Australia’s Gold Coast.

For Australia, a fourth straight crown and a 13th title confirm what the last month suggested: the Kangaroos remain the sport’s benchmark, and the benchmark is not moving. For New Zealand, 2008 stays where it has stayed ever since — in the history books, waiting for company.
