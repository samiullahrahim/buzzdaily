Ezra Frech Makes History as First Paralympian to Win Dancing With the Stars — Prosthetic Leg Falls Off Mid-Freestyle and He Dances On

BuzzDaily Staff · US Desk
· 25 November 2026 · 6 min read
Photo: International Paralympic Committee via Paralympic.org · Ezra Frech in the high jump at the Paris 2024 Paralympic Games (file photo)

LOS ANGELES — Ezra Frech had already made history before the confetti fell. When the 21-year-old Paralympic champion and his professional partner Daniella Karagach were named winners of Dancing With the Stars season 35 on Tuesday night, 24 November 2026, Frech became the first Paralympian — and the first amputee — ever to lift the Len Goodman Mirrorball Trophy, and the youngest man ever to win the show. He did it with a perfect score of 90 out of 90, on a night when his prosthetic leg came off in the middle of his freestyle and he simply fitted it back on and danced to the end. “This is the most special moment of my life,” Frech said.

A THREE-HOUR FINALE AND A PERFECT 90

The three-hour finale aired live on ABC and Disney+, with streaming on Hulu the following day, and brought a long season down to its final five couples. Frech and Karagach’s perfect 90 was assembled across three dances, each awarded full marks by judges Carrie Ann Inaba, Derek Hough and Bruno Tonioli. Their Judges’ Choice dance was a salsa to “Danza Kuduro”, which scored 30. Their Instant Dance — the round in which couples learn their music and style only moments before performing — was a tango to “Levels” by Avicii, which scored another 30. And their freestyle, the showpiece round in which the professionals can use any staging and any number of extra dancers, was set to “Numb” by Linkin Park and completed the perfect set with a third 30. In a format where a single dropped mark usually decides the trophy, three flawless scores in one night is about as complete a finale performance as the show produces.

THE MOMENT HIS PROSTHETIC LEG CAME OFF

The moment the night will be remembered for arrived during that freestyle. Mid-routine, Frech’s prosthetic leg came off. Rather than stopping, appealing to the judges or waiting for the music to end, he fitted the leg back on and carried on dancing, finishing the routine and collecting the third perfect score of his night. It was the kind of unplanned moment live television cannot script, and it distilled the story of his season into a few seconds: a problem that would have ended most performances became part of his. For viewers who had followed him from the Paralympic track to the ballroom floor, the recovery said as much about why he won as any of the steps did — the freestyle was not perfect because nothing went wrong, but because the worst thing that could have gone wrong did, and it changed nothing.

“EZRA, YOU ARE A HERO”

The judges’ response matched the scale of the night. Derek Hough told him: “Ezra you are a hero, this performance will have a ripple effect far beyond this show.” Bruno Tonioli called him “a national treasure”. Frech’s own verdict was simpler: “This is the most special moment of my life.” Between them, the three reactions captured the unusual double life of the win. Inside the ballroom it was a dance result, earned on technique across three rounds. Outside it, the panel was openly treating the performance as something with consequences beyond entertainment — a prime-time audience of millions watching a Paralympic champion win a mainstream dance competition outright, on merit, against non-disabled celebrity competitors.

HOW THE FINAL FIVE FINISHED

The standard behind Frech was high enough that his perfect score was not unique. Frech and Karagach finished first, but Amber Glenn and Pasha Pashkov, who took second place, also scored 90 out of 90 — as did Maura Higgins and Mark Ballas, who finished fifth. Connor Wood and Rylee Arnold placed third, and Tatyana Ali and Jan Ravnik placed fourth. With three of the five couples level on a perfect judges’ total, the audience vote carried unusual weight, and the audience duly delivered a record: more than 86 million votes were cast across the finale. That combination — maximum marks from the judges and a record-breaking public vote — is what carried Frech and Karagach to the trophy on a night when the scoreboard alone could not separate the field.

FROM PARIS GOLD TO THE MIRRORBALL

Frech arrived in the ballroom with a sporting record most celebrity contestants cannot approach. At the Paris 2024 Paralympic Games he won two gold medals, in the T63 100 metres and the T63 high jump, establishing himself as one of the standout American athletes of those Games while still a teenager in international terms. The T63 classification covers athletes who compete with a prosthesis, and Frech has spent his career as one of the most visible faces of Paralympic sport in the United States. His Dancing With the Stars win extends that visibility into an entirely different arena: he is the first Paralympian and the first amputee to win the American series, and, at 21, the youngest man ever to take the title. The prosthetic leg that came off during his freestyle is the same technology he races and jumps with — a reminder that the storyline the show could not script was, for Frech, simply his everyday equipment failing at an inconvenient moment, and being dealt with the way he deals with everything else.

THE ROAD THROUGH THE SEMIFINALS

The finale closed a season whose penultimate week had already produced a shock. In the semifinals on 17 November, Harry Shum Jr. was eliminated despite two perfect scores of his own — a result BuzzDaily reported in its semifinals coverage — after the combined vote left him short of the final five. That result underlined the lesson the finale confirmed: in the closing weeks of this season, perfect dancing was necessary but never sufficient, and the public vote decided who the perfect scores belonged to. A week later, with 86 million votes counted, the public chose the Paralympian — and chose him, fittingly, on the night he refused to stop dancing.
