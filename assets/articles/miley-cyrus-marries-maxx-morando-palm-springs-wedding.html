Miley Cyrus Marries Maxx Morando in Intimate Palm Springs Wedding

BuzzDaily Staff · US Desk
· 22 November 2026 · 8 min read
Photo: Raphael Pour-Hashemi / CC BY 2.0 via Wikimedia Commons · Miley Cyrus performing at Primavera Sound, Barcelona, 2019 (file photo)

PALM SPRINGS — Miley Cyrus has married Maxx Morando, the musician she has been with for five years, in a private ceremony in Palm Springs, California, on Saturday, 21 November 2026. The wedding has been confirmed by PEOPLE and Entertainment Tonight, and reported by Reuters, Entertainment Weekly, Page Six and Hello! magazine. There has been no personal statement from the couple: the news rests on reporting and photographs from the day, not on an announcement of their own — a notably low-key confirmation for one of the most recognisable pop stars in the world.

A POOLSIDE CEREMONY IN PALM SPRINGS

The ceremony took place at a private residence in the desert resort city. Cyrus, 33, and Morando, 28, exchanged vows beside a swimming pool, under an arch of white flowers and roses. The celebrations had begun the night before, with a rehearsal dinner on Friday, 20 November, ahead of the Saturday wedding — a full weekend built around family and close friends rather than a single public spectacle.

The service was led by a friend from the music world: Wayne Coyne, the frontman of The Flaming Lips, officiated. Coyne is a long-standing figure in Cyrus's musical life, a collaborator from her psychedelic pop era — a choice of officiant that says something about the couple's taste for keeping the day personal rather than formal, with the ceremony conducted by someone from their own world.

140 GUESTS, ALL IN BLACK

Around 140 guests attended, and the dress code was striking in its simplicity: everyone wore black. The guest list was led by family. Cyrus's mother, Tish Cyrus, attended with Dominic Purcell. Her father, Billy Ray Cyrus, was there with Elizabeth Hurley, who was accompanied by her son, Damian Hurley. Brandi Cyrus, the bride's older sister, attended with her fiancé, Matt Southcombe. For a family that has lived much of its life in public — and not always in harmony — the wedding photographs show the whole clan assembled in one place, in matching black, for the same photograph.

THE DRESS: WHITE SATIN, HIGH NECK, NO VEIL

Against the sea of black, the bride wore white: a long-sleeved white satin gown with a high neck and a low back. She wore no veil, and her hair was styled up, in an updo. Morando kept to the guests' palette in a black tuxedo. The contrast — one white dress in a gathering dressed entirely in black — gave the images from the day an unusually graphic, editorial quality, and the styling was characteristically assured: classic in silhouette, with the drama supplied by the dress code around her rather than by the gown itself.

FIVE YEARS, THEN A RING AT THE AVATAR PREMIERE

Cyrus and Morando were first linked in 2021 and have been together for five years, largely out of the spotlight: the occasional red carpet, the occasional photograph, and very little commentary from either of them. Their engagement was confirmed in December 2025, after Cyrus debuted her engagement ring at the premiere of Avatar: Fire and Ash. Saturday's wedding, eleven months later, was conducted in the same register as the relationship itself — private, family-centred, and confirmed after the fact rather than trailed in advance.

WHO IS MAXX MORANDO?

Morando, 28, is a drummer and producer. He first came to attention as the drummer of The Regrettes, the Los Angeles pop-punk band, and he now plays in Liily, the LA rock band. He has also worked behind the scenes as a producer, and he and Cyrus move in overlapping musical circles — the relationship grew out of that shared working world rather than the celebrity circuit, which may explain how completely the couple have managed to keep it on their own terms for five years.

MILEY'S YEAR: SOMETHING BEAUTIFUL

The wedding caps a settled and successful period in Cyrus's career. Her ninth studio album, Something Beautiful, arrived in 2025, continuing the run of form that carried her to the top of the charts in the “Flowers” era. In October she was on stage at the Hollywood Bowl for the tribute to her godmother, Dolly Parton, as BuzzDaily reported at the time — an appearance that underlined how completely Cyrus now moves between pop's present and its heritage. A wedding conducted quietly, at a home in the desert, fits the same pattern: the spectacle is reserved for the work, not the private life.

For a star whose life has been documented since childhood, Saturday's ceremony was a reminder that the biggest moments can still be kept small. One hundred and forty people, a swimming pool, an arch of white roses, a friend to conduct the service — and, from the couple themselves, no statement at all. The reporting has done the talking, and on the evidence of the photographs, that was exactly the plan.
