Gabriel Iglesias Falls Ill On Stage in Raleigh — Rep Says the Comedian Is ‘Doing Fine’

BuzzDaily Staff · US Desk
· 24 November 2026 · 6 min read
Photo: Gage Skidmore / CC BY-SA 3.0 via Wikimedia Commons · Gabriel Iglesias at a fan convention (file photo)

RALEIGH — Gabriel Iglesias had warned the crowd. The comedian, 50, known to fans around the world as ‘Fluffy’, told the audience at the Lenovo Center in Raleigh, North Carolina, at the start of his set on Sunday, 22 November 2026, that he was not feeling well. He performed anyway — and almost made it. With only minutes left in the one-hour show, Iglesias stood up from his stool, hunched over and began to sway, and the night ended with fellow comedian Martin Moreno and security staff helping one of the biggest touring comics in the world off his own stage. By Monday, his representative was reassuring fans that Iglesias is ‘doing fine’ — but the footage from Raleigh had already travelled everywhere.

WHAT HAPPENED ON STAGE

The show was part of Iglesias’s ongoing ‘The 1976 Tour: A Fluffy Celebration’. According to reports from the room, the closing minutes turned alarming quickly: a TikTok video from the show captured Iglesias crouched over the stool he had been sitting on, and Moreno — who had introduced him earlier in the evening — rushed over with security to help him off stage. Iglesias called out for Moreno and asked for oxygen to be ready backstage, and he received a breathing treatment once he was off. One audience member told TMZ that the comedian was sweating profusely and looked really sick. Despite the dramatic scene, Iglesias was not hospitalized, according to TMZ sources — a detail that shaped the calmer tone of the statements that followed on Monday.

‘HE TRIED TO PULL THROUGH’

On Monday, 23 November, Iglesias’s representative gave the same account to PEOPLE and USA TODAY: the comedian had been sick for ‘the last week or so’ but did not want to cancel on his fans, ‘so he tried to pull through’. The representative said ‘Gabe is doing much better today’ and that he is ‘doing fine’. Iglesias himself has not publicly addressed the incident, and the exact nature of the illness has not been disclosed. That leaves the official picture deliberately narrow: a comic who worked through a week of sickness because he did not want to disappoint a sold-out arena, a frightening few minutes at the end of a Sunday show, treatment backstage rather than in a hospital, and a recovery his team describes as already well under way.

THE TOUR CARRIES ON

Nothing in the schedule, so far, has moved. Iglesias’s previous show — on Saturday, 21 November at Chartway Arena in Norfolk, Virginia — went fine, a reminder of how quickly a manageable illness can turn into a stage emergency under lights, in front of thousands of people, at the end of an hour of performing. His next shows are close together: Saturday, 28 November in Memphis, at FedExForum, and Sunday, 29 November in Chattanooga. December dates in Australia follow, and the tour runs deep into 2027 — the last currently listed show is on 1 May 2027 in Chicago. As of Tuesday, 24 November, no cancellations have been confirmed, and fans holding tickets for Memphis and Chattanooga have been given no reason to change their plans. Arena stand-up is an endurance sport disguised as a night out, and the Raleigh show is now the tour’s defining image: not the size of the room, but the sight of its star being carried out of it — and, if his team is right, walking back into the next one within the week.

FLUFFY AT 50

The tour itself is a celebration with the joke built into its title. ‘The 1976 Tour: A Fluffy Celebration’, announced in May 2025, is named for Iglesias’s birth year — a 50th-birthday lap for a comedian whose act has always turned on appetite, family and self-deprecation. Iglesias built his following on animated storytelling: characters, voices and sound effects conjured out of a stool, a microphone and an apparently bottomless memory for the rhythms of everyday embarrassment. His screen work has carried that persona into film, including a turn in Magic Mike XXL. Fifty, a birthday tour, a week of ignored illness and a collapse minutes from the end of a show is a sequence that writes its own moral, and it is one his fans have been quick to supply online: rest is also part of the job. Whether the tour builds any rest into the weeks ahead is the question now hanging over Memphis.
