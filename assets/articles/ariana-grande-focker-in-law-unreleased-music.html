Ariana Grande Teased Unreleased Music to Her ‘Focker-in-Law’ Co-Stars — ‘Nobody Should Be Able to Do This’

BuzzDaily Staff · US Desk
· 4 November 2026 · 6 min read
Photo: Emma Sheehan / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 2.0) · Ariana Grande on the Dangerous Woman Tour, February 2017

The cast of Focker-in-Law has heard the next chapter of Ariana Grande’s music career before anyone else — and by one co-star’s account, they were left almost indignant at how good it was. Skyler Gisondo, who stars opposite Grande in the upcoming comedy, told The Hollywood Reporter that during production Grande played a selection of unreleased songs for her fellow cast members: a private preview of material that has no title in public, no track listing and no release date. Gisondo’s review, delivered straight to Grande, has since travelled around the entertainment press: “Nobody should be able to do this.”

It is the kind of quote publicists dream of, and it has now been carried by People, by The Hollywood Reporter’s reporting via Bang Premier, and by US radio coverage of the film this week. What it describes is simple enough: one of the biggest pop stars on the planet, in the middle of shooting a studio comedy, pressing play on music the world has not heard — for an audience of actors. No announcement accompanied it. No teaser campaign followed. The songs, as far as the public record goes, exist only in the memory of the people who were in the room.

THE FILM: A FOURTH TRIP TO THE FOCKERS

Focker-in-Law is the fourth film in the Meet the Parents series and arrives in theaters on 25 November 2026. Grande, 33, stars opposite Gisondo, 30, playing his love interest — the new partner brought home to meet a family that has been professionally suspicious of newcomers for a quarter of a century. Around them sits one of the most decorated comedy ensembles of the modern era: Ben Stiller and Robert De Niro, whose in-law rivalry powered the original film, return alongside Blythe Danner, Teri Polo and Beanie Feldstein.

The series began with Meet the Parents in 2000 and grew through Meet the Fockers and Little Fockers into a defining comedy franchise of its period, built on a single reliable engine: an outsider, a household’s worth of suspicion, and a family gathering that goes wrong. Focker-in-Law resets that engine for a new generation, with Grande — fresh from the Wicked films — stepping into the outsider’s chair. Her recent screen work has already generated its own headlines, including her appearance with Cynthia Erivo at the Wicked premiere in Singapore, and the casting gives this franchise a pop-cultural charge it has never had before.

A YEAR OF ALBUMS, TOURS AND FILM SETS

To understand why an impromptu listening session matters, consider Grande’s calendar. She last released music in July, when her eighth studio album, Petal, arrived. In September she concluded her Eternal Sunshine tour, closing out the live chapter of the era that had defined her previous two years. And through it all she was shooting Focker-in-Law. Album, tour, franchise film: by any normal standard that is a full year’s work several times over. The unreleased songs suggest it was not the whole story — that somewhere inside that schedule, new music was being written and recorded as well.

Artists at Grande’s level rarely have empty hard drives, of course. Songs are stockpiled, shelved, reworked and sometimes abandoned between album cycles. What makes this different is the audience. Playing new material to a film cast — colleagues from a different corner of her career, with no stake in her release strategy — reads less like marketing and more like confidence: the instinct of a singer who has finished something she likes and cannot resist sharing it. Gisondo’s reaction suggests the songs landed exactly as intended.

WHAT IT COULD MEAN FOR THE NEXT ALBUM

So what happens next? The honest answer is that nobody outside Grande’s camp has said. There is no official release date for the new songs, no confirmed album announcement, and no indication yet whether the tracks Gisondo heard are bound for a ninth studio album, a deluxe edition of Petal, a standalone single, or the vault. BuzzDaily is not going to pretend otherwise. What can be said is this: the material exists, it was finished enough to play to colleagues, and it impressed a room full of professional performers. Historically, that combination — a completed-sounding batch of songs and an artist eager to play them — tends to surface in public sooner rather than later. But ‘sooner’ in Grande’s recent pattern could mean anything from a surprise release to a carefully staged campaign well into next year. For an artist who has spent two years turning one album cycle into a film-and-tour era, a handful of finished, unheard songs is not a footnote — it is a starting gun waiting for a date.

For now, the calendar offers one fixed point: 25 November, when Focker-in-Law opens and Grande’s year of doing everything at once reaches its final premiere. The music will follow when she decides it follows. Until then, the only review on record belongs to Skyler Gisondo, and it is hard to improve on: “Nobody should be able to do this.”
