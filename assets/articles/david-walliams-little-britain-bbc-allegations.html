BBC Removes Little Britain From iPlayer as David Walliams Denies Sexual Misconduct Allegations

BuzzDaily Staff · Entertainment Desk
· 11 November 2026 · 7 min read
Photo: Sean Reynolds / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY 2.0) · David Walliams at a Britain's Got Talent event (file photo) · Free licence — full author and licence details on the Commons file page

LONDON — The BBC is removing Little Britain from BBC iPlayer while it reviews all material featuring or written by David Walliams, after a joint investigation by The Guardian and ITV News published allegations of sexual misconduct against the comedian and author on Tuesday 10 November 2026. Walliams, 55, denies all of the allegations. Responding to the investigation, he said: “I am not a sexual predator. I have not broken the law.” The allegations are unproven: no police investigation or criminal charges have been reported, and nothing alleged in the investigation has been tested in court. BuzzDaily reports the claims strictly as allegations, and Walliams's denial stands at the centre of this report.

THE INVESTIGATION

The reporting at the centre of the story is the product of a three-year joint investigation by The Guardian and ITV News, published on Tuesday 10 November 2026. According to that investigation, multiple women allege that they were preyed on by Walliams for sexual purposes when they were teenagers or young women. Two of the women say they first met him when they were aged 15. Those are the investigation's allegations, reported here as claims made by the women who spoke to the two news organisations — not as findings of fact. Walliams denies them in full, and his denial is set out below in his own words.

“I AM NOT A SEXUAL PREDATOR” — THE DENIAL

Walliams has denied all of the allegations. His denial, issued as the investigation was published, is short and categorical: “I am not a sexual predator. I have not broken the law.” That denial applies to the investigation's claims in their entirety, and it is the position he maintains as broadcasters, publishers and partners act around him. Readers should hold the two elements of this story together: serious allegations, published by two major news organisations after a three-year investigation — and a complete denial from the man they concern, against whom no criminal process has been reported and whose case no court has heard.

THE BBC ACTS: LITTLE BRITAIN REMOVED FROM iPLAYER

On 10 November, the BBC said it is reviewing all material featuring or written by Walliams, and that it is removing some programmes from iPlayer while that review is under way. The removals are extensive. The whole of Little Britain — the sketch show Walliams co-created with Matt Lucas, and the programme that made both men stars — has been taken down. Come Fly With Me, the pair's later sketch series, has also been removed, along with episodes of The Wheel and Would I Lie To You? featuring Walliams, and “The God Complex”, the 2011 Doctor Who episode in which he appeared. The BBC's position, as stated, is one of review rather than final judgement: programmes are being removed while the review is under way, and the corporation says it is looking at all material featuring or written by him.

AGENT, PUBLISHER AND SKY MOVE ON THE SAME DAY

The professional consequences extended well beyond the BBC, and they arrived on the same day. Walliams's literary agent, Independent Talent Group, dropped him on 10 November. HarperCollins confirmed that it had ended its publishing relationship with him — a significant step given his standing as a bestselling children's author. Sky, meanwhile, has dropped his film Fing! as UK distributor, and his cameo in the film is being cut. Each organisation has acted on its own position, for its own reasons. None of those decisions is a finding about the truth of the allegations, which remain untested, and none of them changes the legal position: the allegations are denied, and unproven.

THE CAREER IN THE BALANCE

To understand the scale of the moment, it helps to recall the scale of the career. Walliams rose to fame with Little Britain, the sketch show he co-created and starred in alongside Matt Lucas, which became one of the defining British comedies of its era. He went on to sit on the judging panel of Britain's Got Talent, one of the country's biggest entertainment shows, and built a parallel career as a children's author whose books became a publishing phenomenon. It is that breadth — sketch comedy, prime-time television judging and children's publishing — that explains why a single day's developments touched a public broadcaster, a talent agency, a major publisher and a film distributor at once. For readers following the wider context of how British entertainment handles cases of this kind, the contrast with a concluded court case is instructive: as BuzzDaily reported this week, Russell Brand was jailed after a trial and conviction — a separate case, decided by a jury. No such process has taken place here.

WHAT HAS NOT HAPPENED — AND WHAT HAPPENS NEXT

It is equally important to record what has not happened. No police investigation into the allegations has been reported. No criminal charges have been reported. The allegations have not been tested in court, and no court has made any finding about them. What exists, as of 11 November 2026, is a published investigation containing serious allegations, a complete denial from David Walliams, and a series of institutional decisions — by the BBC, Independent Talent Group, HarperCollins and Sky — taken while reviews are under way. BuzzDaily will report any further developments, including any statement from the authorities and any further response from Walliams, as they are confirmed by the sources listed below.
