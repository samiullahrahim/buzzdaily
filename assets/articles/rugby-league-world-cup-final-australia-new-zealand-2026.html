Australia v New Zealand: Rugby League World Cup Final Preview — Mitchell Out, Ponga v Walsh Headlines Suncorp Decider

BuzzDaily Staff · Australia Desk
· 14 November 2026 · 8 min read
Photo: Tiger Benji / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY 2.0) · Suncorp Stadium, Brisbane, under floodlights (file photo) · Free licence — full author and licence details on the Commons file page

BRISBANE — The Rugby League World Cup comes down to the fixture it was always likely to produce, in the stadium that has already given this rivalry its most famous chapter. On Sunday 15 November 2026, at 7:35pm AEDT (6:35pm Brisbane local time, 08:35 GMT), Australia's Kangaroos face New Zealand's Kiwis in the men's final at Suncorp Stadium — a record-extending 13th title on the line for Australia, a second-ever title on the line for New Zealand, and, after late team news on Saturday, a fullback duel between Kalyn Ponga and Reece Walsh headlining the decider.

MITCHELL OUT, TEDESCO IN: THE LATE MAIL

The final was reshaped 24 hours before kick-off. In the late team news published on Saturday 14 November, Latrell Mitchell was ruled out of the Australian side through illness — a significant loss of strike power on the Kangaroos' edge. James Tedesco replaces Mitchell on the interchange bench, bringing a former captain's experience into the 17, while Reece Walsh starts at fullback, keeping the tournament's most electric runner in the position where he has done his damage. New Zealand's news ran the other way. Ronaldo Mulitalo returns from a one-game suspension to take his place in the Kiwis' side, and two injury doubts were resolved in the Kiwis' favour: Naufahu Whyte (ankle) and Kodi Nikorima (leg) have both been cleared to play. On the eve of the biggest game of the tournament, Australia lost a star and New Zealand got three players back.

PONGA V WALSH: THE DUEL THAT FRAMES THE FINAL

Every final needs a headline matchup, and this one has the best available. Kalyn Ponga arrives at Suncorp having won the Golden Boot as the world's best player — the game's highest individual honour, awarded on the strength of the form he has carried into this tournament. Opposite him, in the Australian No. 1 jersey, stands Reece Walsh, who scored a hat-trick in the semi-final against Samoa and has been the Kangaroos' spark all month. Fullbacks decide modern finals: they set the attacking shape, they field the kicks under pressure, and they are usually the player with ball in hand when the decisive moment comes. Ponga's polish against Walsh's explosiveness is the contest within the contest, and it may well be the one the final turns on.

HOW THEY GOT HERE

The semi-finals confirmed what the tournament had been building towards. Australia beat Samoa 44-12, a performance in which Walsh's hat-trick grabbed the headlines and Cameron Munster was among the Kangaroos' top performers. New Zealand beat Tonga 34-10 to book their place in the decider. But the most telling result of the tournament may be its first: in the opener in Sydney on 15 October, Australia beat New Zealand 24-22. Two points, a month ago, between the same two teams. The Kiwis have had four weeks to close a two-point gap; the Kangaroos have had four weeks knowing exactly how small their margin is.

HISTORY ON THE LINE

The historical stakes are unusually balanced. Australia chase a record-extending 13th men's World Cup title and a fourth in a row, and they arrive having won their last 22 World Cup matches — a streak of dominance that now spans tournaments. New Zealand's counterweight is a single, luminous memory: their only World Cup win, a 34-20 victory over Australia in the 2008 final, was achieved at this very stadium, Suncorp, when the Kiwis produced one of the great upsets in the sport's history. Australia hold the more recent final memory — they won the 2022 final 30-10 against Samoa at Old Trafford — but New Zealand hold the Suncorp one, and on Sunday night that counts for something.

FIRST, THE WOMEN'S FINAL

Sunday at Suncorp is a double-header, and the first final is a heavyweight occasion in its own right. At 4:10pm AEDT, Australia's Jillaroos face New Zealand's Kiwi Ferns in the women's final, with the Jillaroos chasing a fourth straight World Cup title. Two finals, the same two nations, in the same stadium on the same day: the trans-Tasman rivalry will effectively be settled twice in one evening, and a crowd arriving early gets a final worthy of top billing as the warm-up act. For New Zealand, a women's win would also be the perfect destabiliser before the men's decider; for Australia, it would set up the possibility of a clean sweep on home soil.

WHAT TO WATCH ON SUNDAY NIGHT

For viewers in Britain, the men's final kicks off at 08:35 GMT — a Sunday morning appointment — while Australian and New Zealand audiences get it in prime time. Beyond the fullback duel, watch the battles the late team news created: how Australia's attack adjusts without Mitchell, whether Tedesco's introduction from the bench steadies or sparks the Kangaroos, and whether Whyte's cleared ankle holds up against the Kangaroos' pack through the middle. New Zealand's path to an upset runs through completing sets, kicking to corners and keeping Walsh's touches to a minimum — the 2008 blueprint, updated. Australia's path is the one they have walked 22 World Cup matches in a row.

Finals between these two nations rarely disappoint, and this one has everything the tournament could have asked for: the Golden Boot winner against the semi-final hat-trick hero, a dynasty on the line, an 18-year-old Suncorp ghost for New Zealand to chase, and two points of separation the last time they met. Sunday night decides it.
