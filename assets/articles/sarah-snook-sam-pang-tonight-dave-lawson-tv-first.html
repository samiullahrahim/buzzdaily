Sarah Snook's TV First: Succession Star Joins Husband Dave Lawson on Sam Pang Tonight — and Her Dorian Gray Homecoming

BuzzDaily Staff · Australia Desk
· 16 November 2026 · 8 min read
Photo: Eva Rinaldi / Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 2.0) · Sarah Snook at The Sapphires premiere, State Theatre, Sydney, in 2012 (file photo) · Free licence — full author and licence details on the Commons file page

Australian television gets a genuine first on Monday night. Sarah Snook, the Emmy-winning star of Succession, is the headline guest on Sam Pang Tonight at 8:40pm on Network 10 on Monday 16 November — and she is not coming alone. Her husband, comedian Dave Lawson, takes the guest announcer's chair for the episode, in what is being billed as a television first for the couple: husband and wife, on the same show, in two different jobs. The appearance topped Australia's entertainment search charts on Monday, ranking number one on Google Trends with searches up 1,000 per cent, and it brings one of the country's most decorated actors back to local screens in the middle of a theatrical homecoming. Cricketer Usman Khawaja completes a couch that mixes Hollywood, comedy and sport in a single hour.

A TELEVISION FIRST

The guest announcer slot is the show's signature bit of mischief — a second comic voice woven through the episode's introductions and links — and handing it to the headline guest's husband turns a standard chat-show booking into something closer to a double act. Lawson, known to Australian audiences from Utopia and a long career in local comedy, is no novelty casting: he is a working comedian walking into a format built for exactly his skills, opposite a host in Sam Pang whose deadpan is the show's engine. Snook, for her part, rarely does the couch circuit as half of a couple, which is precisely why the billing works — viewers are being offered the version of her that Australian audiences almost never get to see, at home, relaxed and playing along.

THE HOMECOMING: DORIAN GRAY

The reason Snook is in Australia at all is the stage. She is home for the Australian homecoming run of The Picture of Dorian Gray, the one-woman adaptation in which she plays every character in Oscar Wilde's story herself — a feat of stamina and quick-change artistry that has already won her both an Olivier Award in London's West End and a Tony Award on Broadway. The production's journey from Sydney to the West End to Broadway and back again is one of the great Australian theatre stories of the decade, and the homecoming dates carry the weight of a victory lap: the show that left as an ambitious local production returns with its star holding the two biggest acting prizes in world theatre for the same performance.

That context explains the scale of the television booking. Network 10 is not simply landing a famous guest; it is catching Snook at the exact moment her home country is celebrating her, with the Dorian Gray run as the backdrop and the Sam Pang appearance as the popular-culture peak of the visit. For an actor whose defining screen role — Shiv Roy in Succession — was all controlled surfaces and buried feeling, the sight of her doing Australian talk-show comedy with her husband announcing her onto the set is a deliberate change of register, and a gift to the local audience that watched her career from the beginning.

FROM SHIV ROY TO ALL HER FAULT

Snook's screen work has kept pace with the stage triumph. Her Peacock thriller series All Her Fault aired in late 2025, giving her a leading role in a glossy suburban mystery built entirely around her — further evidence that, post-Succession, she is one of the small group of actors who can carry a prestige series on her name alone. She belongs, in that respect, to the same moment as fellow Australian Nicole Kidman, whose own streaming-era run BuzzDaily recently chronicled in our report on Kidman's red-hot renaissance — two Australian actors, a generation apart, defining what television stardom looks like now.

NEXT: THE BIRDS — WITH A CAVEAT

The next screen chapter is already on the books, with an important qualification. HBO has picked up a limited-series reimagining of Alfred Hitchcock's The Birds, with Snook set to star as Myra Massey and a pilot script by Tom Spezialy. To be plain about what that means: this is a development pickup. HBO has commissioned the project into development with a pilot script — there is no series order yet, no filming date and no premiere date, and development projects do not always become finished series. What can be said is that the combination is formidable: Hitchcock's most elemental horror premise, rebuilt as a limited series, with one of the most acclaimed actors of her generation at its centre. If it proceeds, it will be an event; for now, it is a promising page in a development slate.

None of which is required viewing for Monday night. Monday night is simpler than that: a great actor, home with a triumph in her luggage, sitting down with one of Australia's sharpest comic hosts while her husband works the announcer's microphone a few feet away. Sam Pang Tonight airs at 8:40pm on Monday 16 November on Network 10 — and for once, the billing undersells it. This is not just a guest spot. It is a homecoming, televised.
