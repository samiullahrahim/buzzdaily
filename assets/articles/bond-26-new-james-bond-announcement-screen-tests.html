Bond 26: New James Bond Expected to Be Named Before Thanksgiving as Five Actors Screen-Test for 007

BuzzDaily Staff · UK Desk
· 16 November 2026 · 8 min read
Photo: Denis Villeneuve at San Diego Comic-Con in 2017. Gage Skidmore, CC BY-SA 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons.

The longest wait for a new James Bond is almost over. More than five years after Daniel Craig bowed out in No Time to Die (2021), the search for his successor has entered its final days — and according to Deadline's Justin Kroll, fans should "expect a decision before Thanksgiving", which falls on 26 November this year. The expectation of an imminent announcement is what sent "Bond 26" surging up the UK's trending searches on Monday, as the question that has hung over British cinema for half a decade finally nears an answer.

The team around the new 007 is now complete

While the casting remains officially secret, the film itself is fully staffed. Oscar-winning editor Joe Walker has confirmed he will cut Bond 26, in an interview with The Hollywood Reporter published on 13 November. Walker has edited every Denis Villeneuve film since Sicario in 2015 and won the Academy Award for Best Film Editing for Dune in 2022, so his confirmation completes a creative team that pairs Villeneuve as director with Peaky Blinders creator Steven Knight, who is writing the screenplay, and producers Amy Pascal and David Heyman.

Villeneuve was confirmed as director on 25 June 2025 and Knight that July. Villeneuve, a lifelong Bond fan, was honoured at LACMA's Art+Film Gala on 7 November, where the casting search followed him into every interview. Knight has said his story goes back to Ian Fleming's source material in a big way — fitting for a formal reboot, the 26th film in the official series and the first made under Amazon MGM's creative control, agreed with long-time custodians Barbara Broccoli and Michael G. Wilson in 2025.

Five names in the frame — all of them reports, none confirmed

Amazon MGM Studios has confirmed none of the casting stories and has declined to comment on specifics while the process runs. But the shape of the shortlist is now consistent across outlets. The BBC reported on 9 November that three actors — Jack Lowden, Josh O'Connor and Callum Turner — are the frontrunners following final screen tests, which reportedly began on 23 October and covered dialogue, camera presence and stunt work. Reports in the same week added two bigger names to the testing list: Robert Pattinson and Nicholas Hoult.

Each brings a different flavour of Bond. Lowden is best known as the disgraced spy River Cartwright in Slow Horses. O'Connor played Prince Charles in The Crown and a tennis pro in Challengers. Turner, who appeared in the Fantastic Beasts films and Masters of the Air, was publicly backed for the role by former 007 Pierce Brosnan earlier this month. Pattinson would arrive straight from The Batman; Hoult from playing Lex Luthor in Superman. Any of the five would be a plausible reboot choice — which is precisely why the studio's silence is holding.

The Turner rumour, handled with care

No name has generated more noise than Turner's. A short Showbiz411 post by Roger Friedman in early November, saying "word on the street" pointed to Turner, was treated by some websites as confirmation that he had landed the role. It was not: the item offered no sourcing beyond that phrase, and Amazon MGM has announced nothing. Men's Journal has also noted that Turner recently resigned as a director of Twentytwo Films, the production company he ran with his wife Dua Lipa — a routine-looking move that rumour pages have nonetheless folded into the speculation. Until the studio speaks, every name in this story remains a report, not a casting.

What happens next

Producer Amy Pascal said in August that the end of the year was "a good bet" for casting news; Kroll's Thanksgiving line points a few weeks earlier. Production is expected to begin in 2027 with a release in 2028, though the studio has confirmed neither date. If the film does land in 2028, the gap since No Time to Die would be the longest between Bond films in the series' history — longer even than the six years between Licence to Kill (1989) and GoldenEye (1995).

For now, the tuxedo is still unclaimed. But for the first time since Craig handed it back, the wait for the next James Bond is measured in days rather than years.
