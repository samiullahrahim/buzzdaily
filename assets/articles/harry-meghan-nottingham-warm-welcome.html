Harry and Meghan Make First Joint Appearance Since UK Return — Nottingham Gives Them a Warm Welcome

BuzzDaily Staff · Entertainment Desk
· 15 October 2026 · 7 min read
Illustration: Original artwork created for BuzzDaily

It was nearly nine years in the making. On Thursday morning, Prince Harry and Meghan Markle stepped out together in Nottingham — the very city where they made their first joint appearance as an engaged couple in 2017 — for their first public outing together since moving their family back to Britain.

The Duke and Duchess of Sussex arrived at the Community Recording Studio (CRS) shortly after 10:30 a.m., greeted by a crowd of well-wishers and schoolchildren, balloons and bouquets in hand. Meghan, dressed in an all-British-designer ensemble, leaned in to one woman in the crowd and said: "It's great to be back."

Britain's PA Media news agency reported that Meghan grew emotional as one woman handed her a large bouquet of flowers and pulled her into a hug. Harry and Meghan stopped to chat, pose for selfies and thank the people who had turned out to see them — a noticeably warmer reception than some of the pre-visit chatter had predicted.

Inside the studio, the couple met young people taking part in programs run by the charity, which teaches film, video and music skills to young people — many of them affected by violence in their communities. The charity has been close to Harry's heart for some time: he announced a £1.1 million donation to the organization last year, money that has helped fund its work with young people across Nottingham.

Meghan joined a group of girls for a confidence-building and bracelet-making workshop. When the conversation turned to what makes the girls feel most like themselves, one said she liked doing gymnastics — and Meghan lit up. "Our daughter is doing that too right now. She loves it," she said, referring to Princess Lilibet, 5.

It was a rare, unguarded glimpse into the Sussexes' family life since their return. The duchess — whose Netflix lifestyle series "With Love, Meghan" centered on hosting and cooking — added: "I feel most like myself when I'm with my family and also when I'm cooking. I love doing that. I love to cook."

The couple also watched an acting workshop led by André Squire at the studio, part of the charity's work helping young people affected by violence find their voice through the arts.

The CRS was only the first stop on a full day of engagements. The couple were then due at the Himmah Hub food bank, where Meghan has a long-standing connection — she donated £10,000 from sales of a cookbook released to raise funds in the wake of London's Grenfell Tower fire disaster, money that went to the charity which tackles food poverty. The day's schedule concluded with a visit to another youth charity linked to the couple's 2017 Nottingham visit.

Every detail of the day seemed chosen to tell a story. Meghan's outfit was head-to-toe British designers — a burgundy coat, matching turtleneck and earrings by British luxury label Joseph, a skirt by The Fold London and heels by Jennifer Chamandi, her hair pulled back into a sleek ponytail. The Joseph connection was no accident: Meghan also wore Joseph during her first official royal outing in Nottingham on December 1, 2017 — four days after the couple announced their engagement. One well-wisher reminded the couple of that visit with a throwback photo; "That's so special," Meghan replied.

The choice of Nottingham carried its own weight. This summer, the couple made a surprise announcement that stunned the royal family and the public alike: they were leaving their home in California and resettling in Britain with their two children, Prince Archie, 7, and Princess Lilibet, 5. The family landed in August, ahead of the school year, and settled in the Cotswolds. They returned, the announcement stressed, as private citizens — not resuming any official duties on behalf of King Charles III.

It was a remarkable reversal for a couple who, since stepping down from royal duties and relocating to the United States in 2020, had delivered scathing criticism of the royal institution — including claims about the family's treatment of Meghan, and Harry's 2023 memoir "Spare," in which he alleged that Prince William had physically attacked him during a 2019 confrontation. The couple remain deeply polarizing figures in Britain: opinion polls show a majority of Britons hold a negative view of them, though younger people are far more sympathetic.

The Nottingham outing had been trailed with the kind of nervous pre-publicity that says everything about the Sussexes' current standing. The Sunday Times first reported the planned outing over the weekend; a source told the paper: "Nottingham is a really special place for them and they are looking forward to getting back out there into the community, together."

But behind the scenes, according to reports, there were doubts. One source told the entertainment outlet Naughty But Nice that early talk of recreating the couple's famous 2017 walkabout — when flag-waving crowds turned out to greet the newly engaged couple — was abandoned. "The nightmare is people turning up to boo them — or nobody bothering to turn up at all. Neither is a risk anyone wants to take," the insider said. In 2017, crowds were desperate to see them; this time, the team reportedly did not want to test that affection in public.

Thursday's reality told a more encouraging story for the couple. The visit was a lower-key affair than the 2017 crowds, but the couple chatted with around 100 schoolchildren and well-wishers, joined by large numbers of media. No boos were reported — only cheers, flowers and requests for selfies.

Royal commentators, meanwhile, read the symbolism carefully. Speaking to the Express, commentator Megan Bull said the choice of Nottingham felt deliberate: "Given that it served as the backdrop for their milestone royal engagement in 2017, it feels like the couple are cultivating a very specific and deliberate narrative here." The message, she suggested, is a reset — an attempt to return to square one, setting aside the interviews, the memoir and the controversies, and to present the Sussexes as they were before it all began.

Whether that reset works will play out in the months ahead. But for one Thursday morning in Nottingham, the story was simpler: two familiar faces, a crowd that turned out to greet them, and a duchess who said it felt great to be back.
