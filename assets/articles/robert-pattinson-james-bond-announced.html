Robert Pattinson Is James Bond: Amazon MGM Names the Batman Star as the New 007

BuzzDaily Staff · UK Desk
· 17 November 2026 · 8 min read
Photo: Robert Pattinson at the Venice International Film Festival, 5 September 2026. Colleen Sturtevant, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons.

The wait is over. Amazon MGM Studios announced on Monday, 16 November, that Robert Pattinson will play James Bond, ending a search that has run for more than five years since Daniel Craig's final appearance in No Time to Die (2021). The studio unveiled the casting on the official James Bond accounts on Instagram and X with a 26-second teaser video showing Pattinson emerging as Bond to the classic 007 theme, alongside a first official photograph of the actor in a tuxedo, holding a gun. The message was short and definitive: “Robert Pattinson is James Bond.”

‘The honour of a lifetime’

In a statement released with the announcement, Pattinson said: “James Bond is one of the most legendary characters in cinema, and to be entrusted with 007 is the honour of a lifetime. I'm deeply grateful to Denis, Amy, David, Tanya, and everyone at Amazon MGM Studios for their belief in me. To say this role is a dream come true is an understatement. I couldn't be more excited about this partnership and the work ahead with this fantastic team.” The names in that thank-you list map the team making the film: director Denis Villeneuve, producers Amy Pascal and David Heyman, and executive producer Tanya Lapointe.

Villeneuve, who has just directed Pattinson as a villain in Dune: Part Three, was equally direct. “We were surrounded by extraordinary talent, but there can only be one 007. I am overjoyed to welcome Robert Pattinson as our new James Bond,” he said, in comments reported by Entertainment Weekly. Pascal and Heyman, in their own statement, said Pattinson's “fearlessness as an actor, his self-deprecating wit, and his ability to continually reinvent himself make him the perfect Bond for our times.” Reaction from inside the Bond family came quickly too: George Lazenby, 87, the oldest living actor to have played 007, publicly congratulated Pattinson on the casting, as PEOPLE reported.

How a late screen test won the role

As BuzzDaily reported yesterday in its Bond 26 screen-tests story, the final round of testing began on 23 October and covered dialogue, camera presence and stunt work. The widely reported finalists were Jack Lowden, Callum Turner and Josh O'Connor — until, in the first week of November, Pattinson and Nicholas Hoult were added to the testing list. According to reporting by CBR summarising the trade coverage, Villeneuve had wanted to see Pattinson from the beginning, but The Batman Part II limited his availability; when that production paused for two weeks, Pattinson flew out to read for the role — and, in the phrase that has run through the coverage since, usurped the frontrunner position.

The deal terms reported by Matthew Belloni in his Puck newsletter on Friday, 13 November, underlined how far the studio was prepared to go. Where the younger contenders had reportedly signed test deals covering four films, Pattinson negotiated a three-film commitment at substantially higher pay. The Guardian, citing Belloni, read those favourable terms as a sign the studio was willing to give ground to land a proven star for the first film of its Bond era. None of those deal details has been confirmed by Amazon MGM, which has announced the casting itself but not the contract behind it.

The markets moved before the studio did

The announcement landed after a weekend in which the betting markets had already made up their minds. Prediction markets tracked by Prediction.com showed Pattinson's price rising from roughly 30% to 70% in the three days after the Puck report, and by Monday morning, 16 November, bookmakers had him odds-on: Ladbrokes made him the 1/2 favourite, ahead of Lowden at 2/1 with Coral and Turner at 5/1, according to Bookies.com's market round-up published that morning. Insider Jeff Sneider had posted on Sunday, 15 November, that the announcement would arrive on Monday morning and that the name would be Pattinson — a prediction that proved correct within a day. As recently as early November, Pattinson had been available at 16/1 with some bookmakers; by the time the studio spoke, the market had long since settled.

The seventh Bond, and a very different kind of star

Pattinson, 40, becomes the seventh actor to play Bond in the official series, following Sean Connery, George Lazenby, Roger Moore, Timothy Dalton, Pierce Brosnan — who had publicly backed Callum Turner for the role earlier this month — and Daniel Craig. He is also the first Bond to arrive already fronting another major franchise: he plays Bruce Wayne in The Batman films, and PEOPLE has already noted that the 007 casting raises questions about his future in Gotham beyond The Batman Part II, due in 2028, particularly after recent changes at DC Studios. He closes 2026 with a villain role in Dune: Part Three, which opens on 18 December, and January's The Fix, at the end of a year in which he also starred in The Drama, The Odyssey and Primetime.

There is a pleasing piece of symmetry in the timing. In 2012, a 26-year-old Pattinson told interviewers he was “definitely” interested in playing Bond — but in about 20 years' time, saying he would not want to play the spy as “some young posh kid”. He has taken the role at 40, a little ahead of his own schedule, and as one of the most scrutinised actors of his generation: a performer who followed the Twilight phenomenon with a decade of demanding independent films before returning to blockbusters with The Batman.

What is — and is not — confirmed about Bond 26

The film will be the 26th in the official series and the first produced since Amazon MGM took creative control of the franchise in 2025, when Barbara Broccoli and Michael G. Wilson stepped back. Villeneuve directs from a screenplay by Peaky Blinders creator Steven Knight, with Pascal and Heyman producing and Oscar-winning editor Joe Walker — Villeneuve's editor since Sicario — confirmed to cut the film. Beyond the casting, Amazon MGM has announced nothing else: the film is untitled, no other cast members have been named, and the studio has set neither a production start nor a release date. What happens next — a title, a cast around its new 007, and a start date for filming in 2027 — is now the only Bond question left open.
