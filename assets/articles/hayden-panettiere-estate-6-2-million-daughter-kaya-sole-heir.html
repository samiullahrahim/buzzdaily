Hayden Panettiere Left a $6.2 Million Estate With No Will — Her 11-Year-Old Daughter Kaya Is the Sole Heir

BuzzDaily Staff · Entertainment Desk
· 25 November 2026 · 5 min read
Photo: Wikimedia Commons · Hayden Panettiere at a public event (file photo)

LOS ANGELES — Hayden Panettiere left an estate worth $6,232,119 and no will, according to court documents reported on 24 November 2026 — which means her entire estate passes to a single heir: her 11-year-old daughter, Kaya. The filings in Los Angeles probate court are the first detailed public accounting of what the Heroes and Nashville star left behind when she died in August, aged 36, and they set out both the scale of her fortune and the legal process now under way to settle it for her daughter’s benefit.

THE ESTATE, IN NUMBERS

The court documents put the total value of Panettiere’s estate at $6,232,119. Of that, $5,208,519 is in personal property — the category that covers money, investments and belongings — and $1,023,600 is in real property. Because Panettiere died without a will, she died intestate in legal terms, and under the applicable rules her estate passes to her closest heir. Panettiere had one child, Kaya, who is 11 years old and is therefore the sole heir to the full estate. A sole heir who is a minor cannot simply receive and manage a fortune of this size directly, which is why the probate process — the court-supervised settling of the estate — and the question of who administers it have become the immediate legal story.

KLITSCHKO ASKS THE COURT TO APPOINT AN ADMINISTRATOR

Kaya’s father is Wladimir Klitschko, Panettiere’s former fiancé. On Wednesday, 18 November 2026, Klitschko filed a petition in Los Angeles probate court asking for Melissa Moskal Bencivenga, a tax and financial adviser, to be appointed administrator of the estate. An administrator’s role is to gather and value the estate’s assets, settle any debts and taxes, and manage the transfer of what remains to the heir under the court’s supervision. Klitschko’s involvement in the arrangements for Kaya’s inheritance is already established: in October he was granted temporary guardianship of Kaya’s estate, the legal mechanism that allows a responsible adult to protect and manage a minor’s financial interests while the longer-term arrangements are decided. The petition to appoint a professional administrator is the next step in that process, and the figures in the court documents — reported this week — come from the same proceedings.

THE OFFICIAL RULING: AN ACCIDENTAL OVERDOSE

Panettiere died on 16 August 2026, aged 36, in Greenville, South Carolina. The coroner ruled her death an accidental overdose, caused by the toxic effects of fentanyl, 4-ANPP, alprazolam, methocarbamol and quetiapine. Greenville police investigated the circumstances of her death and closed their investigation on 28 October 2026, with no charges filed against anyone. Those two findings — the coroner’s ruling of an accidental overdose and the police decision to close the investigation without charges — are the official record of how Panettiere died, and they are the findings against which any other account of her death has to be measured.

HER MOTHER’S CLAIM — AND WHAT IS NOT ESTABLISHED

Panettiere’s mother, Lesley Vogel, has publicly offered a different view of responsibility for her daughter’s death. In an Inside Edition interview aired on Monday, 16 November 2026, Vogel said she blames Panettiere’s former boyfriend, Brian Hickerson, for her daughter’s death, and she also explained why she did not attend the funeral. That is Vogel’s claim, made as a grieving parent in a television interview, and BuzzDaily reports it as exactly that — a claim. It is not the finding of the coroner, who ruled the death an accidental overdose; it is not the conclusion of the Greenville police, who closed their investigation on 28 October with no charges filed; and Hickerson has not been charged in connection with Panettiere’s death. Readers should hold the two things separately: a mother’s stated belief about her daughter’s final years, and the official rulings that stand as the established record.

A CAREER THAT STARTED YOUNG

Panettiere had been famous for most of her life by the time of her death. She broke through worldwide as Claire Bennet, the indestructible cheerleader at the centre of Heroes, and later starred as Juliette Barnes in the country-music drama Nashville, a role that made her a recording artist as well as an actor. In May 2026 she published a memoir, This Is Me: A Reckoning, an account of a career that began in childhood and of the personal struggles that ran alongside it. Her death was the second loss of a child for her family in three years: her younger brother, Jansen Panettiere, died in 2023, aged 28. The probate filings now under way in Los Angeles are, in the end, about the person she leaves behind — an 11-year-old daughter who is her sole heir, and for whose benefit the $6.2 million estate her mother built across a lifetime in the public eye will now be administered.
