Jennifer Lawrence and Josh Hutcherson Reunite at the Sunrise on the Reaping Premiere, 14 Years After Their Hunger Games Debut

BuzzDaily Staff · Australia Desk
· 17 November 2026 · 7 min read
Photo: Josh Hutcherson, Jennifer Lawrence and Liam Hemsworth at San Diego Comic-Con, 9 July 2015. Gage Skidmore, CC BY-SA 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons.

Real or not real? On Monday night, 16 November, it was very real. Jennifer Lawrence and Josh Hutcherson walked the red carpet together at the New York premiere of The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping, 14 years after the first Hunger Games film made them two of the most famous young actors on the planet — and they did not come alone. Woody Harrelson and Elizabeth Banks, their co-stars from the original four films, joined them at Lincoln Center for the launch of the prequel in which all four return to Panem.

The date carried its own echo. Eleven years earlier to the day, on 16 November 2015, Lawrence, Hutcherson and Liam Hemsworth had walked a premiere carpet together for The Hunger Games: Mockingjay – Part 2 in Los Angeles, closing the original run of films: The Hunger Games (2012), Catching Fire (2013) and the two Mockingjay films (2014 and 2015). Monday's reunion was the first time the core cast has reassembled in public for the franchise since — this time to hand the story to a new generation while appearing in it themselves.

Who returns, and how

Sunrise on the Reaping is set 24 years before Katniss Everdeen volunteered as tribute, and its story belongs to a teenage Haymitch Abernathy, played by Joseph Zada, competing in the 50th Hunger Games — the Second Quarter Quell, which doubled the number of tributes sent into the arena. Suzanne Collins' novel, published on 18 March 2025, ends with a flash-forward epilogue, and the film follows it: Lawrence, 36, returns as Katniss and Hutcherson, 34, as Peeta Mellark for that closing sequence, as Variety first reported last December and Lawrence confirmed on the record last month. Harrelson, 65, returns as Haymitch in the same framing, and Banks, 52, reprises Effie Trinket — while the younger versions of their characters are played by Zada and by Elle Fanning, who takes on the young Effie.

The new cast around them is stacked: Whitney Peak, Mckenna Grace — playing twins Merrilee and Maysilee Donner, the latter a District 12 tribute — Ralph Fiennes as President Snow, Jesse Plemons as Plutarch Heavensbee, Kieran Culkin as Caesar Flickerman, and Glenn Close, Maya Hawke, Kelvin Harrison Jr. and Ben Wang. Francis Lawrence, who directed three of the four original films, returns behind the camera, from a screenplay by Billy Ray and Michael Lesslie. The film runs 158 minutes and opens in cinemas worldwide on Friday, 20 November.

‘We didn't walk to the bathroom without our arms around each other’

Lawrence described the return on the 28 October episode of the Call Her Daddy podcast, in comments PEOPLE has returned to throughout the premiere coverage. Rejoining the franchise was, she said, “unbelievable”. “It was so emotional. Josh and I didn't walk to the bathroom without our arms around each other. It was so beautiful,” she said. “It was so crazy because it was the same crew. It was the same camera guys, the same AD who I've worked with a million times too because I love him, and Josh and Woody.” The trio's dynamic, she added, had not changed in 11 years: “Woody obviously came through the window of my hotel room uninvited because it would not be the same if he listened to boundaries and used a door.”

The handover is already happening on set as well as on the carpet. Grace told PEOPLE that while filming her own arena ordeal she reached out to Hutcherson — her Five Nights at Freddy's 2 co-star — to ask whether his Games had felt as physically taxing and emotional. “He was like, ‘Yeah. Yeah, no, they're all like that. It's definitely hard,’” she recalled, before admitting that as “the resident kind of fangirl on this project” she was at least as interested in his stories from the original films as in his advice. Sam Claflin, who played Finnick Odair in the originals and does not appear in the prequel, told CBR last week he was delighted to see the band back together for what he called a “devastating” instalment.

Why this premiere matters for the franchise

Sunrise on the Reaping is the second prequel in the series, following 2023's The Ballad of Songbirds & Snakes, but the first to bring the original leads back on screen — a bridge between the franchise's past and its future that Lionsgate has been building towards since Collins announced the novel. The soundtrack has been part of that build: as BuzzDaily reported last month, the film's companion album, led by Gigi Perez's “Favor”, gathers 29 artists under producer Dave Cobb. Monday night's carpet — original stars out in force, four days before release — was the clearest signal yet of how the studio is positioning the film: not as a spin-off, but as a homecoming. The film opens on Friday, 20 November, and the first verdicts from the premiere audience will follow this week.
