PJ Gallagher Dies Aged 51: Tributes Pour In for the Naked Camera Star and The Young Offenders' Principal Barry Walsh

BuzzDaily Staff · UK Desk
· 28 November 2026 · 6 min read
Photo: Sinn Féin / CC BY 2.0 via Wikimedia Commons · PJ Gallagher in 2014 (file photo)

DUBLIN — PJ Gallagher, the Irish comedian, actor and radio presenter who co-created the hidden-camera series Naked Camera and played Principal Barry Walsh in The Young Offenders, has died aged 51. His agency, the Lisa Richards Agency, confirmed his death on Friday evening, 27 November 2026, saying his family were devastated. He died in Dublin, the city where he co-hosted the Radio Nova breakfast show, Morning Glory, with Jim McCabe — and within hours, tributes were being led by the Taoiseach and echoed across Irish and British broadcasting.

THE ANNOUNCEMENT

The Lisa Richards Agency announced Gallagher's death in a statement on social media on Friday evening. His family and friends were said to be devastated, and the agency asked for privacy. Radio Nova, where Gallagher had presented breakfast since 2021, posted its own tribute: “We're so sad and we still can't quite believe the loss of our friend and colleague. PJ will be so dearly missed by all who had the privilege to cross his path. Rest in peace, our dear friend, PJ.”

Bay Broadcasting, the group behind Radio Nova and Ireland's Classic Hits Radio, described Gallagher as a “treasured friend whose humour, warmth, kindness and wonderfully individual outlook on life enriched the lives of those fortunate enough to work alongside him”. Its statement ended: “The airwaves will be a little quieter, our mornings a little less bright, and our radio family will never quite be the same without him.” No cause of death has been announced.

TRIBUTES FROM THE TOP

Taoiseach Micheál Martin led the political tributes, saying Gallagher “brought immense warmth and laughter to so many across Ireland”. Tánaiste Simon Harris called him “a true treasure of Irish broadcasting and comedy” and described his passing as “a devastating loss to Irish broadcasting and comedy, and to the countless people whose lives he touched”. Harris added: “PJ had that rare and wonderful ability to make people feel as though they knew him personally. His infectious humour, natural warmth and extraordinary connection with his listeners made him a much-loved part of people's daily lives.”

From the BBC, Chris Sussman, director of BBC Comedy, said: “We are saddened to hear of the passing of PJ Gallagher. He will be fondly remembered for his role in The Young Offenders. Our thoughts are with his family and loved ones.” Harris also sent his sympathies to Gallagher's partner, Kelly, his young twins, Milo and Stevie, his wider family, and all his friends and colleagues.

NAKED CAMERA: THE SHOW THAT MADE HIM

Gallagher rose to fame as presenter and co-creator of Naked Camera, RTÉ's hidden-camera series, which ran from 2005 to 2007. In it he played a string of comedy characters — among them the ladies' man Jake Stevens, a “Dirty Auld One” and a mentally unstable taxi driver — accosting an unsuspecting Irish public. The show made him one of the most recognisable faces in Irish comedy while he was still building his stand-up career, and its characters remained the ones strangers quoted back at him for the rest of his life.

PRINCIPAL BARRY WALSH

To audiences in Britain, Gallagher was Principal Barry Walsh. The Young Offenders began as a 2016 film, in which he played Barry, before becoming a television series on BBC Three and RTÉ2. Gallagher played the put-upon school principal across the series' run from 2018 to 2026 — 20 episodes in all — becoming, for a generation of viewers who had never seen Naked Camera, the face of exasperated authority against the show's two young leads. It is the role the BBC singled out in its tribute, and the one that carried his name furthest beyond Ireland.

MORNING GLORY: THE BREAKFAST YEARS

Gallagher and Jim McCabe formed one of Irish radio's longest-running breakfast partnerships. They presented together first on 4FM, later Ireland's Classic Hits Radio, until April 2021, when the pair moved to Dublin's Radio Nova to launch Morning Glory on 1 April 2021. The show aired from 6am to 10am on weekdays and won gold for Breakfast Programme at the 2024 IMRO Radio Awards. Bay Broadcasting recalled: “For so many listeners, PJ and Jim were more than voices on the radio; they were friends who shared their mornings, brightened their days and made the everyday a little brighter.” Gallagher also co-hosted the Dubland podcast with Suzanne Kane from 2015 until his death.

MADHOUSE AND AN HONEST CONVERSATION

In 2023 Gallagher published his memoir, Madhouse. He had long spoken openly about his mental health, including a three-month stay in St Patrick's hospital, and both Harris and Bay Broadcasting praised that candour in their tributes. Harris said: “Beyond his talents as a comedian and broadcaster, PJ showed remarkable courage in speaking so openly about his own mental health struggles. He was brave, articulate and supremely altruistic in sharing his deepest experiences to try to shine a light that could help others.” Bay Broadcasting said his willingness to speak candidly “helped break down barriers, encouraged important conversations and gave countless people the courage to speak about their own struggles”.

FAMILY AND LEGACY

Gallagher was born Paul Joseph Gallagher in Cork on 18 April 1975 and was adopted by Seán and Helen Gallagher. He met his partner, Kelly Doolin, at a Pride march in June 2022, and their twins, Milo and Stevie, were born in August 2023. The scale of the reaction to his death reached well beyond Ireland: searches for Gallagher topped the entertainment chart on Google Trends in the UK (No. 1, 50K+, up more than 1,000 per cent) and in Canada, and charted in New Zealand (No. 2), Australia (No. 3) and the United States (No. 4) — a measure of how far a career built on Irish radio, hidden cameras and one long-suffering school principal had travelled.
