Kim Kardashian Reveals Doctors Found Irregularities on Her Esophagus That May Need Cutting Out in Emotional Health Update

BuzzDaily Staff · Entertainment Desk
· 29 October 2026 · 7 min read
Photo: Eva Rinaldi · CC BY-SA 2.0 · via Wikimedia Commons · Kim Kardashian on the red carpet in Sydney, Australia, in 2012 · Free licence — full author and licence details on the Commons file page (file photo)

LOS ANGELES — Kim Kardashian has revealed that she has been diagnosed with esophagitis, after doctors found inflammation and “irregularities” on her esophagus that may need to be cut out. The health update, first teased in the trailer for season eight of Hulu’s The Kardashians — “I have something called esophagitis” — unfolds in full in the episode released on Thursday, 29 October, and it arrives with an emotional charge that has little to do with television. Kardashian’s father, Robert Kardashian Sr., died of esophageal cancer in 2003, and she makes clear that anything involving her esophagus carries that loss with it.

The reveal is striking not only for what Kardashian says, but for how long she sat on it. She explains in the episode that she kept the diagnosis private at first — a notable decision for a woman whose career is built on sharing nearly everything — because, in her words, it “hits home.” By the time viewers hear the diagnosis, the show has already shown them the days that led up to it: Kardashian at work, unwell, and pushing through a film shoot without knowing what was wrong.

A SCARE THAT BUILT ON SET

Much of the episode follows Kardashian on the set of The Fifth Wheel, the Netflix comedy she is currently filming. She is visibly struggling: fatigued, nursing a headache, and dealing with pain she cannot shake. “My throat is, like, killing me today,” she tells those around her — the kind of throwaway line that, in retrospect, reads as the first signpost of the storyline to come. Reality television is edited with hindsight, and the producers know exactly where this is heading. But the discomfort on screen does not look performed; Kardashian is working a full production day while feeling genuinely unwell.

The answer arrives later, and not in a doctor’s office. Kardashian receives the diagnosis on a Zoom call, learning that tests have found inflammation on her esophagus — and that the examination has also turned up irregularities that will need closer attention. It is a distinctly modern way to receive frightening news: on a screen, between other commitments, with the cameras capturing her reaction in real time.

WHAT THE DOCTOR FOUND

According to Kardashian’s account in the episode, her doctor told her there is inflammation and “irregularities” on her esophagus that may need to be cut out. The suggested path forward is medication first, together with a follow-up procedure so doctors can look more closely at what they are dealing with. No further definitive diagnosis is offered in the episode, and Kardashian does not speculate about one — a restraint worth noting, because the word “irregularities” does a great deal of work in this storyline, and the show leaves it as the open question it genuinely is.

WHY IT HITS HOME

For most patients, esophagitis is a treatable inflammation. For Kardashian, the word “esophagus” can never be only medical. Her father, Robert Kardashian Sr. — the attorney who first brought the family name to national attention — died of esophageal cancer in 2003, aged 59, when Kardashian was in her early twenties. The speed and severity of his illness have been a touchstone for the family ever since, and it is the reason, she explains, that she initially kept this diagnosis to herself.

When she does share the news with her mother, the reaction says everything about the family history underneath it. Kris Jenner’s response is immediate and unguarded: “I can’t, it makes me want to cry.” There is no branding in that moment and no punchline — just a mother hearing the name of the organ that took her first husband, attached now to her daughter’s medical chart.

WHAT ESOPHAGITIS IS — AND WHAT IT ISN’T

Esophagitis simply means inflammation of the esophagus, the tube that carries food from the throat to the stomach. HealthDigest, drawing on NBC News medical reporting, notes the context offered by specialists such as Dr. Stuart Spechler: the most common form, reflux esophagitis, develops when stomach acid repeatedly washes back up into the esophagus, irritating and inflaming its lining. It is common, it is usually manageable with medication and follow-up monitoring, and it is not the same thing as esophageal cancer — an important distinction, and one that coverage of Kardashian’s reveal has been careful to preserve.

That distinction cuts both ways. Inflamed or irregular tissue is precisely what follow-up procedures exist to examine, and doctors caring for any patient with Kardashian’s family history would be expected to look carefully and act cautiously. The medication-and-monitoring plan described in the episode is the standard, measured response: treat the inflammation, re-examine the area, and decide on next steps based on what is actually found rather than on what anyone fears.

A FAMILY’S LONG CONNECTION TO THIS DISEASE

The Kardashian family’s connection to esophageal health did not begin with this diagnosis. In 2019, the family helped open the UCLA Robert G. Kardashian Center for Esophageal Health, a facility named for their father that supports research, treatment and awareness around conditions of the esophagus. At the time, it was a philanthropic footnote to the family story. This autumn it reads differently: the centre exists because of what esophageal disease did to this family, and the diagnosis now arriving for its most famous member involves exactly the part of the body the centre was built to study.

A SECOND HEALTH SCARE IN A YEAR

Remarkably, this is not Kardashian’s first on-screen health fright of the past year. A year earlier, she revealed that doctors had found a small brain aneurysm on a scan — another diagnosis absorbed in front of the cameras, another round of monitoring and unanswered questions. Two significant findings in roughly twelve months would unsettle anyone. That both became storylines on her own show is a choice Kardashian continues to make, and it raises the question of what that openness is for: part disclosure, part control of the narrative, and — judging by the reaction each time — part something her audience clearly relates to.

WHAT HAPPENS NEXT

The immediate medical picture, as presented in the episode, is medication and a follow-up procedure. But the preview for the next episode points further, suggesting that Kardashian may need surgery. Previews are designed to hold an audience, and nothing in the current episode confirms an operation — the possibility is simply now part of the story viewers will follow into next week, and whether the irregularities will indeed need to be cut out remains, for now, unanswered.

Analysis — there is a version of celebrity health coverage that treats every diagnosis as content, and The Kardashians has never entirely escaped that charge. This storyline is harder to dismiss. It involves the disease that killed the family patriarch before the fame arrived, a diagnosis his daughter initially could not bring herself to say out loud, and a mother whose one-line reaction carries more than two decades of grief. Whatever the follow-up procedure finds, Kardashian has turned a private fright into a public reminder of the simple point her family’s UCLA centre was built on: symptoms in the esophagus are worth taking seriously, and taking early.
