Nicole Kidman's Red-Hot Renaissance at 59: Lioness, Practical Magic 2 and a Hollywood Fight She Is Refusing to Lose

BuzzDaily Staff · Entertainment Desk
· 24 October 2026 · 8 min read
Photo: Georges Biard · CC BY-SA 3.0 · via Wikimedia Commons

LOS ANGELES — Hollywood loves a comeback, but what Nicole Kidman is having at 59 is something rarer: a renaissance with no apology attached. Nearly a year after her shock split from husband Keith Urban stunned the entertainment world, the Oscar winner is not retreating from the spotlight — she is occupying more of it than ever, across television, film and fashion at the same time.

Page Six, in a profile published today, calls it a “red-hot renaissance” — and the workload backs the description up. Kidman stars in the third season of “Lioness,” Taylor Sheridan's Paramount+ hit, she is filming “Discretion,” a new thriller for the same streamer, and she will reprise her role in HBO's “Nine Perfect Strangers.” On top of the acting slate, she remains the face of Chanel — the kind of multi-front presence usually associated with stars half her age.

The fashion world saw the proof earlier this month in Paris, where Kidman appeared at the Chanel show in little more than an oversized blazer, legs on display, as photographers' flashes fired around her. She brought her youngest daughter, Faith Margaret, along to the show — a front-row appearance that Page Six framed as equal parts glamour and statement: poise, continuity, and a very public refusal to hide.

THE FIGHT OVER ‘PRACTICAL MAGIC 2’

But the most striking part of the Page Six report is not the schedule — it is the fight. Kidman's latest film is “Practical Magic 2,” in which she stars alongside Sandra Bullock, and according to the report she is pushing back against what she sees as deeply unfair criticism of how the film's opening was covered by the industry trades.

“All of the trades [magazines] slated it because it opened at $30 million,” a Tinseltown source told Page Six. “But the [following] week, Brad Pitt's movie, ‘Heart of the Beast,’ opened with $20 million and they were celebrating it as a great opening for him.”

To be fair — and Page Six makes the point itself — Pitt's film has since moved ahead in the reported totals, grossing $156 million worldwide compared with $105 million for “Practical Magic 2.” But the argument was never really about the final tally. It was about the first-weekend verdicts: how a $30 million opening for a film led by two women was written up as a disappointment, while a smaller opening for a male contemporary was celebrated as a success.

The comparison cuts deeper still. Kidman's ex-husband Tom Cruise saw his new film “Digger” open to just $8 million on its opening weekend on October 2 — a fraction of the “Practical Magic 2” debut — and the industry's response, as BuzzDaily has reported, was to rally behind the film rather than bury it. Three films, three openings, three very different receptions.

A DOUBLE STANDARD HOLLYWOOD STILL WON'T NAME

The source's wider point, as Page Six reports it, is that Hollywood still struggles to take women-fronted projects — and the female audiences who turn out for them — seriously. Those audiences are among the most loyal in the business: they show up on opening weekend, they come back, and they carry films through word of mouth long after the opening-frame headlines have moved on. Yet the industry's first draft of a film's story is still too often written as if their money counts for less.

Kidman, by this account, is pushing back — not with a single statement, but with a slate. Every project on her current list is a bet on the proposition that a woman approaching 60 can be the commercial center of a hit series, a fashion house and a cultural conversation all at once.

FOUR DECADES OF REFUSING TO DISAPPEAR

None of this should surprise anyone who has followed the whole career. Kidman broke through in Australian film in the late 1980s, crossed over to Hollywood with “Days of Thunder” in 1990, and spent the following decades alternating blockbusters with riskier auteur work. She won the Academy Award for Best Actress for “The Hours,” shape-shifted through “Moulin Rouge!,” “The Others” and “Cold Mountain,” and then — at the age when Hollywood traditionally begins writing actresses out — moved into prestige television as both star and producer, headlining “Big Little Lies” and building a second career championing female-led stories.

That longevity is the context the opening-weekend numbers miss. Kidman has been a top-of-the-call-sheet star across five different decades, through the studio era, the indie era, the franchise era and the streaming era. Reinvention was never a phase for her; it has been the operating system of the entire career.

Which is why the “Practical Magic 2” argument matters beyond one film's grosses. If a $30 million opening can be waved away when the stars are women nearing 60 — even as those same stars front hit series, command the fashion front rows and deliver nine-figure reported worldwide totals — then the problem is not the audience. The audience showed up. The problem is a scoreboard whose goalposts keep moving. Kidman's answer, characteristically, is not a press release. It is another season, another film, another front row — a red-hot renaissance that shows no sign of asking anyone's permission.
