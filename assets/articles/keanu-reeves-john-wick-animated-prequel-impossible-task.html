Keanu Reeves Officially Returns as John Wick in Animated Prequel About the Impossible Task

BuzzDaily Staff · US Desk
· 6 November 2026 · 7 min read
Photo: Microsoft Corporation · CC BY-SA 4.0 · via Wikimedia Commons · Keanu Reeves on stage at the Xbox E3 briefing in Los Angeles, June 2019 · Free licence — full author and licence details on the Commons file page (file photo)

LOS ANGELES — The most famous story in the John Wick films is the one audiences have never seen. That is about to change. Lionsgate has officially greenlit an animated John Wick prequel film, and Keanu Reeves has closed his deal to return and voice the assassin — a confirmation the studio made on Thursday 5 November, following its quarterly earnings report, as Deadline first reported.

Lionsgate CEO Jon Feltheimer said the project is already in preproduction. The announcement converts one of the studio’s most talked-about reveals of last year into an active production, and it locks in the one piece of casting the film could not work without. An animated John Wick without Reeves’s voice would be a curiosity; with it, the prequel becomes a central chapter of a franchise that has grown from a lean 2014 revenge thriller into a billion-dollar property — and the films’ most retold legend finally gets told in full.

THE IMPOSSIBLE TASK, AT LAST

The film is set before the events of the first John Wick movie, and it tells the story fans have heard about since 2014: the Impossible Task. In the mythology of the films, Wick was set a job considered unsurvivable — to eliminate all of his rivals in a single night. Completing it freed him from his obligation to the High Table, the council that governs the franchise’s assassin underworld, and earned him the right to retire from the life and be with his wife, Helen. The Task is the foundation of Wick’s legend: other characters speak of it the way soldiers speak of a famous battle. But across four films it has only ever been described, never shown — a gap this film exists to fill.

THE TEAM BEHIND THE FILM

Directing is Shannon Tindle, who wrote and directed Netflix’s Ultraman: Rising and whose credits include Kubo and the Two Strings and the Emmy-winning Lost Ollie. The screenplay is by Ryan and Kaz Firpo, the cousins who developed the screen story and an early draft of Marvel’s Eternals. Producing are the franchise’s core team — Reeves himself; Chad Stahelski, director of the live-action films, through 87Eleven Entertainment; and Thunder Road’s Basil Iwanyk and Erica Lee — joined by Point Grey, the company founded by Seth Rogen and Evan Goldberg, whose animation credits include Sausage Party and Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles: Mutant Mayhem, and which brings deep adult-animation experience to a series built on live-action stunt work.

UNVEILED AT CINEMACON — GREENLIT NOW

The animated prequel was first unveiled at CinemaCon in April 2025, where Lionsgate presented it alongside John Wick 5, which is also in development. At that announcement, Adam Fogelson, chair of the Lionsgate Motion Picture Group, made the case for both the format and the story: “In both animation and the world of John Wick, the possibilities are endless. And there is no John Wick story fans are clamoring for more than the Impossible Task.” At the time, the project was an intention on a slate. Thursday’s greenlight — with Feltheimer confirming preproduction is under way and Reeves’s deal closed — makes it a production with a director, a script and its star attached.

A UNIVERSE STILL EXPANDING

The animated prequel is one strand of a wider expansion. John Wick 5 remains in development, and at the same CinemaCon presentation Lionsgate also announced a spin-off centred on Donnie Yen’s character Caine — evidence that the studio treats the Wick universe, described by Animation World Network as a billion-dollar franchise, as a long-term asset rather than a finished story. Animation lets Lionsgate grow that universe in a new direction without waiting for the fifth chapter to be ready, and the property is unusually well suited to it: its mythology has always been larger than its screen time, a whole underworld of hotels, councils and codes glimpsed at the edges of the live-action films. A drawn prequel about its founding legend is a logical next step.

WHY ANIMATION SUITS THIS STORY

Analysis — The Impossible Task has resisted filming for a decade for practical as much as creative reasons: it is, by design, one man against every rival he has, in a single night, at a scale the live-action films describe in almost mythic terms. Animation removes the physical ceiling on that premise. It also sidesteps the chronology problem that stops most prequels — a younger Wick can be drawn, while Reeves’s voice keeps the character continuous with the films audiences know. Hiring Tindle, a director associated with emotionally grounded, visually ambitious animation, suggests Lionsgate wants more from the format than a novelty spin-off.

WHAT IS STILL UNKNOWN

Lionsgate has announced no release date, and the film remains untitled. No other voice casting has been confirmed — including who might play Helen, or whether familiar figures from the live-action films will appear in their younger days. Those answers will come as preproduction proceeds. The confirmation caps a notable week for film casting and franchise news: in Australia, Daniel MacPherson has joined Priyanka Chopra Jonas and Russell Crowe in the sci-fi thriller Bluefly, which is about to film on the Gold Coast. For John Wick fans, though, the essentials are settled: the Impossible Task is being made, in animation, with the only John Wick there has ever been at its centre.
