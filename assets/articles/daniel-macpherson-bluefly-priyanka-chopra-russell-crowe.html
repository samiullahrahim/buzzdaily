Daniel MacPherson Joins Priyanka Chopra and Russell Crowe in Sci-Fi Thriller Bluefly — Filming on Australia’s Gold Coast

BuzzDaily Staff · Australia Desk
· 6 November 2026 · 7 min read
Photo: Shruti Jha · CC BY-SA 4.0 · via Wikimedia Commons · Aerial panorama of Surfers Paradise on Australia’s Gold Coast, Queensland · Free licence — full author and licence details on the Commons file page (file photo)

GOLD COAST — Daniel MacPherson is going back to work with Russell Crowe. The Australian actor has joined the cast of Bluefly, the science-fiction action thriller starring Priyanka Chopra Jonas and Crowe, in a casting move reported on Friday 6 November by Cinema Express, citing a Variety report, and covered the same day by The Industry and The Digginator.

MacPherson will play Dugan, a Special Forces soldier hardened by years of combat. His signing is the film’s newest headline rather than a footnote: Bluefly itself was first announced back in August 2026, with Chopra Jonas and Crowe attached and cameras expected on Australia’s Gold Coast. What is new today is MacPherson — and with him, the clearest picture yet of the team the film will send into the jungle.

A MISSION INTO THE CONGO

Bluefly is a science-fiction action thriller set in the Congo. Chopra Jonas plays a United Nations translator who is grieving a personal loss; Crowe plays a troubled former pilot. Together — now with MacPherson’s Dugan alongside them — they are drawn into a secret operation to retrieve a shot-down aircraft deep in the Congo. Beyond that outline, the production is keeping its details close: what the aircraft is, why it was downed and why it must be recovered remain undisclosed in the verified reporting, which describes the mission as covert and the aircraft at its centre as mysterious. It is also a notable piece of construction: the person at the centre of a military retrieval story is not a soldier but a translator — a civilian whose skill is language — flanked by a pilot carrying troubles of his own and, now, a professional soldier. That triangle gives the film three very different relationships to the same mission before a single scene has been shot.

THE FILMMAKERS

Directing Bluefly is Nimród Antal, the filmmaker best known for Predators, the 2010 entry in the Predator series — a film that, like this one, dropped a group of hardened fighters into hostile jungle territory. The screenplay is by David Frigerio, Carlyle Eubank and William Eubank. The production also marks the first on-screen collaboration between Chopra Jonas and Crowe: two of the most recognisable screen performers in the world, whose careers have run in parallel for years without intersecting until now. Adding MacPherson — a familiar face to Australian audiences and a veteran of action roles — gives the ensemble a third, distinctly physical presence alongside them.

A BEAST REUNION

For MacPherson, Bluefly is a reunion on two fronts. He recently starred opposite Crowe in Beast, the 2026 mixed martial arts drama, and Beast was written by Frigerio — who is now one of Bluefly’s screenwriters. Casting that grows out of a successful recent collaboration is a familiar pattern in the industry, and it hands Antal a ready-made working relationship at the centre of his ensemble: his newest cast member arrives already knowing both his leading man and one of his writers, on a production that will ask its cast to carry long stretches of a Congo-set story far from the Congo itself.

WHAT MACPHERSON BRINGS

Casting announcements usually trade in names; this one also trades in fit. Dugan is written as a man defined by combat experience, and MacPherson has built his career on physically demanding roles, most recently opposite Crowe in the fighting world of Beast. He steps into Bluefly as the ensemble’s soldier — the character most at home inside the operation, set against a grieving civilian and a pilot described as troubled. If the film delivers on its premise, much of its tension will live in that contrast: three people carrying three different kinds of damage, sent into the Congo to recover a downed aircraft whose full story has not yet been told.

THE GOLD COAST STANDS IN FOR THE CONGO

Production is expected to begin shortly on Australia’s Gold Coast, which will stand in for the Congo-set portions of the story. The arrangement pairs one of the world’s most recognisable coastlines with one of its least filmed settings: the beaches and hinterland of Queensland will double for Central Africa while the cameras roll close to home for MacPherson and Crowe. It also continues the Gold Coast’s run as a base for large international productions — the kind of shoot that brings a global cast, led by Chopra Jonas in her first film opposite Crowe, to Australian shores.

WHAT HAPPENS NEXT

No theatrical release date has been announced, and no direct comment from MacPherson, Chopra Jonas or Crowe accompanied the casting reports — the news arrived as a casting announcement, not a press conference. Further casting and production details are expected as filming gets under way on the Gold Coast. The signing rounds out a busy week for major screen-casting news: in Hollywood, Keanu Reeves has been officially confirmed to return as John Wick in an animated prequel about the Impossible Task. For Bluefly, the next headline will likely come when the cameras start rolling — and a corner of Queensland becomes the Congo.
