Brad Pitt and Angelina Jolie Settle Bitter Château Miraval Winery Battle — a Decade After Their Split

BuzzDaily Staff · Entertainment Desk
· 26 November 2026 · 6 min read
Photo: Harald Krichel / CC BY-SA 4.0 via Wikimedia Commons · Brad Pitt at the Venice Film Festival, 2019 (file photo)

LOS ANGELES — Brad Pitt and Angelina Jolie have settled their years-long legal battle over Château Miraval, the French winery they once co-owned, bringing one of Hollywood’s most bitter post-divorce disputes to a quiet end a decade after the couple split. The settlement was announced on Wednesday, 25 November 2026, in a joint statement issued by representatives for both actors. “We have settled out of court in the best interests of our family and will not be commenting further,” the statement said. The terms of the settlement have not been disclosed, and neither side offered any detail beyond those agreed words.

THE ESTATE WHERE THEY MARRIED

Château Miraval, in the south of France, was never just an investment. Pitt, now 62, and Jolie, now 51, bought a controlling interest in the estate in 2008, and in 2014 they married there, in front of their children. Under their ownership the winery became a serious commercial success, its rosé among the most recognisable celebrity wines in the world. For years, Miraval stood in the public imagination for the couple’s life together — which is precisely why, after their split in 2016, it became the asset they could not divide. Their divorce was not finalised until December 2024, eight years after the separation, an extraordinary length of time even by the standards of contested Hollywood divorces. The winery question outlasted even that.

THE SALE THAT STARTED THE FIGHT

The winery dispute began in 2021, when Jolie sold her 50 per cent stake in Miraval to Tenute del Mondo, the wine division of the Stoli Group, the drinks business associated with Yuri Shefler. Pitt responded in February 2022 by suing, arguing that Jolie had broken an agreement between them that he would buy her half. Jolie’s side contested that account, and the case ground forward for nearly four years, generating the kind of detailed, hostile filings that had characterised the couple’s wider legal separation. It was, by any measure, a remarkable afterlife for a marriage: two of the most famous actors in the world, a decade on from their split, still litigating a vineyard in the south of France.

The winery litigation also ran alongside the couple’s divorce proceedings, in which filings submitted on Jolie’s behalf contained allegations about Pitt’s behaviour during the marriage. Those claims were allegations made in court papers — contested within the litigation and never established as fact — and BuzzDaily reports them only in those terms. The settlement announced this week resolves the winery dispute between Pitt and Jolie themselves. It does not retrospectively settle anything that was claimed, on either side, in filings that will now never be tested at the trial that had been scheduled.

WHAT THE SETTLEMENT DOES — AND DOES NOT — END

What has ended is the direct fight between the former spouses over Miraval. The case had been scheduled for trial beginning 4 August 2027, a date that would have returned both actors to a courtroom more than a decade after their separation; that trial will now not happen. What has not ended, on the available reporting, is Pitt’s separate litigation against the Stoli Group, which is understood to be ongoing. The distinction matters. Jolie’s exit from the dispute removes one party, but the questions raised by the 2021 sale — and by Pitt’s claim that Jolie’s half of the estate should have been his to buy — continue in the case against the company that bought her stake. Neither side’s representatives offered any comment beyond the joint statement, whose closing line appeared designed to close the subject for good.

A DECADE OF LITIGATION, CLOSED IN ONE SENTENCE

The brevity of the ending is in striking contrast to the length of the dispute. Pitt and Jolie defined the Hollywood power couple for a decade, split in 2016, spent eight years finalising their divorce, and then spent a further two years fighting over the winery where they had married. That the end should arrive in a single joint sentence — agreed by both camps, framed around the best interests of their family — suggests a calculation on both sides that the costs of continuing, financial and otherwise, had overtaken anything left to win. Search interest in the settlement was immediate and international: on Thursday, 26 November, “angelina jolie” was among the most searched entertainment terms in Canada, and the end of the Miraval dispute was trending in Australia — two of the markets where the couple’s story has been followed most closely for twenty years.

For the winery itself, the settlement closes the ownership chapter that began with a wedding. Miraval’s wines will continue to be made and sold, as they have been throughout the litigation. What changes is the removal of the most famous obstacle to its future: a courtroom fight between the two people whose names built its modern reputation. After ten years of filings, counter-filings and scheduled trials, the final document in the Pitt–Jolie winery case turns out to be the shortest one — a statement of a few dozen words, saying that it is over, and that nothing more will be said.
