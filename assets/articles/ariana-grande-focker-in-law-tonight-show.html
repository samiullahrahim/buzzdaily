Ariana Grande Steals the Show as She Sings ‘O Christmas Tree’ with Robert De Niro and Ben Stiller on The Tonight Show

BuzzDaily Staff · US Desk
· 9 November 2026 · 6 min read
Photo: Emma · CC BY-SA 2.0 · via Wikimedia Commons · Ariana Grande performing on the Dangerous Woman Tour in Manchester, 2017 · Free licence — full author and licence details on the Commons file page (file photo)

NEW YORK — The Tonight Show does not usually begin with a carol, and its guests do not usually wander into the monologue unannounced. Both things happened on Sunday night. Ariana Grande, Robert De Niro and Ben Stiller crashed Jimmy Fallon’s opening monologue on a special Sunday-night episode of The Tonight Show Starring Jimmy Fallon on 8 November 2026, breaking into a rendition of “O Christmas Tree” prompted by the arrival of the 2026 Rockefeller Center Christmas tree — and it was Grande, taking the lead vocals and reaching for the high notes to loud applause, who walked away with the moment.

THE COLD OPEN

The set-up was pure late-night theatre. With New York’s most famous tree newly arrived at Rockefeller Center — the annual starting gun for the city’s festive season — Fallon’s monologue was interrupted by three of the biggest names in American entertainment, assembled in one place for one reason: they have a film to sell. Grande took the lead on the carol, her voice doing what it reliably does, climbing to the high notes as the studio audience applauded. The comedy of the bit rested on the mismatch of its parts — a pop vocalist of Grande’s precision flanked by two actors not hired for their singing — and it landed its punchline at the end: per JustJared, De Niro’s vocals at the close of the song were dubbed. The joke, generously, wrote itself.

TRUE CONFESSIONS AND 25 YEARS OF HISTORY

The appearance did not end with the song. The trio stayed to play Fallon’s “True Confessions” game, and the conversation turned to first meetings and to the filming of the original Meet the Parents — a production now more than 25 years in the past. That gap is the engine of the whole enterprise: a comedy that opened at the turn of the century has become a four-film family saga, and the men who built it, De Niro’s intimidating Jack Byrnes and Stiller’s perpetually embattled Greg Focker, are now the elder statesmen of their own franchise, telling war stories on late-night television while the new generation waits in the wings.

THE FILM THEY ARE SELLING

The film is Focker-in-Law, the fourth Meet the Parents movie, opening in US cinemas on 25 November 2026 in Thanksgiving week — the most contested family-movie slot of the American year, and a deliberate one for a comedy about family. It is written and directed by John Hamburg. Grande plays Olivia Jones, who is dating Henry Focker, played by Skyler Gisondo: the grown-up son of Greg Focker (Stiller) and Pam Focker (Teri Polo). De Niro returns as Jack Byrnes, the ex-CIA father-in-law whose approval remains the series’ final boss, and Owen Wilson and Blythe Danner also return. BuzzDaily has been following the production’s long runway — Gisondo previously described how Grande played unreleased music for her Focker-in-Law co-stars on set — and Sunday night’s ambush was the clearest sign yet that the promotional campaign has shifted into its final, loudest gear.

A SUNDAY-NIGHT EXPERIMENT

The episode itself was part of a format experiment worth noting. It was the second of four special post-Sunday Night Football editions of The Tonight Show, following the first after the Buccaneers’ game against the Bears — NBC using its football audience, the largest regular audience in American television, as a launchpad for late night. Rapper Lil Baby was the episode’s musical guest. The run continues next Sunday, 15 November, with Jennifer Lopez and Anthony Mackie — a booking that sets the bar the Grande–De Niro–Stiller hour has now raised.

There was a neat symmetry to the choice of song. The Rockefeller Center tree is not merely a decoration; its arrival each year is one of New York’s fixed seasonal rituals, the moment the city’s long commercial run-up to Christmas becomes official. By staging their ambush around it, the show’s producers gave three film stars selling a Thanksgiving-week comedy a ready-made piece of December iconography to sing in front of — and gave Grande, whose voice has carried more than one festive standard in her career, the one job in the room nobody else could have done. De Niro and Stiller supplied the fame and the timing; the carol belonged to her from the first note.

GRANDE’S MOMENT

For Grande, the appearance continues a screen run that has made her as much a film story as a music one — the same month has seen her Wicked co-star Cynthia Erivo speaking out about the pair’s press-tour treatment, and Focker-in-Law now positions her inside one of Hollywood’s most durable comedy dynasties, opposite De Niro, in a role built on the franchise’s favourite dynamic: the newcomer the family has to absorb. On Sunday night she was, briefly, something else as well — the best singer ever to hijack a monologue, hitting notes a Christmas tree would be proud of, while two Hollywood legends mimed along beside her. The film opens on 25 November. The carol is already out there.
