Godzilla Minus Zero Roars Toward a Record $30M+ Opening — Biggest Ever for a Foreign-Language Film

BuzzDaily Staff · US Desk
· 7 November 2026 · 6 min read
Photo: Wpcpey · CC BY-SA 4.0 · via Wikimedia Commons · The Godzilla statue at Godzilla Square, Hibiya, Tokyo, photographed in 2018 · Free licence — full author and licence details on the Commons file page (file photo)

LOS ANGELES — Godzilla has returned to American cinemas, and the record book is officially on notice. Godzilla Minus Zero opened in US cinemas on Friday 6 November 2026, grossing $13.5 million domestically — the United States and Canada combined — on its first day, including $6.7 million from Thursday previews, across 3,254 theatres, according to figures reported by Deadline on Saturday morning. On the strength of that Friday, the film is projected to open with $30–33 million for the weekend. Those are projections, not final totals — the confirmed numbers arrive on Monday — but if the estimates hold, Minus Zero will not simply break a record. It will shatter one that has stood for more than twenty years.

THE RECORD IN ITS SIGHTS

The mark at stake is the biggest domestic opening ever for a live-action foreign-language film. It currently belongs to 2002’s Hero, which opened to $17.8 million — a figure that has survived more than two decades of challengers, including some of the most acclaimed international releases of the streaming age. Minus Zero is eyeing $30–33 million, which would clear Hero’s benchmark by more than $12 million at the bottom of the range and come close to doubling it at the top. The projected weekend would also be the biggest opening in the history of GKIDS, the film’s North American distributor — a company built on acclaimed animation and international cinema now hosting the largest launch of its life, with a subtitled Japanese production playing in Japanese with English subtitles in more than 3,250 theatres.

THE SHADOW OF MINUS ONE

To understand the scale of the jump, consider the film’s own predecessor. Godzilla Minus One opened to $11.4 million in North America — a strong result that was treated, rightly, as a breakthrough — and went on to earn around $57 million domestically. It then completed one of the most beloved underdog stories in recent awards history by winning the Academy Award for Best Visual Effects. Minus Zero is now heading for an opening weekend roughly three times the size of Minus One’s debut, powered by years of word of mouth, an Oscar’s worth of prestige, and an audience that discovered the previous film long after it left cinemas. Opening-night viewers, crucially, appear to love this one just as much: Minus Zero earned an A CinemaScore, the same grade Minus One received — the kind of score that, as BuzzDaily’s coverage of CinemaScore-driven box-office runs has shown, tends to turn a big opening weekend into a long and profitable run.

A RECORD START AT HOME FIRST

The American launch follows an even more emphatic start in Japan, where the film opened on Tuesday 3 November — Godzilla Day, marking the 72nd anniversary of the original 1954 film. Its opening day in Japan brought in ¥910 million, around $5.7 million, a record for a live-action film in Japan. Over its first three days it reached ¥1.38 billion, roughly $8.7 million. Add the rest of the world and the picture grows larger still: worldwide, Minus Zero is tipped for an opening weekend of around $60 million across some 60 markets — a global launch on a scale that very few non-English-language productions have ever attempted, built market by market on the reputation of a single predecessor.

A GODZILLA OF FIRSTS

Almost everything about the production breaks new ground for a franchise now in its eighth decade. Godzilla Minus Zero is written and directed by Takashi Yamazaki, who returns after Minus One. It runs 135 minutes. It is the first R-rated Godzilla film, carrying its rating for “some violent content/bloody images” — a harder edge than the series has ever taken into American cinemas. And it is the first Japanese production ever filmed for IMAX, a milestone with direct box-office consequences: premium large-format screens have become the engine room of event-movie openings, and their availability across the film’s 3,254 theatres is part of the story behind a Friday gross of $13.5 million — nearly half of it banked before Friday itself, in $6.7 million of Thursday previews.

WHAT IT MEANS — AND WHAT MONDAY DECIDES

Analysis — Foreign-language films are usually discussed at the box office as exceptions: beloved, decorated, and commercially contained. A projected $30 million-plus opening weekend argues something different — that a subtitled film, released at genuine blockbuster scale with premium screens and preview shows, can behave like a blockbuster. It helps that the Godzilla name carries 72 years of recognition, and that Minus One converted sceptics in unusual numbers. But the comparison with other recent event releases, including the box-office run of Tom Cruise’s “Digger”, is instructive: audiences turned out for this opening the way they turn out for franchise launches, not arthouse crossovers. The honest caveat belongs at the end, and Deadline’s reporting carries it: weekend projections built on a Friday figure can move in either direction, and the record is only set when Monday’s confirmed actuals are counted. On the numbers reported so far, though, the direction of travel is not in doubt. Hero’s $17.8 million has been the ceiling for a generation. By Monday, in all likelihood, it will be history — and BuzzDaily will update this report with the final figures when they land.
