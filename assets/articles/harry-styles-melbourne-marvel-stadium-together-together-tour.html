Harry Styles Turns Melbourne's Marvel Stadium Into a Berlin Nightclub as Australian Tour Opens to Rave Reviews

BuzzDaily Staff · Australia Desk
· 28 November 2026 · 7 min read
Photo: GYSZ / CC BY-SA 4.0 via Wikimedia Commons · Harry Styles on his Together, Together tour in Amsterdam (file photo)

MELBOURNE — Harry Styles opened the Australian leg of his Together, Together tour at Marvel Stadium on Friday, 27 November 2026, turning a closed-roof stadium on a muggy, rain-soaked day into something closer to a Berlin nightclub for some 50,000 fans. It was the first of four Melbourne nights and his first show back in the city in nearly four years — and by the time the house lights came up for the last time, it had drawn four-star reviews from both The Guardian and the Sydney Morning Herald.

A STADIUM TURNED INTO A CLUB

The production is built around the world of Styles' fourth album, Kiss All the Time. Disco, Occasionally., and reviewers agreed on the reference point: Berlin's Berghain, the renowned techno nightclub that partly inspired the record. The Sydney Morning Herald described Styles transporting the 50,000-strong crowd to Berghain, with the cavernous venue transformed into a pulsing nightclub; Particle News noted the pulsing lights and techno textures, lasers and strobe lights reworking songs for stadium scale. A 360-degree stage, with linked catwalks and two raised bridges, let Styles roam the floor — singing, skipping and jumping, in The Guardian's account — while a live band, a local string section and Melbourne saxophonist Lorren Chiodo, with Laura Bibbs on trumpet, filled out the sound.

FIVE CHAPTERS, MORE THAN 20 SONGS

The roughly two-hour set was arranged in five chapters and featured more than 20 songs drawn from across Styles' four-album catalogue, with the running order set to rotate — swapped tracks and changing “secret songs” are promised across the four Melbourne nights. The established hits landed early and often: “Golden”, “Adore You”, “Watermelon Sugar” and “American Girls” were all there. “Aperture”, from the new album, was singled out by The Guardian as a particular highlight, transforming the stadium into a euphoric rave, while “Dance No More” supplied the night's defining lyric — “It's feelin' like the music has been Heaven sent / And that there's no difference in between the tears and the sweat.”

The deep cuts were chosen with the same care. “Taste Back” arrived with an interpolation of Underworld's “Born Slippy” folded into it — the nightclub thesis stated in a single segue — and the propulsive electronic number “Ready, Steady, Go!” brought Styles and his band to the centre of the stadium, the entire arena on its feet. Later, “Treat People With Kindness” carried a verse from Talking Heads' “This Must Be the Place (Naive Melody)”. The back-to-back acoustic encore of “Matilda” and “Sign of the Times” quietened the room before the closer, “As It Was”, sent everyone back out dancing.

‘DANCE, SWEAT AND SING’ — AND CARE FOR THE CROWD

Midway through the show, Styles told the crowd what he wanted from them: “Dance, sweat and sing.” The sweat, on a humid night under a closed roof, was guaranteed — and it came with a responsibility Styles visibly took seriously. Twice during the evening he paused the show and had the house lights turned on so staff could locate and assist dehydrated fans on the floor — fittingly, The Guardian noted, during “Treat People With Kindness”. He had promised early on to stop the show if anyone needed help, and he kept the promise. Reviewers returned to that care as the thing that sets him apart: the degree to which Styles focuses on the crowd, the Sydney Morning Herald wrote, is practically unparalleled.

THE REVIEWS

The Guardian's Giselle Au-Nhien Nguyen gave the show four stars, describing a performer who has mostly shaken off boy-band associations to be regarded as a credible artist in his own right, and noting that while the 2026 album was met with mixed reception from critics and fans, its songs blend seamlessly into the live set. The Sydney Morning Herald's Nell Geraets also gave four stars, opening with the image of the roof closed on a muggy, rain-soaked day and no one noticing the heat: “they're too busy watching the cavernous venue transform into a pulsing nightclub.” Two four-star verdicts, from two mastheads, on the same morning — the Australian leg could hardly have asked for a stronger opening.

SEVEN CITIES, ONE VERY LONG YEAR

Melbourne and Sydney are two of just seven cities on the 2026 Together, Together itinerary, alongside Amsterdam, London's Wembley Stadium, São Paulo, Mexico City and a 30-night stand at Madison Square Garden in New York. The tour celebrates Kiss All the Time. Disco, Occasionally., released on 6 March 2026 and produced by Kid Harpoon. Three more Melbourne nights follow before the Australian run closes with two shows in Sydney. The opening also registered in search interest far from Melbourne — on Google Trends in the UK it charted at No. 24 in entertainment (5K+, up more than 1,000 per cent) — modest numbers, honestly stated, for a tour whose centre of gravity this week is firmly in Australia. On Friday night's evidence, that is exactly where it belongs.
