Druski Makes SNL History Tonight as First-Ever Digital Creator to Host — With The Strokes

BuzzDaily Staff · US Desk
· 7 November 2026 · 6 min read
Photo: Raph_PH · CC BY 2.0 · via Wikimedia Commons · Julian Casablancas of The Strokes performing live in Brooklyn, New York · Free licence — full author and licence details on the Commons file page (file photo)

NEW YORK — Tonight, Saturday Night Live does something it has never done in its 51-year history. Druski — real name Drew Desbordes — hosts the show tonight, Saturday 7 November 2026, for the first time, becoming the first digital content creator ever to host SNL. The Strokes return as musical guest. The episode airs at 11:30 p.m. ET on NBC and Peacock, with streaming available the following day on Peacock. In its 52nd season, a programme that has been hosted by film stars, athletes, politicians and pop royalty is handing its stage to a man who built his entire career on a phone — and the booking reads less like a stunt than like an acknowledgment of where comedy’s centre of gravity now sits.

“GET YA POPCORN READY”

Druski announced the booking to his Instagram followers on 22 October 2026, in the voice that built his following: “IM HOSTING SNL🍿GET YA POPCORN READY.” The numbers behind the announcement explain NBC’s enthusiasm. Druski, 32, is a satirical sketch creator whose characters and catchphrases travel the internet at a speed television rarely matches: he brings 13.7 million followers on Instagram, 15.6 million on TikTok and more than 6 million subscribers on YouTube — an audience assembled entirely outside the network system he steps into tonight. It is the platform he has described in his own words: “I’ve built my platform by creating characters and comedy that resonate with people.” Tonight, that platform gets its biggest single stage.

THE PROMO

NBC’s official promo for the episode, released on Wednesday 4 November, leaned happily into the occasion. In the spot, filmed with cast member Marcello Hernández and also featuring Sarah Sherman and The Strokes, Druski introduced himself with characteristic confidence: “Hi, I’m Druski, and I’ll be the host and musical guest on SNL this week!” Sherman was quick to correct him: “Druski, we already have a musical guest — The Strokes.” The Strokes return as musical guest, completing a line-up built for two audiences at once: SNL’s broadcast faithful, and the far larger online crowd that has never needed a television to follow Druski’s work.

THE ROAD HERE — VIA DRAKE AND THE BET AWARDS

Tonight is a milestone, but it is not an overnight one. Druski credits Drake with launching his mainstream career, through his appearance in the 2020 video for “Laugh Now Cry Later” — the moment a sketch comic beloved online became a face the wider entertainment industry could place. This year the ascent became undeniable: on 28 June 2026 he hosted the BET Awards for the first time, handling one of live television’s biggest comedy stages. The path from that night to tonight’s hosting debut is the clearest version yet of a route that now runs from social feeds to legacy institutions — a route SNL itself underlined this week with its plans for a British version of the show, as the format looks for new audiences on both sides of the Atlantic.

WHAT COMES NEXT

If tonight proves the concept, Druski’s calendar suggests the industry already believes. This December he headlines a new Netflix series, Collect & Praise, a megachurch-pastor parody that takes his character comedy into scripted streaming. He also has a role in The Catch, alongside Emma Stone and Chris Pine — a feature-film credit that would have seemed unlikely for a sketch creator five years ago and now looks like a natural next step. And for SNL, the experiment is time-limited by design: next week’s host is John Mulaney, one of the show’s most beloved returning hosts, whose presence on the schedule underlines exactly how unusual tonight’s booking is. One week, the most traditional host imaginable in waiting; this week, a first in 51 years.

A FIRST THAT SAYS SOMETHING

SNL has refreshed itself many times across its 51 years, but its host’s chair has always belonged to people who arrived through the traditional gates: film, television, sport, music, politics. Druski arrives through none of them. His sketches were made for feeds, his characters built for sharing, his following counted in the tens of millions across Instagram, TikTok and YouTube. That is precisely the point of the booking in season 52: the show is not merely borrowing a famous face for a week — it is recognising a parallel comedy industry, one with its own stars, its own formats and, increasingly, its own claim on Saturday night. Whether characters honed in vertical video translate to live sketch television is the open question of the evening, and it gives tonight’s episode a curiosity factor no routine booking could match. Wednesday’s promo suggested a performer entirely comfortable with the machinery of the show. Tonight’s monologue, delivered live to a studio audience and a television audience at the same time, will provide the answer — and, if it works, a template other legacy programmes will study closely.

HOW TO WATCH TONIGHT

Saturday Night Live airs tonight, Saturday 7 November 2026, at 11:30 p.m. ET on NBC and Peacock, and will be streaming the following day on Peacock. However the episode lands — and first-time hosts have produced some of the show’s most remembered nights, in every sense — the history is already made the moment Druski walks through the door for the monologue: the first digital content creator to host Saturday Night Live. Popcorn, as instructed, ready.
