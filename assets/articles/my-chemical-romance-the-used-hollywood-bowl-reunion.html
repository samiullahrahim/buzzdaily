My Chemical Romance and The Used End Their Feud On Stage at the Hollywood Bowl — and Gerard Way Announces a New Record

BuzzDaily Staff · Entertainment Desk
· 31 October 2026 · 8 min read
Photo: Lara Janssen / CC0 via Wikimedia Commons · My Chemical Romance performing at Rock en Seine, Paris, August 2011

For one night at the Hollywood Bowl, the most famous feud in emo simply stopped existing. On Friday 30 October, at one of the two finale shows of My Chemical Romance's 'Long Live the Black Parade' US tour, Gerard Way and Bert McCracken — the two frontmen whose friendship, fallout and long silence became as legendary as their music — performed together on stage in Los Angeles, in what Parade described as their first joint performance in years. It was the moment an entire generation of fans had stopped expecting and never stopped wanting.

The reconciliation announced itself early. During The Used's opening set, McCracken looked out at the Bowl and told the crowd that 'mom and dad are back together again' — a line met, by all accounts, with huge applause. Shortly afterwards, he proved it: Way walked out during The Used's set and joined McCracken to sing 'Buried Myself Alive', the bruised fan favourite from The Used's self-titled 2002 debut album. It was a deliberate, affectionate choice — and, pointedly, it was not the song the diehards had come hoping to hear.

That song, of course, was 'Under Pressure'. The Queen and David Bowie classic has hung over this pairing for more than twenty years, ever since the two bands first performed it together. About a quarter of the way through My Chemical Romance's headline set, Way addressed the expectation directly and told the crowd the band would not be playing the cover — an announcement that drew some boos from the Bowl. Instead, the reunion got a second act on MCR's own terms: McCracken returned to the stage to join My Chemical Romance for 'You Know What They Do to Guys Like Us in Prison', the feral deep cut from their 2004 breakthrough album 'Three Cheers for Sweet Revenge'. If 'Under Pressure' belongs to both bands' shared past, 'Prison' belongs to the era when they were conquering the world side by side — and on Friday night, that proved to be the point.

As if a healed feud were not enough for one evening, Way used the show to deliver the night's second headline: My Chemical Romance are making a new record, and the band will take all of 2027 off to record it. For a group whose modern era has been built almost entirely on celebration — of 'The Black Parade', of their catalogue, of simply existing again — it is the clearest signal yet that the reunion is becoming a second act rather than a victory lap.

The setting could hardly have been more loaded. The Hollywood Bowl shows close out the American leg of 'Long Live the Black Parade', the stadium-scale tour on which My Chemical Romance have performed their 2006 masterpiece 'The Black Parade' in full, in character, night after night. Friday's show with The Used was the first of two finale dates; the final US show follows on Saturday 31 October, with Thrice — rather than The Used — in the support slot. That scheduling alone made Friday the one night this could happen, and everyone in the building seems to have known it.

To understand why two guest spots and one quip could carry this much weight, you have to go back to the early 2000s, when emo broke out of the underground and these two bands were at the front of it. The Used, formed in Orem, Utah, arrived with a self-titled debut in 2002 full of screaming vulnerability; My Chemical Romance, from Newark, New Jersey, followed with 'I Brought You My Bullets, You Brought Me Your Love' the same year, then detonated with 'Three Cheers for Sweet Revenge' in 2004 and 'The Black Parade' in 2006. They were peers, tourmates and, by every contemporary account, genuine friends — two bands dragging each other up the same ladder at the same time.

The friendship was not merely social; it was practical. As McCracken himself has recalled, The Used took My Chemical Romance out on an early European tour, and he personally encouraged his label's A&R representative to sign them. The bond produced its defining artefact on the 2005 Taste of Chaos tour, when the two bands closed shows together with a joint encore of 'Under Pressure' — two frontmen trading lines on a song about pressure, solidarity and holding on, in front of crowds who treated the pairing as a double headline act. A studio version released that year turned the moment into the era's definitive collaboration, and the image of Way and McCracken sharing a microphone became one of the defining photographs of emo's mainstream breakthrough.

Which is why the fallout, when it came, became as famous as the music. The two singers drifted into a rift that played out over years of pointed interviews and pointed silences. As McCracken has since explained, the split was tied to Way getting sober: as Way withdrew to focus on his recovery, McCracken took the distance as a personal slight. He has admitted that he felt hurt, and that the hurt curdled into public criticism — including remarks questioning Way's artistic integrity — that kept the feud alive long after the original wound. For fans, 'Way versus McCracken' became emo's great unresolved story: two men who had quite literally sung about holding each other together, not speaking.

The thaw, when it finally came, was unusually public and unusually honest. Appearing on Steve-O's Wild Ride! podcast in October 2025, McCracken spoke about the relationship with a candour that startled listeners. 'We had a really beautiful friendship,' he said, recalling the early European tour and the role he played in pushing his label's A&R representative toward signing My Chemical Romance. He described their dynamic as a 'long-standing joke that we're frenemies' — adding, pointedly, 'It's only coming from my side.' He acknowledged that he had misunderstood Way's distance during his recovery, admitted the hurt behind his critical remarks, and said the two had since spoken, and that he has 'full respect' for Way.

Then McCracken turned reflection into an invitation. On the same podcast, he said it plainly: 'take us out on tour, My Chem' — insisting The Used were 'ready'. Steve-O, grasping exactly what a reunion would mean to the generation that grew up on both bands, called the prospect 'the sickest thing ever' and predicted it would 'break the internet'. It sounded, at the time, like one of rock's great what-ifs being wished into the open. Few can have expected My Chemical Romance to actually take him up on it.

By February 2026, the joking had hardened into something closer to a promise. Appearing on The Sappenin' Podcast with Sean Smith, McCracken teased the reunion outright — and hung it on the one song everybody wanted. 'There's no way we won't do Under Pressure. We have to,' he said. Which makes Friday night's plot twist almost perfect: the reunion happened, exactly as teased, but the cover did not. Whether Way's refusal was stagecraft, stubbornness or a band determined to write a new shared memory instead of re-enacting an old one, the effect was the same — the Hollywood Bowl got a reunion on the bands' terms, not on nostalgia's.

The timing matters beyond one fandom. Emo's revival has been building for years — through nostalgia festivals, anniversary tours and a new generation discovering 'The Black Parade' as a classic album rather than a period piece — and 'Long Live the Black Parade' has been its stadium-sized proof of concept. A public, on-stage reconciliation between the genre's two defining frontmen is the kind of full-circle moment that turns a revival into a restoration: not younger bands imitating the era, but the era's central figures settling its central unfinished business in front of 17,000 people.

Friday also clarified where My Chemical Romance go next. The band's return since 2019 has rested on performance rather than new material — their 2022 single 'The Foundations of Decay' remains their only post-reunion release, and they have not released a studio album since 'Danger Days: The True Lives of the Fabulous Killjoys' in 2010. Way's announcement changes that calculus completely. Taking the whole of 2027 off to record means the 'Long Live the Black Parade' era is being deliberately closed, not milked: the costumes, the concept and the nightly resurrection of a twenty-year-old album end with this tour, and what follows is supposed to be new.

There is a pleasing symmetry in who shared the stage for the announcement. The last time Way and McCracken performed together with any regularity, both bands were young, ascendant and inseparable; this time, they are elder statesmen of a genre that refused to die, playing a landmark Los Angeles venue at the end of one of the biggest rock tours on the planet. British fans, who took both bands to their hearts during that first wave — and who packed out MCR's UK stadium dates on this very era of touring — will recognise the significance instantly: this was the feud that defined emo's first chapter, ending in public, on stage, with a joke about mom and dad.

On Saturday night, Thrice will open the final US show and the 'Long Live the Black Parade' tour will take its last American bow without The Used on the bill. But Friday 30 October is the date that will be remembered from this finale: the night Gerard Way and Bert McCracken sang together again, the night 'Under Pressure' stayed in its case and nobody much cared by the end — and the night My Chemical Romance told their fans that the story, against all odds, has another chapter coming.
