Take-Two says GTA VI set a $3.79 billion day-one entertainment launch record.

BuzzDaily Staff · US Desk
· 21 November 2026 · 8 min read
Photo: Sergey Galyonkin / CC BY-SA 2.0 via Wikimedia Commons

NEW YORK — Take-Two Interactive says Grand Theft Auto VI has set a new entertainment launch record — on the publisher's own numbers. The company announced on Friday, 20 November, that the game reached $3.79 billion in day-one retail sales, a figure it calls a record for entertainment across any medium and "the biggest entertainment launch in history". The game, developed by Rockstar Games, launched worldwide on Thursday, 19 November for PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S.

The number carries an asterisk, and Take-Two prints it in its own announcement: the $3.79 billion is based on the company's estimates of worldwide retail sales through the end of 19 November. It is the publisher's estimate of what shoppers spent at retail around the world on day one — not audited revenue booked by Take-Two, and not a figure the company has split by platform or by digital versus physical sales. Every figure in this report is presented on that basis.

THE PRE-RELEASE RECORD: 32.5 MILLION UNITS BEFORE LAUNCH

Alongside the day-one figure, Take-Two said the game had sold 32.5 million units before release — pre-release sales it describes as a new record across all platforms. The queue had been building for months: pre-orders opened in June, and by the time the pre-load window opened on 10 November, players were being asked to clear up to 212 GB of storage, as BuzzDaily reported when the pre-load went live. Sensor Tower's tracker counted $300 million of pre-orders inside the first week alone, and the total passed the previous all-time pre-order record — $709 million — with roughly three weeks still to go.

The shape of that pre-order curve shows how unusual the demand was. Sensor Tower put the total at about $400 million inside six weeks, and the final stretch did the heaviest lifting of all: the running total more than doubled in the last ten days before launch, finishing at more than three times the previous all-time record the firm had ever tracked.

THE INDEPENDENT COUNT IS LOWER — AND MEASURES SOMETHING DIFFERENT

Independent trackers put the launch lower, and say so openly. Sensor Tower, which models digital storefront data, closed the pre-order window at 24.5 million copies and $2.36 billion in pre-order revenue across the PlayStation and Xbox storefronts — the largest pre-launch total it has ever recorded. Set against Take-Two's 32.5 million pre-release units, the difference is one of measurement, not a contradiction: Take-Two's figure counts pre-release sales across all retail — physical copies and digital downloads, worldwide — on the company's own estimates, while Sensor Tower models digital storefronts only, on PlayStation and Xbox, and does not count physical pre-orders at all. The two numbers measure different things and are not directly comparable. Sensor Tower's split shows where the digital money went — 75 per cent of pre-order revenue on PlayStation against 25 per cent on Xbox — and how buyers spent it, with 82 per cent of pre-orders going on the $100 Ultimate Edition rather than the standard edition.

BIGGER THAN ANYTHING THAT CAME BEFORE

The comparisons underline the scale. The previous game in the series, Grand Theft Auto V, was credited with about $800 million in its first 24 hours back in 2013 — a record at the time. Take-Two's day-one estimate for GTA VI is more than four times that. Across its lifetime, GTA V has sold-in nearly 235 million units, and the Grand Theft Auto series as a whole nearly 480 million units, according to the same announcement — figures that show both how big the series already was and how much of its lifetime business the new game's first day is claimed to represent. Rockstar's other flagship series, Red Dead Redemption, has sold-in nearly 118 million units by comparison.

Critics, for their part, have matched the commercial noise. The game holds a score of 99 on Metacritic, which Take-Two bills as making GTA VI the highest rated game of all time — a picture BuzzDaily reported on launch day, when the game unlocked to a 99 Metacritic score and its voice cast was revealed. Rockstar's founder and president, Sam Houser, said in the announcement: “It is with immense gratitude that we enter a new chapter of Rockstar Games with the release of Grand Theft Auto VI. We are incredibly humbled by the response from players around the world and we couldn't be more excited to share this moment with you.”

The game itself returns the series to Vice City and the fictional state of Leonida, following Jason Duval and Lucia Caminos, who are drawn into a conspiracy stretching across the state after an easy score goes wrong, in Rockstar's own synopsis. It is the series' first new entry since Grand Theft Auto V in 2013, and it arrived as an M-rated release on the PlayStation Store, the Microsoft Store, the Rockstar Games Store and at retailers worldwide, at a standard price of about $80 in the United States.

WHAT THE ESTIMATE CANNOT SHOW YET

What a company estimate cannot show is staying power, and the harder numbers arrive later. Take-Two's next quarterly results will report net bookings — an audited measure of what the company itself took in — rather than an estimate of what retailers sold. Refunds, discounts and the split between the $100 Ultimate Edition and the standard edition will all shape that figure. For now, the record stands exactly as its publisher states it: the biggest entertainment launch in history, by Take-Two's own count of its own first day.
