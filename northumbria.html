Robson Green’s Northumbria Mystery Gets Its BBC One Launch Date

By BuzzDaily Staff
· 7 October 2026

The Northumbria Mysteries will begin on BBC One on Friday 23 October 2026, with Robson Green leading a new detective drama rooted in the landscape of England's North East.

The series runs to eight episodes of 45 minutes each, airing in BBC One's 2pm Friday slot. The first four episodes arrived on BBC iPlayer from launch day, and the remaining four follow on iPlayer on Friday 20 November, aligned with the second half of the linear run — so viewers who raced through the opening mysteries do not have long to wait for the rest.

Green, who was born in Hexham, plays Joe Ruby, an ex-convict and gambler on a community rehabilitation programme who volunteers at a local priory. Liz White co-stars as DI Rose O'Connell, an Oxford-educated detective, creating an odd-couple pairing across Northumberland's coast and market towns, with Gurjeet Singh as her right-hand man DS Raj Singh. The cast also includes Simon Callow as Father David Rutherford, the priest connected to Joe's new life, and Chris Gascoyne as local criminal figure ‘Ace’ Dawson, with guest appearances from Paula Wilcox, Cathy Tyson, Jason Merrells and Julian Rhind-Tutt.

The first four cases show the range of the format. The series opens with a monk found dead at the priory where Joe volunteers — a case that makes his criminal past a liability, because it makes him a suspect. A death during a high-stakes poker gathering then pulls Rose toward Joe's knowledge of that world; a suspicious drowning at the wealthy Armstrong family's country estate follows, and the fourth story begins with a body discovered on a beach, connecting the worlds of windsurfing and funeral directing.

The production is rooted in the region it depicts. Filming took place across Northumberland over several months, wrapping in August 2026, with locations including Dunstanburgh Castle and the tidal island of Holy Island, and the series is made by Newcastle-based Lonesome Pine Productions as part of the BBC's North East creative investment. Green — long associated with crime drama through “Wire in the Blood” and “Grantchester” — has described the region's landmarks as a principal character in the drama, not merely a backdrop.
