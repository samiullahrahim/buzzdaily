Jimmy Kimmel Fires Back After Trump Calls Him a "Loser" at Tennessee Rally

BuzzDaily Staff · Entertainment Desk
· 14 October 2026 · 7 min read
Illustration: Original artwork created for BuzzDaily

Jimmy Kimmel has never been shy about taking on Donald Trump in his late-night monologues — and this week, the president handed him fresh material at a rally in Clarksville, Tennessee.

During a Saturday rally, Trump singled out the 58-year-old Jimmy Kimmel Live! host, calling him a "loser" and "crooked" after Kimmel described one of the president's recent events as "sparsely attended."

Kimmel waited until Monday night's show to answer — and he came armed with receipts.

"You'll never guess who got a shoutout," Kimmel told his audience on the Oct. 12 edition of Jimmy Kimmel Live!, before rolling a clip of the president's rally remarks.

In the footage, Trump vents about the late-night host's crowd-size crack: "This loser, Jimmy Kimmel. What a loser," Trump says in the clip. "I had just left Texas or somewhere. The place is packed. And maybe it was Ohio, place is packed. He said, 'Donald Trump had a sparsely attended [rally].' I said, 'Sparsely attended?!' They had to walk thousands of people – they couldn't get into the [event]. These guys are crooked people. They're losers."

Kimmel's first reaction was pure comedy: "Do I really sound like that? Be honest," he quipped, drawing laughs from the studio audience.

Then he got to the point. "Of all the things I said about him last week. Of all the many moronic and criminal words and deeds I detailed, that's what bothers him?! Me pointing out the fact that his rallies have empty seats??" Kimmel said. "Well, in that case, let's do it again. There were many empty seats at the rally. So many empty seats."

As if to underline the point, the show's editors cut to photographs of empty seats at the president's rally — a visual rebuttal that got one of the biggest reactions of the night.

"There were even empty seats at the rally where he complained about me saying he had empty seats at the rally," Kimmel added, before taking aim at Trump's explanation that supporters in the upper sections migrate down to the main floor out of enthusiasm.

"He keeps claiming the reason there are empty seats in the back and the top is because the people love him so much they make their way down to the front," Kimmel said. "Isn't it interesting that that never happens at a Lakers game? People just moving up to the front. But it does at an event controlled by the United States Secret Service."

The host then landed one of the night's sharpest lines, claiming the president's rallies were "not at full capacity" — much like, he joked, "Trump's cognitive function."

The White House fires back

The exchange didn't stay one-sided for long. White House spokesperson Davis Ingle issued a blistering statement in response to Kimmel's monologue: "Jimmy Kimmel is a no-talent loser who has beclowned himself with tanking ratings and by spewing disgusting lies to his dwindling audience. He is so devoid of actual talent that nobody with any commonsense would ever watch him unless it was to laugh at his miserable failures."

It was a notably personal rebuttal — and only the latest volley in a feud that has been running for years.

The empty-seats obsession

Trump's sensitivity about crowd sizes is nothing new, but the past week has seen the dispute escalate into something of an obsession. In an Oct. 5 post on Truth Social, the president insisted that "what does happen at all of my SOLD OUT Rallies, is that the people in the upper sections tend to migrate down to the Main Floor." He added: "I never have empty seats — Never have, and never will!"

In another post, Trump went further, suggesting news outlets that report on empty seats at his events "should be arrested for their dishonesty and disservice to our Country."

Kimmel, for his part, has made the president's crowd-size fixation a recurring bit — and Monday's monologue suggests he has no intention of dropping it.

A week of Kimmel vs. Trump

The rally-size spat was far from the only Trump material in Kimmel's recent shows. Earlier in the week, the host tore into the president over comments about Iran, after Trump suggested during a rally that Iran could "take out" Los Angeles and San Diego — calling the loss of American cities a "small price to pay" amid the ongoing conflict.

Kimmel called it one of the craziest, most indefensible statements he had ever heard, noting the United States is currently at war with Iran.

The host also devoted time to Trump's reaction to losing the 2026 Nobel Peace Prize, which was awarded on Oct. 9 to human rights advocate Navi Pillay. After the president lashed out at the Nobel committee — calling the decision an indelible stain on Norway — Kimmel mocked the outburst, joking that Trump had posted a 600-word tirade over the perceived snub. "What better way to deserve a Nobel Prize than attacking the woman who won it," Kimmel said.

Kimmel's monologue also took aim at the appointment of Katie Zacharia as the next White House press secretary. The comedian sarcastically told his audience the person who got the job was a Harvard graduate with a master's degree from the Columbia Journalism School — before revealing the position had actually gone to a conservative commentator. "Katie met a number of qualifications for the job," Kimmel joked. "She has blonde hair. She can do a thumbs up. End of list. Really, what else do you need?"

Why this feud matters for late night

The Kimmel-Trump back-and-forth has become one of the defining rivalries of American late-night television. Kimmel has been one of the president's most persistent critics in the genre, and the president has returned the favor repeatedly — making the host a fixture of his rally speeches and social media posts.

For viewers, the feud has become appointment television: Kimmel's monologues regularly generate millions of views online, with clips of his Trump segments among the most-watched late-night content on YouTube.

Whether the White House's latest broadside cools the exchange or pours fuel on it remains to be seen. But if Monday night's monologue is any indication, Jimmy Kimmel isn't backing down — and the empty seats, he made clear, will be mentioned again.
